# Finding: two kinds of check in Communication Craft: is it well expressed, and is it right?

## Question

Communication Craft is the communication part of Workcraft, the user's framework for automating work with AI. Its working model separates two things:
- the **working essence**: what a contribution means to convey, with its reasons, claims, decisions and qualifications;
- the **materialization**: the actual text that expresses it.

While designing an operational question bank, a split appeared:
- some problems in the manager Duncan's feedback could be found from the text;
- others ("four people for a 15-minute job") were problems with the plan itself, which no reading of the text can settle.

The user asked to make this distinction explicit for every communication:
- one check of the **materialization**;
- one check of the **content itself**: whether it is right and sensible for the request. This needs context, not just the text.

What exactly is each, and how do we tell them apart?

## Finding Summary

- **Every communication can fail in two different places, and they need different checks:**
  - **Materialization check: is the meaning well expressed?** Does the text state what is meant, explicitly, in the order, view and level the recipient's need calls for, readably?
  - **Content check: is the meaning right?** Is what the message means true to its sources, and sensible for the request and the work?
- **They differ in what they need and in how they are fixed:**
  - a materialization check needs the text plus the recipient's declared need, and is fixed by **rewriting**;
  - a content check needs sources, the request and reasoning, and is fixed by **changing the meaning, getting information or doing the work. Never by rewording.**
- **The deciding test is where the right meaning can be quoted from:**
  - from **what the author has already settled** (the written essence, an earlier version of the message, an author answer) → materialization: rewrite using that quote;
  - **only from the sources**: the meaning exists in the material but was never selected into what is being said → content (a selection or faithfulness problem). Finding it takes reading the sources, which is context work, even when the fix is then cheap;
  - from nowhere, or it contradicts the message → content (missing information or unfinished work);
  - when unsure, ask the author.
  - *(Corrected on 2026-10-08 after comparison with the 12-40 substrate development-order finding. The first version counted the sources as settled meaning.)*
- **Problems are classified by what is at fault, not by which tool found them.** A text-only tool can expose a content gap. On the trip benchmark, about 90% of the explicitness detector's gaps could only be answered by the author.
- **Some problems have both halves, and both must be checked.** Example: "that was the last thing in the way".
  - Does the text state the status of each item (closed, estimated, open)? That's materialization.
  - Is that status true? That's content.
- **The content check has two parts:**
  - **faithful:** true to its sources and authorised by its author, with nothing material from the sources dropped (a reason, a qualification);
  - **sound:** sensible for the stated request and the work.
- **Applied to Duncan's examples:**
  - wording problems: 3, 4 and 7, plus today's draft errors;
  - content problems: 1, 5 and 6;
  - both halves: 2.
- **The operational guide's checks (O1–O5) each mix both kinds.** That is why an AI pointed at them produced fluent rewrites and missed the content problems.
- **Every check should declare which kind it is, and every report should say what it did *not* check.** A clean text check must never be read as "the message is right".
- **Communication owns the materialization check and the content *interface*:** it records grounds and raises questions.
  - Judging truth needs Context Accumulation.
  - Judging soundness needs Semantic Formulation and Alignment.
- **The present focus stays on materialization.** The content side is named, and handled now as author questions plus checking against the material a task already comes with. No general content-checking tool is built yet.

## Finding

### 1. Why this matters now

Two things happened on 8 October.

**Seven examples from Duncan's feedback were collected,** and they don't all fail the same way:
- "You pointed me to a 6,200-word document" can be fixed by writing the answer into the message.
- "Why are we overcomplicating this with four people involved?" cannot. The arrangement itself had to change, however clearly it was described.

**The AI rewrote an operational draft four times after being pointed at `operational-communication.md`.** Each round fixed wording, and none of the four rounds touched content. That works when the problem is wording. It fails silently when the problem is the plan.

The user's point: unless the two kinds of check are named and kept apart, we keep making these mistakes:
- polishing text whose meaning is wrong;
- trusting a text check as if it vouched for the content;
- giving an AI a mixed checklist and getting only the easy half applied.

### 2. The two checks

| | **Materialization check** | **Content check** |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Is the meaning well expressed? | Is the meaning right? |
| What it looks at | the text, against the intended meaning and the recipient's need | the intended meaning, against sources, the request and the work |
| Covers | explicitness; order (the point before what depends on it); view and level (operational vs technical, process vs single case); length and leading with the decision; readability; no unintended implication | **faithful:** claims, status and commitments true to sources and authorised; **sound:** the answer exists, the plan makes sense, the arrangement is proportionate, it serves the request |
| Inputs | the text, the declared recipient need, the essence where written | sources (Context Accumulation), the request (Alignment), reasoning (Semantic Formulation) |
| Fixed by | rewriting | changing the meaning, getting information, doing the work |
| Can be a question bank? | yes (explicitness exists; operational presentation is next) | faithful: partly, when sources are supplied (quote the source); sound: only as questions to the author or a reasoner, never as a verdict from text |

The existing substrate model already holds this split, unnamed:
- The 6 October finding on essence, materialization and review routes each problem to its fix: re-express it, correct the essence, or obtain information and do the work (`devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_19-33__communication-substrate-materialization-and-review-loop/finding.md`).
- It also lists four comparisons. Grounding of the essence is content; realization and intelligibility are materialization; fit is split.

What it lacked:
- the two names;
- a test between them;
- a rule that each check declares its kind.

### 3. The test: can the right meaning be quoted?

"Could rewriting fix it?" isn't enough on its own: an AI can always claim the author "knew" the meaning. The observable version:

- **Materialization:** the missing or wrongly expressed meaning can be **quoted from what the author has already settled.** That means the written essence, the author's own document or earlier version of the message, or an author answer. The meaning was decided; the text lost or garbled it. Rewriting uses that quote.
  - In Example 3, the answer was in the author's own framing document, so it was settled meaning that the message didn't carry.
- **Content (selection or faithfulness):** the meaning exists **only in the sources**, such as a reason in a reply or a qualification in a record, and was never selected into what is being said. Finding it takes reading the sources, which is the context work the content check needs, even when the fix afterwards is a simple rewrite.
  - Christine's endorsement is this kind: her reply held the limits; the summary never took them in.
- **Content (missing or wrong):** no quote exists anywhere, or the available quote contradicts what the message says.
- **Unsure:** ask the author. The answer either supplies settled meaning (now materialization) or shows the meaning was never settled (content).

*Corrected on 2026-10-08.* The first version counted any source as settled meaning, which put "a reason dropped from the sources" on the materialization side. That contradicts the definition: a check that has to read the sources is a content check. The correction was prompted by comparison with `devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_12-40__communication-substrate-development-order/finding.md`. It is adopted because it follows from this finding's own definition, not because that finding said so.

This is the explicitness fix skill's rule ("change text only with a quoted basis, otherwise ask") generalised. The fix skill already sorts its work this way:
- "fixed from the text" is materialization;
- "asked of the author" is mostly a content gap the text exposed.

**Problems are classified by what is at fault, not by the tool that found them.** A text-only check reads only text, but it can still reveal a content gap. If no sentence states the position, perhaps none was formed. So text checks also act as **symptom detectors** for content problems. When a materialization question finds nothing to quote anywhere, it becomes a content question for the author.

### 4. Cases with both halves

Some problems sit across the line. Don't force them into one kind; split them into a text question and a truth question.

| Case | Text question (materialization) | Truth question (content) |
|---|---|---|
| A status claim ("that was the last thing in the way") | Does the text give each item's status: closed, estimated, awaiting verification, open? | Is that status true? |
| Answering the request | Does the text answer the recipient's questions, early, in the right view? | Does an answer exist? Is it the right question? |
| A handoff or arrangement | Does the text say why each handoff is needed? | Is the arrangement proportionate? |
| A request to the recipient | Is the ask stated clearly? | Can this recipient actually answer or decide it? |

**The operational checks are almost all mixed.** Each one asks "is it stated?" and "is it true or sound?" in the same item. An AI given such a check tends to do the half it can do from the text. That is what happened today.

### 7. Where this sits

- **The substrate model:**
  - content checks the **contribution** (what is meant, its relations, its grounds);
  - materialization checks the **expression** against it.
- **The three families of communication problems:**
  - intelligibility is materialization;
  - fidelity is content (faithful);
  - **fit splits:** how the answer is presented is materialization; whether there is a right answer is content (sound).
- **Workcraft:**
  - Communication owns the materialization check and the **content interface**: it records what the essence claims and on what grounds, and raises content questions. It never certifies content from text.
  - Context Accumulation supplies the sources for faithfulness.
  - Semantic Formulation develops missing answers.
  - Alignment compares the meaning with the request.
- **Receiving:** the same split. "Do I understand what they mean?" is a materialization judgment. "Is it right, and do I accept it?" is a content judgment.

The pattern is familiar from editing practice: a copy editor fixes expression, a fact-checker checks claims against sources, and an editor judges whether the piece holds up. Those match materialization, faithful and sound. Software's "built it right" against "built the right thing" is the same cut. Both parallels are cited from memory.

### 8. What changes in practice

1. **Every check declares its kind:**
   - materialization or content (faithful / sound);
   - the inputs it needs;
   - how its findings are fixed;
   - what it cannot vouch for.
2. **Every report says what it did not check.** For example: "Checked: materialization (explicitness, order, view). Not checked: content (claims against sources; whether the plan is sound)", followed by any content questions raised.
3. **The operational bank is built in two halves:**
   - materialization questions answered from the text: is the position stated, is each status's class stated, is the reason for each step's order stated, is the reason for each handoff stated, the sentence roles;
   - content questions for the author: does a position exist? is a simpler arrangement possible? are these statuses true?
4. **When drafting, show the content questions alongside the rewriting, not as a gate.** Don't polish a plan before its content questions have been asked. This ordering is untested.
5. **Now vs later:**
   - **Now: materialization side.** Built as banks.
   - **Now: content side, in ordinary work.** Content questions go to the author. When a task already comes with its material (the replies, documents and records it is about), the content check is done against that material, by hand or by the drafting agent. This is the ordinary faithful work the 6 October focus finding allowed. No new content-checking tool is built now.
   - **Later:** a general content check that finds and refreshes its own sources, when Context Accumulation is integrated (deferred on 6 October).
6. **Where the bank fits in development.** The 12-40 finding recommends developing one real operational use end to end: establish the meaning, express it, inspect it, improve the part that failed. The two-half operational bank is the *inspection* step of that path, and its content questions also serve the "establish the meaning" step.
   - That finding also advises applying existing guidance before building new guidance. That has now been done: the AI applied `operational-communication.md` four times on 8 October and fixed only wording. That evidence is why a split bank is justified.

## Reasoning

**What held:**
- **The split by fix route:**
  - it sorts all real cases;
  - it matches the substrate model's existing routing;
  - it prevents the specific mistake seen today (rewriting when the plan was the problem).
- **The quote-based test:** it replaced "could rewriting fix it?" because an AI can always claim the author knew the meaning; a quote is observable.
- **Classifying by fault, not tool:** a text-only detector revealed mostly author-only meanings on the trip benchmark.

**Rejected:**
- **Classifying checks only by tool** ("text tools check text"): would send text-exposed content gaps to rewriting.
- **One unified "does it serve the exchange" check:** true that the two entangle (a buried answer), but it loses the fix routing. Kept as the both-halves rule.
- **Three top kinds** (expression / faithful / sound): faithful and sound share the decisive property (fixed by changing meaning, not words), so they are sub-checks.
- **A content gate before any rewriting:** could stall work the user needs now. Kept as content questions shown alongside.

# Finding: develop the communication substrate through concrete uses

## Update — 2026-10-08

The main recommendation remains: develop the substrate through a concrete use, starting with operational communication. The preliminary advice to try the existing operational guide has now been carried out. The user reports four applications of `operational-communication.md`, each producing wording fixes only; the related [materialization-versus-content finding](/Users/nsstorm/Desktop/projects/AIOpsMan/devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_13-08__materialization-check-vs-content-check-in-communication-craft/finding.md) records the same observation.

The next step is to develop and test operational checks that distinguish whether the meaning is well expressed from whether the meaning is supported and sound for the request. The four applications establish the failure to address content in that instance; they do not by themselves establish whether its cause was the guide, its execution or unavailable context.

## Question

What should Communication Craft develop first: the communication substrate, or practical capabilities such as operational communication, explicitness and person-to-person communication?

The underlying concern is a possible circle. We need to understand meaning and preserve it to build good communication tools. Yet building those tools may be how we discover what their supporting understanding needs to contain.

This inquiry compares the development paths, explains their dependencies and recommends a next focus given the foundation and skills already available. It does not implement the next capability.

## Finding Summary

- **Develop the practical method for establishing and using the substrate through concrete communication work.** The conceptual foundation already gives us a starting point.
- **Understanding can come before expression without a complete substrate system being built before its uses.** A use can begin with available sources, existing task context or a deliberately prepared working account.
- **Meaning and fidelity belong in that development from the beginning.** Define what the particular operation must convey and preserve; a comprehensive automated checker can come later.
- **Concrete uses should inform the substrate deliberately.** Their requirements do not automatically combine into a correct general model or shared format.
- **The next focus should be a bounded use that exercises the whole path:** establish the needed meaning, express it, inspect the result and develop the part that fails.
- **An operational account remains the starting use, and the existing guide has now been tried.** Four applications produced wording fixes only. The next increment should separate expression checks from content questions and test whether the substantive gaps are addressed.
- **Share what proves useful across different uses.** The shared result may be principles or a formation method before it becomes a common record or standalone skill.
- **Foundation-first remains appropriate when a specific shared contribution is missing or demonstrably worth developing early.** Its priority should follow that concrete need.

## Finding

Communication Craft is developing both an understanding of communication and practical skills that AI can apply. The goal is for those two kinds of work to improve each other: the concepts should help us build useful capabilities, and actual communication work should expose where the concepts or methods are incomplete.

The next development effort should address the failure now observed in the operational use: applying the existing guide four times improved wording without addressing content. Use the existing foundation to make those two kinds of judgment explicit, then inspect whether the resulting communication supplies the substantive understanding the task needs.

### 1. Two different meanings of “first” were being combined

Within a particular communication task, we need to establish what we are saying, what supports it and what the exchange needs. Otherwise a fluent message may express an unsupported or incomplete position.

But that does not determine the order in which we must build reusable capabilities.

Suppose we want to develop a method for turning a detailed explanation into a short reply. We can supply the original explanation, the reply's purpose and the meanings that need to survive. That gives us enough to develop and inspect the transformation. We do not first need an automated general system that discovers those inputs for every possible communication.

The distinction is:

| Kind of order | What it concerns |
|---|---|
| **Order within one communication task** | What we need to understand before accepting the particular expression as suitable and faithful. |
| **Order of learning** | Which examples and comparisons reveal what the method needs and where it fails. |
| **Order of building capabilities** | Which reusable instruction, representation or component deserves development now. |

These orders interact. They need not be identical.

The earlier [communication formation and review finding](/Users/nsstorm/Desktop/projects/AIOpsMan/devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_19-33__communication-substrate-materialization-and-review-loop/finding.md) already allowed drafting to reveal missing understanding and allowed existing task context to supply the working basis. It did not establish a compulsory separate essence document for every message.

So our earlier understanding remains useful: **establish a grounded basis for the contribution, and keep it accountable to the actual task and sources.** What does not follow is that a complete reusable substrate producer must be our next development project.

### 2. “Build the substrate” names several different jobs

The [substrate foundation](/Users/nsstorm/Desktop/projects/AIOpsMan/devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_22-09__communication-substrate-foundation-and-dimensions/finding.md) defines the substrate as a working understanding of a particular communication in its situation. Its essence is the important meaning and relationships within that understanding for the present use.

That leaves several development questions:

| Job | What it would produce | Present position |
|---|---|---|
| **Understand the concept** | A map of what can matter: participants, purpose, content, relationships, acts, stance, grounds, expression and reception. | A substantial provisional foundation exists. |
| **Establish one exchange's understanding** | The particular meaning, situation, support and uncertainty needed here. | Available sources and task context can support this; reliability across communication settings remains unestablished. |
| **Choose a representation** | Prose, a table, annotations or another way to retain that understanding. | Some local forms exist. A universal format is not established. |
| **Develop a repeatable formation method** | Instructions for establishing the right account and checking its adequacy. | Existing workflows perform parts of this. Four reported applications of the operational guide changed wording only, exposing a failure to address content that the next increment must examine. |
| **Create shared substrate support** | A reusable method or component that other capabilities consume. | Its useful scope must be established from actual consumers and evidence. |

The conceptual foundation does not need to be restarted simply because shared substrate support is unfinished.

The fourth job—making formation dependable in practice—is where the next development effort can be most informative. We can pursue it inside a useful communication operation, where its decisions have visible consequences.

### 3. Meaning and fidelity are needed now, at the scale of the operation

Your concern about building the uses without understanding meaning is correct. A method that changes communication needs to know what it is allowed to change and what must remain faithful.

Here, **fidelity means respecting the relevant source meaning, intended contribution and commitments under the transformation being requested**. It does not mean preserving every word or reproducing every available fact.

For example, the saved [browser-tool explanation and reply record](</Users/nsstorm/.codex/attachments/797f55f6-8e63-49c7-aa91-9d4b91b9fd95/Pasted text.txt>) contains two reasons for declining a proposed restriction. A later short draft retains one and loses the other. If the current task is to explain the material reasoning behind that choice, the missing reason matters. If the task is merely to acknowledge receipt, those reasons may not belong at all.

As a constructed contrast, another change would matter even if both reasons remained: turning “this could reject legitimate interactions” into “this caused regressions” changes a stated risk into an asserted observation. Keeping the same topics would not preserve that distinction. The technical claims in the transcript remain reported claims; this inquiry did not verify the implementation.

We can make these judgments against a bounded source and task before building a comprehensive fidelity system. What we need initially is an inspectable basis for judging the particular operation:

- What should this contribution make understandable?
- Which particular claims, reasons, relationships, acts and qualifications serve that purpose?
- What supports them?
- What must remain uncertain or open?
- What would count as an unacceptable change or omission?

These questions require judgment. Writing them into a method does not make the judgment reliable automatically. Concrete cases let us examine and improve it.

### 4. The concrete uses have different dependencies

The examples in your question are useful precisely because they do not all do the same thing.

| Operation | What it needs | What it can reveal about substrate |
|---|---|---|
| **Detect an explicitness gap** | The declared text and setting, plus rules for whether the wording demands a particular that the text supplies. | How represented meaning can leave an object, action, criterion or relation unresolved. |

“Operational formatting” also needs a distinction. Reorganizing supplied content is one task. Establishing what the work means operationally is another. If the source contains no supported conclusion, a formatter cannot responsibly produce one merely by adding a “Conclusions” heading.

### 5. Compare the development paths by what they actually require

| Development path | Strongest reason for it | Main limitation | Judgment for the current stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Complete a general substrate first** | Establish shared semantics and avoid incompatible consumers. | Its detailed requirements and useful representation are not yet demonstrated across uses; “complete” has no practical boundary. | Further targeted foundation work can help, but a complete general system is not justified as the next prerequisite. |
| **Build independent uses and combine their requirements afterward** | Learn from real work and obtain useful capabilities early. | Tools may embed incompatible assumptions or never exercise important dimensions. Their fields do not automatically form a coherent substrate. | Keep learning through uses; add deliberate comparison of their underlying meanings and methods. |
| **Develop a bounded use with the understanding and checks it needs** | Produce useful behavior while learning how its working account should be formed. | The use can become too narrow, or expand into the whole system unless its endpoint is clear. | Best-supported next direction for the present learning goal. |
| **Use the task and sources directly, with no separate account** | Avoid an extra representation when ordinary grounded work is sufficient. | Decisions may be difficult to retain, inspect or share in complex cases. | Retain as a legitimate method and a comparison for the value of an explicit account. |
| **Develop a particular shared contribution first** | Resolve a known semantic disagreement, input need or costly repeated reconstruction. | An abstract claim of importance is insufficient to establish its priority. | Prefer when the consumers, common need and benefit of early sharing are concrete. |

The recommended path has a definite development unit: **one useful communication operation, together with the specific understanding needed to perform and inspect it**.

This is more precise than an instruction to develop everything together. It lets us answer what the next effort should produce and what remains outside it.

### 7. How concrete uses should change the substrate

Concrete work can teach us at several levels.

It can reveal a **missing conceptual distinction**. A reply may preserve facts while turning a tentative offer into a promise. That tells us the account must preserve the act and commitment where they matter.

It can reveal a **missing formation step**. The sources may contain both reasons for a decision, but the account consistently selects only one. More complete wording guidance would not fix that selection error.

It can reveal a **representation need**. A handoff between workers may need a retained statement of which claim is reported, inferred or unresolved. The reason to record it comes from the handoff's actual demands.

It can also reveal **unnecessary structure**. If the same task can be performed and checked directly from its sources with less effort, a separate account may not earn its place in that use.

The general map should still challenge the examples. Early operational and explicitness work may say little about disagreement, emotional expression, humor or reception. A local method's success does not remove those from communication.

This is why the substrate will not simply be the union of fields used by tools built so far. We must interpret what each field means, how it is established, who may use it and what remains outside the sampled uses.

### 8. What should be shared, and when?

Sharing is justified when we can identify the same useful contribution across different uses.

For example, several operations might need to distinguish a source-reported claim from an independently established fact. That is a candidate shared principle. If they repeatedly perform the same grounded interpretation, a common method may become useful. A shared record or producer is a further choice, justified by reuse, handoff or maintenance needs.

Ask:

- Do the consumers actually require the same meaning or operation?
- Can they rely on compatible grounds?
- Can one result serve them without losing a qualification or imposing one use's interpretation on another?
- Can we show a case that should remain different?
- Does sharing reduce a demonstrated error, repeated effort or handoff burden?

The existing explicitness detector and fixer show why this matters. Private author material may establish the correct repair, but it must not count as meaning already supplied by the delivered text. Sharing everything would weaken that boundary.

The lightest useful shared result may be a short common reference. A universal format is not the inevitable destination.

### 9. When foundation-first should win

Specific foundational work should move ahead when the practical work exposes a contribution that is missing or valuable to establish in common.

There are several defensible conditions:

- The team cannot agree what the operation should preserve, so candidate outputs cannot be judged coherently.
- Several known consumers use an important term or state incompatibly, and a common meaning must be settled.
- A handoff needs an explicit representation that its producers and consumers can interpret consistently.
- The same formation work is repeatedly reconstructed at substantial cost or with documented errors, and shared treatment addresses that problem.

A temporary supplied input may make consumer development possible without making it the best choice. Early sharing can still be worthwhile when its value is demonstrated.

But a missing fact in one case usually calls first for that fact or an honest limit on the result. It does not automatically justify a general substrate platform.

## Reasoning

The central recommendation survived because it answers both concerns in the question. It gives concrete uses enough grounded meaning to be judged, while giving the substrate method evidence about what it must actually do.

Three strong proposals were rejected:

1. **Feed one complete private account to every communication operation.** This can make a text check count private knowledge as supplied wording. Shared information must respect each operation's comparison target.
2. **Expect independently completed tools to compile automatically into the substrate.** Their assumptions may conflict, and important dimensions may never be exercised. Commonality requires interpretation and deliberate testing.
3. **Define the substrate by merging recurring fields.** Identical names can represent different meanings or evidential states. A union of fields cannot establish those relationships.

The other alternatives were retained or narrowed:

| Proposal examined | Result and reason |
|---|---|
| More common conceptual work before further uses | Retained for an identified conceptual or shared-contract need; rejected as a universal completion gate. |
| Reuse the current foundation and resolve concrete gaps | Retained because the broad map already exists, while particular judgments remain to be exercised. |
| Remove the separate substrate object | Retained as an option; the underlying meaning and grounding responsibilities remain. |
| Use distinct input contracts for different operations | Retained because the actual detector, fixer and communication workflow require different evidence. |
| Use source text alone | Retained for genuinely text-bounded operations; insufficient for unsupported or wholly absent meaning. |
| Save an account before every draft | Narrowed to externalization with a purpose, such as inspection, complexity or handoff. |
| Compare the account and expression against appropriate grounds | Retained because a draft can faithfully express an inadequate account. |
| Let drafting help form the account | Retained when the original task and sources still challenge the result. |
| Develop one bounded use through its full meaning-to-result path | Preferred for the present goal because it produces useful behavior and practical knowledge about formation. |
| Develop a shared contribution first | Retained when a concrete dependency or coordination benefit establishes its value. |
| Extract shared methods after comparing their semantics | Retained; compatible grounds and a case that should remain different constrain the scope. |
| Keep no universal schema | Retained as a legitimate outcome; demonstrated reusable functions may still be shared. |
| Author a new general formation guide immediately | A universal new guide remains unproven. The observed operational failure now supports a targeted bank that distinguishes the two kinds of judgment. |
| Repair existing preparation through cases | The initial application is complete: four revisions changed wording only. Develop and test the treatment of the missed substantive questions. |
| Test existing behavior and retain it if adequate | Retain this principle for other uses. For the reported operational case, the existing guide's application did not address content; another unchanged pass is not the next milestone. |

A related software-design heuristic favors deriving reusable platform support from actual applications. Fowler's [Harvested Platform](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/HarvestedPlatform.html) and [Foundation Platform](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/FoundationPlatform.html) provide that contrast. It is a secondary analogy here, not evidence that the same order is universally best for AI communication. The decisive grounds are the project's actual input contracts, existing foundation and motivating communication records.

## Open Questions

### Research Frontiers

- How reliably can the formation method determine which meanings are necessary when requests are indirect, incomplete or disputed?
- When does an explicit account improve quality enough to justify its creation and maintenance?
- Which treatment genuinely transfers between operational accounts, explanation, interpersonal communication and receiving?
- What parts of meaning require retaining exact expression, timing or history rather than an abstract account?

### Refinement Triggers

- If comparable direct-source work performs as well with less effort, reduce or remove the separate account in that use.
- If several known consumers repeatedly reconstruct the same meaning inconsistently, examine a shared contract or formation function before adding more local versions.
- If a concrete case cannot be judged because the preservation obligation is disputed, clarify that specific concept before claiming a reliable transformation.
- If a local method succeeds on operational cases but changes stance or forces decisions in other uses, revise its scope and underlying account rather than treating the new cases as noise.
- If the available material lacks required substance, distinguish source acquisition or substantive reasoning from communication repair before expanding the substrate implementation.

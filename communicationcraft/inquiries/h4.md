# Communication Craft findings, merged: 8–10 October 2026

# [1] Finding: develop the communication substrate through concrete uses

## Question

What should Communication Craft develop first: the communication substrate, or practical capabilities such as operational communication, explicitness and person-to-person communication?

The underlying concern is a possible circle. We need to understand meaning and preserve it to build good communication tools. Yet building those tools may be how we discover what their supporting understanding needs to contain.

This inquiry compares the development paths, explains their dependencies and recommends a next focus given the foundation and skills already available. It does not implement the next capability.

## Finding

Communication Craft is developing both an understanding of communication and practical skills that AI can apply. The goal is for those two kinds of work to improve each other: the concepts should help us build useful capabilities, and actual communication work should expose where the concepts or methods are incomplete.

The next development effort should address the failure now observed in the operational use: applying the existing guide four times improved wording without addressing content. Use the existing foundation to make those two kinds of judgment explicit, then inspect whether the resulting communication supplies the substantive understanding the task needs.

### 1. Two different meanings of “first” were being combined

Within a particular communication task, we need to establish what we are saying, what supports it and what the exchange needs. Otherwise a fluent message may express an unsupported or incomplete position.

But that does not determine the order in which we must build reusable capabilities.

Suppose we want to develop a method for turning a detailed explanation into a short reply. We can supply the original explanation, the reply's purpose and the meanings that need to survive. That gives us enough to develop and inspect the transformation. We do not first need an automated general system that discovers those inputs for every possible communication.

The distinction is:

| Kind of order | What it concerns |
|---|---|
| **Order within one communication task** | What we need to understand before accepting the particular expression as suitable and faithful. |
| **Order of learning** | Which examples and comparisons reveal what the method needs and where it fails. |
| **Order of building capabilities** | Which reusable instruction, representation or component deserves development now. |

These orders interact. They need not be identical.

The earlier communication formation and review finding already allowed drafting to reveal missing understanding and allowed existing task context to supply the working basis. It did not establish a compulsory separate essence document for every message.

So our earlier understanding remains useful: **establish a grounded basis for the contribution, and keep it accountable to the actual task and sources.** What does not follow is that a complete reusable substrate producer must be our next development project.

### 2. “Build the substrate” names several different jobs

The substrate foundation defines the substrate as a working understanding of a particular communication in its situation. Its essence is the important meaning and relationships within that understanding for the present use.

That leaves several development questions:

| Job | What it would produce | Present position |
|---|---|---|
| **Understand the concept** | A map of what can matter: participants, purpose, content, relationships, acts, stance, grounds, expression and reception. | A substantial provisional foundation exists. |
| **Establish one exchange's understanding** | The particular meaning, situation, support and uncertainty needed here. | Available sources and task context can support this; reliability across communication settings remains unestablished. |
| **Choose a representation** | Prose, a table, annotations or another way to retain that understanding. | Some local forms exist. A universal format is not established. |
| **Develop a repeatable formation method** | Instructions for establishing the right account and checking its adequacy. | Existing workflows perform parts of this. Four reported applications of the operational guide changed wording only, exposing a failure to address content that the next increment must examine. |
| **Create shared substrate support** | A reusable method or component that other capabilities consume. | Its useful scope must be established from actual consumers and evidence. |

The conceptual foundation does not need to be restarted simply because shared substrate support is unfinished.

The fourth job—making formation dependable in practice—is where the next development effort can be most informative. We can pursue it inside a useful communication operation, where its decisions have visible consequences.

### 3. Meaning and fidelity are needed now, at the scale of the operation

Your concern about building the uses without understanding meaning is correct. A method that changes communication needs to know what it is allowed to change and what must remain faithful.

Here, **fidelity means respecting the relevant source meaning, intended contribution and commitments under the transformation being requested**. It does not mean preserving every word or reproducing every available fact.

For example, the saved browser-tool explanation and reply record contains two reasons for declining a proposed restriction. A later short draft retains one and loses the other. If the current task is to explain the material reasoning behind that choice, the missing reason matters. If the task is merely to acknowledge receipt, those reasons may not belong at all.

As a constructed contrast, another change would matter even if both reasons remained: turning “this could reject legitimate interactions” into “this caused regressions” changes a stated risk into an asserted observation. Keeping the same topics would not preserve that distinction. The technical claims in the transcript remain reported claims; this inquiry did not verify the implementation.

We can make these judgments against a bounded source and task before building a comprehensive fidelity system. What we need initially is an inspectable basis for judging the particular operation:

- What should this contribution make understandable?
- Which particular claims, reasons, relationships, acts and qualifications serve that purpose?
- What supports them?
- What must remain uncertain or open?
- What would count as an unacceptable change or omission?

These questions require judgment. Writing them into a method does not make the judgment reliable automatically. Concrete cases let us examine and improve it.

### 4. The concrete uses have different dependencies

The examples in your question are useful precisely because they do not all do the same thing.

| Operation | What it needs | What it can reveal about substrate |
|---|---|---|
| **Detect an explicitness gap** | The declared text and setting, plus rules for whether the wording demands a particular that the text supplies. | How represented meaning can leave an object, action, criterion or relation unresolved. |
| **Repair an explicitness gap** | The detected concern and a supported answer, or a clear statement that the answer is unavailable. | How missing meanings, their sources and consistent wording can be represented locally. |
| **Create an operational account** | The actual work position, relevant findings, practical consequences and the understanding this exchange needs. | How to select and connect substance that the recipient otherwise has to assemble. |
| **Adapt person-to-person communication** | The intended act and stance, the relevant relationship and medium, and the meaning the author is willing to express. | How tone, authority, conditions and commitments affect what the message does. |

The current explicitness detector does not require a complete communication substrate as an input. The separate fixer already constructs a limited missing-meanings table from supported answers. Those are concrete examples of different input and representation needs.

“Operational formatting” also needs a distinction. Reorganizing supplied content is one task. Establishing what the work means operationally is another. If the source contains no supported conclusion, a formatter cannot responsibly produce one merely by adding a “Conclusions” heading.

Likewise, person-to-person work can change more than style. Replacing “I can review this after the scope is agreed” with “I’ll have it finished by Friday” introduces a different commitment. The relevant understanding must constrain the rewrite from the start.

### 5. Compare the development paths by what they actually require

| Development path | Strongest reason for it | Main limitation | Judgment for the current stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Complete a general substrate first** | Establish shared semantics and avoid incompatible consumers. | Its detailed requirements and useful representation are not yet demonstrated across uses; “complete” has no practical boundary. | Further targeted foundation work can help, but a complete general system is not justified as the next prerequisite. |
| **Build independent uses and combine their requirements afterward** | Learn from real work and obtain useful capabilities early. | Tools may embed incompatible assumptions or never exercise important dimensions. Their fields do not automatically form a coherent substrate. | Keep learning through uses; add deliberate comparison of their underlying meanings and methods. |
| **Develop a bounded use with the understanding and checks it needs** | Produce useful behavior while learning how its working account should be formed. | The use can become too narrow, or expand into the whole system unless its endpoint is clear. | Best-supported next direction for the present learning goal. |
| **Use the task and sources directly, with no separate account** | Avoid an extra representation when ordinary grounded work is sufficient. | Decisions may be difficult to retain, inspect or share in complex cases. | Retain as a legitimate method and a comparison for the value of an explicit account. |
| **Develop a particular shared contribution first** | Resolve a known semantic disagreement, input need or costly repeated reconstruction. | An abstract claim of importance is insufficient to establish its priority. | Prefer when the consumers, common need and benefit of early sharing are concrete. |

The recommended path has a definite development unit: **one useful communication operation, together with the specific understanding needed to perform and inspect it**.

This is more precise than an instruction to develop everything together. It lets us answer what the next effort should produce and what remains outside it.

### 6. What I would focus on next

**Continue developing the operational use by separating and testing its expression checks and content questions. Use the four wording-only revisions as the starting evidence.**

I would start with an operational account because we have an actual need and relevant guidance to work from. In the recorded exchange with the manager, the recipient asks for the author's understanding, conclusions and operational progress. They want an assembled explanation. They also explicitly say not to follow their suggested structure verbatim.

The original update already contains substance, including a proposed starting point and outstanding requirements. The development question is how to use that substance to supply the requested understanding, while keeping prepared guidance, proposals and completed operational results distinct.

That is a general communication problem illustrated by this case. It does not require building a “brief the manager” skill.

The operational communication reference has now been applied four times, with wording fixes only. The earlier advice to try it first is therefore complete. The inbound communication workflow and supported revision protocol remain useful starting material, but their existence does not establish that the needed content judgment was performed.

The related finding on materialization and content checks makes the next distinction concrete:

- **Materialization: is the settled meaning well expressed?** Inspect the wording, organization, view and detail against the intended meaning and recipient's need. Repair expression from established meaning.
- **Content: is the meaning supported and sound for this request?** Inspect claims, selected reasons, status, commitments and the proposed arrangement against available sources and reasoning. A missing answer may require information, a substantive judgment or a change to the work.

For example, stating each item's status clearly and establishing that each status is true are separate judgments. An operational check should identify which question it asks, what evidence it uses and what remains unchecked. Content concerns can be raised alongside supported rewriting; they need not stop independent improvements or require a new general content-checking system.

The development sequence now starts from that observed failure:

1. **Use the four revisions and their task as the initial case.** Identify which substantive questions remained unanswered and what material was available. The trial of the existing guide is already done; the next effort should diagnose its result.
2. **Separate the operational checks by what they judge.** Develop explicit materialization questions and separate content questions. Where one guide item covers both, retain both with their respective inputs and remedies.
3. **Establish the particular meaning and grounds.** Use available sources and authorized reasoning to supply conclusions, relationships and qualifications. Identify content questions needing an author answer, unavailable information or underlying work.
4. **Produce and inspect the communication.** Improve expression using established meaning, and show unresolved content concerns separately. Report which judgments were actually made; fluent wording does not establish that the content is right.
5. **Test whether the failed behavior changes.** Compare with the wording-only revisions: are the previously missed content issues now identified and either resolved on grounds or returned as specific unmet needs? Distinguish a detected gap from a completed substantive answer.
6. **Try a materially different use.** A short explanation preserving reasons, or a person-to-person response preserving stance and commitment, can expose requirements the operational case did not exercise.
7. **Decide what deserves shared treatment.** Extract common principles or formation operations where the evidence supports them. Keep local requirements local.

The next deliverable should contain operational questions with their kind, inputs, remedy and limits stated, together with worked communications showing their application. It should demonstrate the treatment of substantive gaps as well as expression problems. The value of a separate working-account document remains a further comparison against equally grounded direct work.

This recommendation is based on the current learning gap and available material. It is not a measured claim that operational communication has the highest return of every possible next capability.

### 7. How concrete uses should change the substrate

Concrete work can teach us at several levels.

It can reveal a **missing conceptual distinction**. A reply may preserve facts while turning a tentative offer into a promise. That tells us the account must preserve the act and commitment where they matter.

It can reveal a **missing formation step**. The sources may contain both reasons for a decision, but the account consistently selects only one. More complete wording guidance would not fix that selection error.

It can reveal a **representation need**. A handoff between workers may need a retained statement of which claim is reported, inferred or unresolved. The reason to record it comes from the handoff's actual demands.

It can also reveal **unnecessary structure**. If the same task can be performed and checked directly from its sources with less effort, a separate account may not earn its place in that use.

The general map should still challenge the examples. Early operational and explicitness work may say little about disagreement, emotional expression, humor or reception. A local method's success does not remove those from communication.

This is why the substrate will not simply be the union of fields used by tools built so far. We must interpret what each field means, how it is established, who may use it and what remains outside the sampled uses.

### 8. What should be shared, and when?

Sharing is justified when we can identify the same useful contribution across different uses.

For example, several operations might need to distinguish a source-reported claim from an independently established fact. That is a candidate shared principle. If they repeatedly perform the same grounded interpretation, a common method may become useful. A shared record or producer is a further choice, justified by reuse, handoff or maintenance needs.

Ask:

- Do the consumers actually require the same meaning or operation?
- Can they rely on compatible grounds?
- Can one result serve them without losing a qualification or imposing one use's interpretation on another?
- Can we show a case that should remain different?
- Does sharing reduce a demonstrated error, repeated effort or handoff burden?

The existing explicitness detector and fixer show why this matters. Private author material may establish the correct repair, but it must not count as meaning already supplied by the delivered text. Sharing everything would weaken that boundary.

The lightest useful shared result may be a short common reference. A universal format is not the inevitable destination.

### 9. When foundation-first should win

Specific foundational work should move ahead when the practical work exposes a contribution that is missing or valuable to establish in common.

There are several defensible conditions:

- The team cannot agree what the operation should preserve, so candidate outputs cannot be judged coherently.
- Several known consumers use an important term or state incompatibly, and a common meaning must be settled.
- A handoff needs an explicit representation that its producers and consumers can interpret consistently.
- The same formation work is repeatedly reconstructed at substantial cost or with documented errors, and shared treatment addresses that problem.

A temporary supplied input may make consumer development possible without making it the best choice. Early sharing can still be worthwhile when its value is demonstrated.

But a missing fact in one case usually calls first for that fact or an honest limit on the result. It does not automatically justify a general substrate platform.

### 10. What this inquiry establishes

The conceptual circle is resolved: **we can understand enough to develop a bounded use, learn from that use, and improve the supporting method without completing a general substrate system first.**

The substantive next focus remains the formation and use of grounded meaning through operational communication. The four wording-only revisions now give that work a concrete failure to address: expression improvements occurred without the necessary content judgment. The existing explicitness work remains useful within its declared scope.

The immediate test is whether distinguishing these judgments prevents another wording-only response when substantive questions remain. Wider questions also remain open: how reliably the formation method identifies what matters, and how much an explicit account improves quality and effort compared with direct source-grounded work. These need practical evidence; another complete conceptual foundation is not a prerequisite.

# [2] Finding: two kinds of check in Communication Craft: is it well expressed, and is it right?

## Question

Communication Craft is the communication part of Workcraft, the user's framework for automating work with AI. Its working model separates two things:
- the **working essence**: what a contribution means to convey, with its reasons, claims, decisions and qualifications;
- the **materialization**: the actual text that expresses it.

While designing an operational question bank, a split appeared:
- some problems in the manager's feedback could be found from the text;
- others ("four people for a 15-minute job") were problems with the plan itself, which no reading of the text can settle.

The user asked to make this distinction explicit for every communication:
- one check of the **materialization**;
- one check of the **content itself**: whether it is right and sensible for the request. This needs context, not just the text.

What exactly is each, and how do we tell them apart?

## Finding

### 1. Why this matters now

Two things happened on 8 October.

**Seven examples from the manager's feedback were collected,** and they don't all fail the same way:
- "You pointed me to a 6,200-word document" can be fixed by writing the answer into the message.
- "Why are we overcomplicating this with four people involved?" cannot. The arrangement itself had to change, however clearly it was described.

**The AI rewrote an operational draft four times after being pointed at `operational-communication.md`.** Each round fixed wording, and none of the four rounds touched content. That works when the problem is wording. It fails silently when the problem is the plan.

The user's point: unless the two kinds of check are named and kept apart, we keep making these mistakes:
- polishing text whose meaning is wrong;
- trusting a text check as if it vouched for the content;
- giving an AI a mixed checklist and getting only the easy half applied.

### 2. The two checks

| | **Materialization check** | **Content check** |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Is the meaning well expressed? | Is the meaning right? |
| What it looks at | the text, against the intended meaning and the recipient's need | the intended meaning, against sources, the request and the work |
| Covers | explicitness; order (the point before what depends on it); view and level (operational vs technical, process vs single case); length and leading with the decision; readability; no unintended implication | **faithful:** claims, status and commitments true to sources and authorised; **sound:** the answer exists, the plan makes sense, the arrangement is proportionate, it serves the request |
| Inputs | the text, the declared recipient need, the essence where written | sources (Context Accumulation), the request (Alignment), reasoning (Semantic Formulation) |
| Fixed by | rewriting | changing the meaning, getting information, doing the work |
| Can be a question bank? | yes (explicitness exists; operational presentation is next) | faithful: partly, when sources are supplied (quote the source); sound: only as questions to the author or a reasoner, never as a verdict from text |

The existing substrate model already holds this split, unnamed:
- The 6 October finding on essence, materialization and review routes each problem to its fix: re-express it, correct the essence, or obtain information and do the work (`devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_19-33__communication-substrate-materialization-and-review-loop/finding.md`).
- It also lists four comparisons. Grounding of the essence is content; realization and intelligibility are materialization; fit is split.

What it lacked:
- the two names;
- a test between them;
- a rule that each check declares its kind.

### 3. The test: can the right meaning be quoted?

"Could rewriting fix it?" isn't enough on its own: an AI can always claim the author "knew" the meaning. The observable version:

- **Materialization:** the missing or wrongly expressed meaning can be **quoted from what the author has already settled.** That means the written essence, the author's own document or earlier version of the message, or an author answer. The meaning was decided; the text lost or garbled it. Rewriting uses that quote.
  - In Example 3, the answer was in the author's own framing document, so it was settled meaning that the message didn't carry.
- **Content (selection or faithfulness):** the meaning exists **only in the sources**, such as a reason in a reply or a qualification in a record, and was never selected into what is being said. Finding it takes reading the sources, which is the context work the content check needs, even when the fix afterwards is a simple rewrite.
  - The coworker's endorsement is this kind: their reply held the limits; the summary never took them in.
- **Content (missing or wrong):** no quote exists anywhere, or the available quote contradicts what the message says.
- **Unsure:** ask the author. The answer either supplies settled meaning (now materialization) or shows the meaning was never settled (content).

*Corrected on 2026-10-08.* The first version counted any source as settled meaning, which put "a reason dropped from the sources" on the materialization side. That contradicts the definition: a check that has to read the sources is a content check. The correction was prompted by comparison with `devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_12-40__communication-substrate-development-order/finding.md`. It is adopted because it follows from this finding's own definition, not because that finding said so.

This is the explicitness fix skill's rule ("change text only with a quoted basis, otherwise ask") generalised. The fix skill already sorts its work this way:
- "fixed from the text" is materialization;
- "asked of the author" is mostly a content gap the text exposed.

**Problems are classified by what is at fault, not by the tool that found them.** A text-only check reads only text, but it can still reveal a content gap. If no sentence states the position, perhaps none was formed. So text checks also act as **symptom detectors** for content problems. When a materialization question finds nothing to quote anywhere, it becomes a content question for the author.

### 4. Cases with both halves

Some problems sit across the line. Don't force them into one kind; split them into a text question and a truth question.

| Case | Text question (materialization) | Truth question (content) |
|---|---|---|
| A status claim ("that was the last thing in the way") | Does the text give each item's status: closed, estimated, awaiting verification, open? | Is that status true? |
| Answering the request | Does the text answer the recipient's questions, early, in the right view? | Does an answer exist? Is it the right question? |
| A handoff or arrangement | Does the text say why each handoff is needed? | Is the arrangement proportionate? |
| A request to the recipient | Is the ask stated clearly? | Can this recipient actually answer or decide it? |

### 5. How the real cases fall

| Case | Kind | What actually fixed it, or would |
|---|---|---|
| 3. Revised document described instead of the answer | materialization | state the answer from the document |
| 4. Stages without why that order | materialization | give the dependency; the manager: "makes sense now that you put it that way" |
| 7. Five documents, about 26,000 words, not distilled | mostly materialization; content where conclusions weren't yet formed | distil; form conclusions where missing |
| Today's drafts: "concerns of any age", "checking what still applies", a 50-word sentence | materialization | rewrite |
| Today's draft: the FreeAgent example | materialization (wrong level: one case instead of the process) | rewrite at process level |
| 2. "That was the last thing in the way" | both | state each status; verify it |
| 1. One evaluation explained, not the continuing operation | content (sound) first: the operation wasn't designed yet | design the operation, then explain it |
| 5. Four people for a 15-minute job | content (sound) | change the arrangement |
| 6. Who does this instance vs who can run the process | content (sound) | change the operating model |
| A coworker's prompt-level endorsement summarised as "signed off" | content (faithful) | narrow the claim to what they said |

Every case sorts, with nothing left over. Examples 1 and 7 need both kinds, in order: settle the content, then express it.

### 6. How the existing checks fall

| Check | Kind |
|---|---|
| Explicitness bank (v6.7.2) | materialization. Its "not supplied" answers can raise content questions |
| O1 states the position | split: stated (materialization) / formed (content) |
| O2 progress states what is actually possible | split: status stated / status true |
| O3 requests explicit and answerable | explicit (materialization) / answerable by this recipient (content: sound) |
| O4 responsibilities | stated (materialization) / accurate (content: faithful) |
| O5 route forward understandable | understandable (materialization) / readiness supported (content) |
| M1 the requested answer is present | split: present but buried / never formed |
| M2, M3, M5 claims, status scope, reasoning | content: faithful (M5's missing inference step is materialization) |
| M6, M7 corrections and meaning survive revision | materialization: the meaning was settled, and the new text lost it |
| P1–P3 purpose, detail, layout | materialization, given the declared need |
| P4 voice | materialization; an invented offer in the author's name is content |
| Supporting Context I1, I7 | materialization (state what is and isn't established) |
| Supporting Context I5 history | content: faithful |

**The operational checks are almost all mixed.** Each one asks "is it stated?" and "is it true or sound?" in the same item. An AI given such a check tends to do the half it can do from the text. That is what happened today.

### 7. Where this sits

- **The substrate model:**
  - content checks the **contribution** (what is meant, its relations, its grounds);
  - materialization checks the **expression** against it.
- **The three families of communication problems:**
  - intelligibility is materialization;
  - fidelity is content (faithful);
  - **fit splits:** how the answer is presented is materialization; whether there is a right answer is content (sound).
- **Workcraft:**
  - Communication owns the materialization check and the **content interface**: it records what the essence claims and on what grounds, and raises content questions. It never certifies content from text.
  - Context Accumulation supplies the sources for faithfulness.
  - Semantic Formulation develops missing answers.
  - Alignment compares the meaning with the request.
- **Receiving:** the same split. "Do I understand what they mean?" is a materialization judgment. "Is it right, and do I accept it?" is a content judgment.

The pattern is familiar from editing practice: a copy editor fixes expression, a fact-checker checks claims against sources, and an editor judges whether the piece holds up. Those match materialization, faithful and sound. Software's "built it right" against "built the right thing" is the same cut. Both parallels are cited from memory.

### 8. What changes in practice

1. **Every check declares its kind:**
   - materialization or content (faithful / sound);
   - the inputs it needs;
   - how its findings are fixed;
   - what it cannot vouch for.
2. **Every report says what it did not check.** For example: "Checked: materialization (explicitness, order, view). Not checked: content (claims against sources; whether the plan is sound)", followed by any content questions raised.
3. **The operational bank is built in two halves:**
   - materialization questions answered from the text: is the position stated, is each status's class stated, is the reason for each step's order stated, is the reason for each handoff stated, the sentence roles;
   - content questions for the author: does a position exist? is a simpler arrangement possible? are these statuses true?
4. **When drafting, show the content questions alongside the rewriting, not as a gate.** Don't polish a plan before its content questions have been asked. This ordering is untested.
5. **Now vs later:**
   - **Now: materialization side.** Built as banks.
   - **Now: content side, in ordinary work.** Content questions go to the author. When a task already comes with its material (the replies, documents and records it is about), the content check is done against that material, by hand or by the drafting agent. This is the ordinary faithful work the 6 October focus finding allowed. No new content-checking tool is built now.
   - **Later:** a general content check that finds and refreshes its own sources, when Context Accumulation is integrated (deferred on 6 October).
6. **Where the bank fits in development.** The 12-40 finding recommends developing one real operational use end to end: establish the meaning, express it, inspect it, improve the part that failed. The two-half operational bank is the *inspection* step of that path, and its content questions also serve the "establish the meaning" step.
   - That finding also advises applying existing guidance before building new guidance. That has now been done: the AI applied `operational-communication.md` four times on 8 October and fixed only wording. That evidence is why a split bank is justified.

# [3] Finding: how an essence gets formed: what we now understand, and the better questions

## Question

In Communication Craft, the **essence** is what a contribution means to convey for a particular exchange: its points, reasons, qualifications, decisions and what stays open. It is written or expressed afterwards as text (the materialization).

Earlier work described *what* the essence is. The 2026-10-06 substrate foundation listed five formation activities:
- establish the need;
- establish the meanings that matter;
- establish the grounds;
- keep what is unknown open;
- revise.

It did not say *how* formation actually happens:
- which parts get filled;
- from what material;
- in what order;
- when it is "enough".

The 12-40 finding named this the most informative gap, and so far it is left to whoever writes.

The user asked for **wider understanding and better questions, not a fixed definition**. Defining too early was a costly mistake in the explicitness work.

## Finding

### 1. From "fill the facets" to "answer the act's questions"

The substrate map has 14 facets in four groups (situation, contribution, expression, reception). It describes what *can* matter in communication. Treating it as a form to fill would produce bulky, unfocused essences: most facets are irrelevant to a given message.

What the real cases show instead: formation is driven by the recipient's need.
- The need is what they must be able to do after the exchange.
- The need raises questions.
- The essence is the answers.

This builds on `devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_12-53__what-determines-the-view-combination-an-exchange-needs/finding.md`. It also fits the 12-40 finding's bounded questions:
- what should this make understandable;
- which claims and reasons serve that;
- what supports them;
- what must stay open;
- what change would be unacceptable.

The facets keep a role: **a final sweep.** After answering the questions, ask whether anything was missed about stance, grounds or relationships. The questions do the work; the facets catch what the questions didn't ask.

**The order is per question, not per facet.** The need comes first. Each question is then worked on its own. Sometimes an answer **sends you back to the need**. For example, discovering there is no decision ready to ask for changes the message from "decide" to "here's where we are". That loop-back is part of formation, not a failure of it.

### 2. The moves, and who can do them

Seen so far (an open list, not a taxonomy):

| Move | What it needs | Who can usually do it | Real case where it was skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| Already settled | the author's decided meaning | anyone, if it's written down | — |
| Gather from sources | access to the material | an AI, showing quotes | the coworker: the limits were in the reply |
| Select for this exchange | the declared need | an AI, if the need is declared | Ex 7: everything described, nothing selected |
| Produce (judge, recommend, design) | reasoning and often authority | **the author**, or an AI proposing for the author to approve | Ex 1: the operation never designed |
| Ground (source + status) | sources | an AI | Ex 2: statuses unclassified |
| Leave open deliberately | the author's intent | **the author** | — |
| Test the plan | reasoning against the request | an AI can raise it; the author decides | Ex 5, 6 |

Possible moves not yet seen in cases: reconciling sources that disagree, and choosing a stance.

**Companions.** Each selected answer should bring along what keeps it true and interpretable: its qualifications, its reasons, its conditions. Ask this from the material ("what in the sources qualifies or explains this?"), not from imagination.

**Today's drafts show what silent formation looks like.** The AI went straight from the analysis to text. The level (process vs single case), the selection (the FreeAgent example) and the term choices ("concerns of any age") were all formation decisions, made invisibly inside the writing. The user's four corrections were corrections to formation, delivered as text edits.

### 3. Forward and backward formation

**Forward:** need → questions → answers with moves and companions → text.

**Backward**, which fits how AI is used today: draft → extract what it claims, selects, decides and leaves out, each quoted from the draft → compare:
- with the need's questions, where unanswered questions show omissions;
- with the sources and settled meaning, where unquotable assertions show silent decisions.

Backward formation is only trustworthy with that outside comparison. An extract alone shows what the draft says, never what it forgot. This repeats the 19-33 warning that an essence can't find its own omissions.

There may be one deeper point (untested): **forming and reviewing could be the same operation run in opposite directions.** If so, one record could serve composing and both checks from the 13-08 finding:
- materialization: text against the record;
- content: the record against sources and need.

### 4. What "enough" means

At least two readings must both hold:
- **Coverage:** every question the act raises is answered, or explicitly marked open or asked.
- **Sufficiency:** from this essence alone, the recipient could do their act.

A possible grounded probe: give a fresh reader only the essence and the act's questions, and see what it can answer by quoting.

Two more readings to keep in view:
- **grounding:** each answer has its source and status;
- **proportion:** further work wouldn't change what the recipient would do.

**When to write the essence down at all** is still open. A three-line Slack reply probably doesn't need it. A decision request to a manager probably does. Stakes, length, number of recipients and how unsettled the material is probably matter, but none of this is tested.

### 5. Creating vs transforming

- **Creating** a message asks "what must be said?".
- **Transforming** one (shortening, replying, adapting for another reader) mostly asks **"what must not be lost?"**. A preservation map of the source's key relations (reasons, conditions, limits) would have kept the dropped second reason.

### 6. Questions the recipient already gave us

Some formation questions are supplied by the recipient:
- The manager's five sections (where we are, what I've concluded, what I've put in place, what's next, what I need);
- their 25 August list (decisions, key evidence, recommendations, material unknowns, owners, next actions);
- the expectations file.

These outrank questions we derive ourselves, in line with the sourcing order in the recipient-need finding (the request first, then standing arrangements). They also stop formation from being invented from scratch every time.

### 7. The better questions (ranked by how much they would unlock)

1. **Can backward formation surface what today's four drafts silently decided?** Extract, compare with the need and sources, list the unquotable assertions. Your four corrections are the answer key. This is the cheapest strong test.
2. **Which moves can an AI do alone, and which must go to the author?** Record who actually settled each answer in the manager's cases.
3. **Does a sufficiency probe catch "covered but not usable"?** Test on example 7 and its revised version.
4. **Do the move labels (settled / from source / to produce / open / ask) separate the manager's cases the way the materialization/content split predicts?**
5. **Is a small core enough for most operational messages:** purpose, key points with their companions, and the ask?
6. **Can a preservation map catch dropped relations before a transformation is written?**
7. **What signals that formation has turned into substantive work** (mostly "to produce" answers), so the right response is to stop writing and do the work?
8. **When does writing the essence down pay for itself?**
9. **Are there moves we haven't seen?** Reconciling sources and choosing a stance are candidates.
10. **Do other kinds of message** (person-to-person, technical explanation) stress different moves?

# [4] Finding: The three fundamental meaning gaps of Communication Craft

## Question

What are the three most fundamental meaning gaps in Communication Craft?

**Communication Craft** is the communication part of Workcraft, the project's work methodology: the theory and the skills for:
- creating communication;
- receiving it;
- assessing it;
- improving it;
- transforming it (shortening, replying, translating, re-targeting).

A **meaning gap** here is a concept the craft depends on but has not defined well enough to build on. The user wants three things:
- the three gaps;
- why each is fundamental;
- what it would take to close each, so they can be well defined before the framework is built.

The answer must hold for any kind of communication, not only operational reports.

## Finding

### 1. Why we are asking this

The communication work so far produced:
- one measured capability: the explicitness question bank and detector;
- several concept findings:
  - the **communication substrate**: a 14-facet map of what can matter in an exchange;
  - the **essence**: the important meaning for the present use;
  - the **recipient's need**;
  - the split between checking the *text* and checking the *content*.

Each new skill so far has quietly invented its own standard of "good". The operational checks are the clearest example: each one mixed "is it well worded?" with "is it true and sensible?". An AI pointed at them produced fluent rewrites and missed the real problems.

The user's request is to stop and identify the few concepts everything rests on, so the framework is built on defined ground.

### 2. The test for "fundamental"

A gap counts as fundamental only if it passes both tests:
- **Dependency:** several Communication Craft operations or checks cannot be defined or run without it.
- **Failure:** observed failures trace back to it being undefined.

Twelve candidates were gathered:
- from the earlier findings;
- from the five operations;
- from observed failures;
- from neighbouring fields: rhetoric, pragmatics, translation theory, requirements engineering, contract interpretation.

### 3. The key observation: every check is a comparison

Listing what each existing check compares exposes four things:

| Check | Compares | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Is the meaning well expressed? (materialization check) | the text | the meaning the author has settled |
| Does it answer what was needed? Is it complete, and without excess? (the fit checks) | the meaning | the recipient's need |
| Is it supported, and faithful to the original? (the fidelity checks) | the meaning | the sources, or the earlier version |
| Is it explicit and unambiguous? (the intelligibility checks) | the text | what the text itself makes available |

Two of the four things are concrete files: the **text** and the **sources**. The other two, the **need** and the **meaning**, are abstract, and both are thinly defined.

The **relation** between a text and what it conveys is the third thing everything leans on. Only one corner of it is defined.

These are the three gaps.

### 4. Gap 1: the need

**What it is.** What this communication must achieve, and for whom.

**What we have.** An earlier finding defines it, in its words, as *"what the recipient must be able to do after the exchange (their next act), and therefore which questions they must have answered."* The finding is devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_12-53__what-determines-the-view-combination-an-exchange-needs/finding.md, and that definition lives in its summary. It also says the need is never guessed: it is taken from the request, a standing arrangement, the workflow, asking the recipient, or a stated assumption, in that order.

**What is undefined.**
- **Whose need counts.**
  - Candidates: the recipient, the sender's purpose, the task, third parties, later readers.
  - When they conflict, how the conflict is shown rather than hidden.
- **What form a need takes.**
  - "Questions to be answered" works for briefings and decisions.
  - A condolence note or a thank-you has no questions. Its need is a state to reach ("the recipient feels their loss is recognised").
- **How a need is found when there is no request and no known reader.** Examples: reference documents, standing documents, broadcasts.
- **How a need is stated so that coverage can be tested.** It must be stated as questions or states, not topics. A message can cover every topic and still leave the recipient unable to act.

**Why it is fundamental.** The need decides:
- what to select;
- what counts as important;
- what the fit checks measure;
- whether the result is usable;
- what a transformation must preserve.

The best-known failures are needs that were missed or guessed: polished briefings the reader couldn't use, and the AI silently choosing the level of detail.

**Hard core:** the unit of need.

**Closed when:**
- one stated form expresses the needs of at least five genres without forcing them;
- the form exposes the "covered every topic, still unusable" failure;
- there is a rule for whose need governs and how conflicts are recorded.

### 5. Gap 2: the meaning

**What it is.** What the communication conveys, held as an object that can be quoted, checked and preserved.

**What we have.** The substrate finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_22-09__communication-substrate-foundation-and-dimensions/finding.md) says: *"Essence names the important meaning and relationships in that account for the present use."* It also says the essence *"need not designate"* a separate document.

The materialization-vs-content finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_13-08__materialization-check-vs-content-check-in-communication-craft/finding.md) decides what kind of problem a gap is by whether the right meaning can be *quoted* from what the author has settled.

These two do not fit together:
- an essence that is allowed to stay unwritten cannot be quoted;
- "important for the present use" depends on the need, which is itself undefined generically.

**What is undefined.**
- **The unit.**
  - What is one piece of meaning: a claim, an answer to a need question, an act (a request, a refusal), a stance, an open item?
  - **The relations between pieces.** Examples: "therefore", "unless", "in contrast". The substrate finding notes that much of the meaning lives in these.
- **What must travel with each unit:**
  - its **companions**: reasons, conditions, qualifications, limits;
  - its **status**: settled by the author; gathered from a source; produced fresh; left open on purpose; to be asked; proposed by an AI but not confirmed; found in a draft; attributed by a reader; superseded;
  - its **basis**: which source, which person.
- **Held meaning vs this communication's meaning.**
  - Held meaning is everything known or settled.
  - This communication's meaning is what is selected or produced for this need.
- **When the exact wording is itself the meaning.** Examples: quotations, "might" vs "will", contractual terms.

**Why it is fundamental.**
- It is the object of:
  - the content check;
  - the target of the wording check;
  - what fidelity and preservation protect;
  - what formation produces;
  - what receiving reconstructs.
- The failures traced to it are:
  - decisions made silently, with no status recorded;
  - dropped second reasons and lost limits (missing companions);
  - checks that mixed kinds, because there was no meaning object to check against.

**Hard core:** the unit of meaning, with its attachments and relations.

**Closed when:**
- the units represent three different genres without losing their relations;
- a dropped reason or an unconfirmed AI decision shows up as a missing attachment or a status;
- the definition agrees with the quote test from the materialization-vs-content finding.

### 6. Gap 3: conveyance

**What it is.** What a text actually makes available to its recipient: stated, connected, and singled out. Also what it may leave to things outside the text.

"Conveyance" is a working label. The plain description is the definition.

**What we have.** The explicitness bank (the project's question bank for finding places where the wording needs something the text doesn't single out) defines one corner: two tests, demand and sufficiency, judged on the text alone. It is the only closed piece of Communication Craft. It closed by defining a relation precisely, not by collecting more examples.

**What is undefined.**
- **What counts as supplied.**
  - Candidates: the text, the stated setting, earlier turns in the exchange, conventions of the genre, declared shared ground.
  - What never counts: undeclared reader knowledge. This keeps the user's earlier correction ("detect the text's explicitness, not reader knowledge").
- **The principled boundary of what may be left implicit.**
  - The bank's record of persistent misses shows exemption rules spreading: "there is one end state", "the point is about the whole".
  - Each was decided case by case, because this boundary has no principle.
- **What conveyance covers beyond singling things out:**
  - connections and order;
  - which act the text performs (is it a request or a report?);
  - meaning the text conveys that was not intended.
- **How receiving rebuilds meaning from a text,** and how to check the rebuild.

**Why it is fundamental.** It underlies:
- every intelligibility check;
- every review of a written text;
- the entire receiving direction.

The failures traced to it are the explicitness detector's gaps and its spreading exemptions.

**Hard core:** the boundary of the implicit.

**Closed when:**
- one stated principle decides the detector's exemption cases that are now handled by separate rules;
- each intelligibility peer maps to a conveyance question. The peers are ambiguity, vagueness, connection, order and implication, from the 15-peer problem taxonomy.

### 7. How everything else reduces to the three

| Candidate | Reduces to |
|---|---|
| **Adequacy** ("is it good enough?") | The verdict over the three: every need unit is answered or marked open, every answer is grounded, and every answer is conveyed. Plus one **grounded sufficiency probe**: a fresh reader with only the text answers the need's questions, and the answers are compared quote by quote with the meaning. The probe catches a need that was stated incompletely. |
| **Grounds and authority** | Each meaning unit's status and basis. |
| **Preservation** in transformations | The same held meaning, under a new need, with a list of units that must survive. Translation theory treats the purpose of the new text as what decides this, which supports the reduction. |
| **Importance / selection** | The need selecting meaning units. |
| **Shared knowledge** | Conveyance's "what counts as supplied". |
| **Reception** | Conveyance run backwards. |
| **Time** (meaning changing over turns) | Successive needs and texts, with meaning units marked "superseded". |
| **Register / relationship** | A state-type need ("feels respected") + a stance unit in the meaning + tone as wording-bound conveyance. This works only if state-type needs work. |
| **The unit of communication; the boundary with other Workcraft concepts; openness** | The meaning unit; the existing text-vs-content split; the "left open" status. |

Requirements engineering ended up with the same three foundations:
- validation (is it the right thing?) is the need against the meaning;
- the specification is the meaning;
- verification (is it built right?) is the meaning against the text.

Its main closing tool was **traceability**: every requirement traces to a need, every part of the product to a requirement. That transfers directly here:
- every meaning unit traces to a need unit;
- every meaning unit traces to the place in the text that conveys it.

### 8. A possible shape for how the gaps close (hypothesis only)

Combining the three suggests one structure:
- a list of **need units**;
- **meaning units** that answer them, each with its companions, status and basis, and with the relations between them;
- for each meaning unit, **the place in the text that conveys it**, or a mark that it isn't conveyed.

Every existing check becomes one comparison along one of those links. Receiving is the same structure filled in backwards from the text.

This is offered as **a hypothesis to test, not a definition.** The substrate finding deliberately deferred a universal schema. The structure has not yet been built for a real text.

### 9. Order of work

- **Define first, jointly:** the need unit and the meaning unit. They form a contract: whatever a need unit is, meaning units must be able to answer it.
- **Then in parallel:**
  - the rest of the need (whose need; sourcing without a request);
  - the meaning's attachments and status vocabulary;
  - conveyance's boundary of the implicit.

  The boundary of the implicit does not depend on the meaning unit, because it is judged on the text.
- **In a single case,** start from the need. **Across the framework,** the meaning is the most depended-on gap.

# [5] Finding: The need — what a communication must achieve, and for whom

## Question

**What is a communication's "need": what it must achieve, and for whom?** And what is one unit of need: a question to answer, or a state the recipient should reach?

The prior definition was "the recipient's next act → the questions it raises". It does not cover three cases:
- several parties wanting different things;
- unknown or later readers, such as documentation;
- purposes that aren't acts, such as thanks, condolence, or keeping a relationship.

The answer had to:
- be generic across all communication;
- go deep;
- not over-define: understanding and tests, not a schema.

## Finding

### 1. Why this matters

Every Communication Craft check that asks "does this message fit?" needs something to compare against. That includes being responsive, being complete, and not carrying what nobody needs.

That something is the need. Our only definition came from briefings: the manager's "next act" and the questions it raises. It breaks on documentation, on thank-yous, and on messages with several readers.

So this inquiry asked what a need is in general, and what its smallest unit is.

### 2. The state: what a communication is for

**The common element in every case** is a state that should be true for some party after the communication, and that was not true before.

| Case | The state |
|---|---|
| Briefing | The manager can decide whether to back the next steps. |
| Documentation | A future developer can call the endpoint correctly. |
| Thanks | The helper knows that what they did was noticed and valued. |
| Condolence | The bereaved knows the loss is recognised, and that support is there without demand. |
| Warning | The on-call engineer knows the risk before their next shift, *so that* the outage is avoided. |
| Agent prompt | The agent completes the task within the constraints, and knows when it is done. |

**Three properties make a state statement useful.**

- **It names a party.** "Inform the team" names no state. "The on-call engineer knows the rollback changed" does.
- **It is set by someone with standing.** That might be the requester, a standing arrangement, the workflow, the document's role, or the sender for unsolicited messages. The setter is recorded, so a check can see when the sender set the state on behalf of someone else.
- **It is relative to what is established.** What is already settled between the parties does not need saying. Established means:
  - earlier turns in the conversation;
  - shared documents;
  - the stated setting.

  It never means what the reader privately knows. This keeps the earlier rule against guessing reader knowledge.

**A one-sentence precision test**, not a form: can you say who, after the communication, can, knows or feels what, and where the source for that is?

**Where the situation fits.** A need could be said to belong to the situation: "the outage must be avoided". But it can only be checked through a party who can act. So the state stays with a party, and the situation goes in a "so that" clause.

### 3. Requirements: what the text must carry

A state produces requirements. These are what checks compare against, because a text can be quoted for them.

**Questions to answer (informational states).**
- To *decide*, you need: what is to be decided, the recommendation and why, the options, the evidence and unknowns, and who does what next.
- The prior finding's act table stays as the default source of these questions. Views stay as labels on them.

**Conditions to meet (expressive and relational states).** A condition belongs to the need only if, without it, the state cannot be reached.

- **Core conditions** come from what the act itself is for:
  - **Thanking** is about something the other person did, so it must name it.
  - **Apologising** is about something the sender did, so it must own it.
  - **Condoling** is about the other person's loss, so it must acknowledge it and expect nothing back.

  These are close to universal.
- **Local conditions** must be sourced: how warm, how formal, what is customary in this team or culture. They come from a quotable convention or the relationship. Otherwise they are a visible assumption.
- This also settles the earlier disagreement about register (voice and tone). It is partly the need (relational conditions) and partly the relationship (local conventions).

**Constraints to respect (must-nots).**
- Examples:
  - do not commit the sender to something they can't promise;
  - do not reveal X;
  - do not ask anything of the bereaved;
  - stay within a length.
- They come from the setter or from affected parties.
- Conditions and constraints share one form ("must" / "must not"). They are kept apart because they come from different places.

**For an AI recipient**, the requirements are the acceptance criteria. The agent cannot reliably ask back "how will I know it's done?", so completeness matters more.

### 4. For whom: three roles

The prior definition had one recipient. Real communication has three roles.

| Role | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Holder** | a party who must reach a state (primary or secondary, present or later) | PR reviewer (primary), future maintainer (secondary) |
| **Setter** | who has standing to decide what the communication is for | the requester; a standing arrangement; the document's role; the sender for a warning |
| **Affected** | a party who constrains it without being the reader | the organisation (no secrets), a person mentioned in it |

**When holders conflict**, there are three options:
- **Layer:** the primary holder's questions and conditions go in the main message; secondary ones go in Workcraft's Supporting Context (its linked explanations and evidence).
- **Split** the communication.
- **Record** the conflict openly.

**Constraints are never layered.** A legal reviewer may read last, but their constraint holds everywhere.

### 5. How the need is found without guessing

The prior sourcing order holds. Two refinements are added.

**The order:**
1. the request;
2. a standing arrangement;
3. the workflow;
4. **a quotable genre or document-role convention** (new);
5. asking;
6. a visible assumption.

**A convention counts only if it can be quoted:**
- a style guide;
- a document-type template (for example, a decision record's "context, decision, consequences");
- the act's own point.

"Readers usually want…" is not a source. It becomes a visible assumption, written in the document.

**The request is evidence of the state, not the state itself.** People ask for a compressed version of what they need. Librarians handle this by asking "what will you use it for?".

In September, the request said "operational view". The standing arrangement held the real state: problems arrive "with a proposed outcome" so they can be "prioritized together". The finding behind this is devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_12-53__what-determines-the-view-combination-an-exchange-needs/finding.md.

Depart from the literal request only with a quoted source, or as a visible assumption the requester can correct.

**When an AI drafts with no request**, the visible assumption is the minimum discipline. Write down the assumed state where the author can see and correct it.

### 6. How the need is checked

**Quote where the text carries each requirement:**
- for questions: where each is answered;
- for conditions: where each is met;
- for constraints: that nothing violates them.

The reader's resulting state is never judged. It cannot be observed when writing, in any genre.

**The sufficiency probe from the three-gaps finding** generalises from "a fresh reader answers the need's questions" to "a fresh reader quotes where each requirement is carried".

**Diagnostic to test: implied versus sourced state.**
1. From a draft, quote what it carries.
2. List which requirements those quotes answer.
3. State the strongest state that this supports.
4. Compare it with the sourced state.

On the September message:
- the implied state was "the manager knows which documents exist";
- the sourced state was "the manager can decide whether to back the next steps".

The mismatch shows in one line. The quoting steps are what stop this from becoming a fluent but wrong model judgment.

### 7. Where the model stops

- **Aesthetic or experiential communication** (art, humour): the state is an experience, and its conditions are hard to state. This is outside work communication.
- **Exploratory exchanges** ("think it through together"): the state is discovered during the exchange. The model applies turn by turn, and the overall state stays open on purpose.

# [6] Finding: Several parties — holders, placement and constraints

## Question

The prior finding said that when a communication serves several parties:
- each holder (each party it must serve) gets their own state;
- the primary holder's requirements go in the main message;
- other holders' requirements go in Supporting Context, Workcraft's linked explanations and evidence;
- constraints are never moved there.

The user asked to understand this better: whether it holds, what it means, and where it breaks. The answer had to be generic and plain, and it had to avoid over-definition.

## Finding

### 1. Why look closer

Many work messages serve more than one party:
- a manager plus a cc'd team;
- a reviewer now and a maintainer later;
- an announcement read by customers, staff and executives.

The prior finding answered this with one rule: the primary holder in the main message, the others in Supporting Context, constraints never moved. That rule would become part of Workcraft's guidance, so it needed testing first.

### 2. Claim 1: a state per holder

This holds. Two additions come from the cases.

- **Shared states are fine.** A team announcement may give everyone the same state. "A state per holder" doesn't mean a different state for each one.
- **Serving through vs serving alongside.**
  - The September manager had to "brief others", so the answers had to "stand on their own" (from devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_12-53__what-determines-the-view-combination-an-exchange-needs/finding.md).
  - When a holder is reached through another holder, their needs become a condition on the primary holder's text: it must survive being forwarded or quoted.
  - That is a different relation from holders who read the same message side by side.

### 3. Claim 2: why "primary in main, others in Supporting Context" breaks

**Supporting Context was designed for one recipient.**
- The guide (`artifacts/skills/orchestrator-inbound-communication/references/supporting-context.md`) opens: *"Use this guide when composing, critiquing or revising a contribution addressed to the human or AI coordinating the work."*
- Its job is optional depth ("how do you know?", "more detail") for that one reader.
- Reusing it for other holders is a new use, and nobody tested it.

**"Layering" hides three operations:**

| Operation | What it decides | How it fails a secondary holder |
|---|---|---|
| Order | what comes first | They stop reading before their part. |
| Access | what is visible without a click | They never click. |
| Selection | what is in the message at all | Their part is not there. |

**The guide's own rules show the breaks:**
- **I6** counts as a problem *"an important source is inaccessible to the recipient"*. A customer cannot open an internal link, so their part cannot live there.
- **I3** requires that *"each reading level supplies faithful meaning … at the points where a reader may stop"*. With several holders there are several stopping points, and what a holder reads before theirs must not mislead them.

### 4. The replacement: placement by stopping point

Layering exists because readers stop: humans skim, executives read the top, cc'd readers read less. So the rule is placement relative to each holder's stopping point.

- **A way in for every holder.** The top tells each holder what their part is and where it is. Example: "Team: one change for you, §3, action by Friday."
  - Rank decides how much of a holder's material sits up top.
  - A secondary holder may get only this line, but never nothing.
- **Essentials before the stop.** Each holder's essential requirements sit before that holder's likely stopping point.
- **No misleading prefixes.** Whatever any holder reads before stopping must not mislead them. This is the guide's I3, applied across holders. It is the same principle as journalism's rule that every prefix of a story must stand alone, because editors cut from the bottom.

**Where stopping points come from.** They come from sources, not personality:
- the holder's role: cc and "information addressee" mean read less;
- the request: "one paragraph please";
- the medium: a chat preview vs a document;
- the holder type: an AI agent reads everything, but needs labels so it doesn't act on other holders' parts.

When nothing settles it, assume early stopping for every non-primary holder. The cost is one pointer line.

**What makes a holder primary.**
- The setter (whoever sets what the communication is for) decides.
- Stakes are the main evidence.
- The To-line is the default when nothing says otherwise.
- Reading order follows from rank. It is not a source of rank.

**Later readers.** For people who find the document later, "the top" is often its title and first line as they appear in search results. Titles carry the way in for later readers.

**Ordering by what is shared.**
- A useful default:
  1. the shared core: what every holder needs;
  2. each holder's part, labelled;
  3. depth and links.
- **Exception:** when the primary holder's action is the whole point (a decision request only they act on), their part leads.
- **Example:** in a PR description, the shared core is what changed and why. The reviewer's part is risk and testing. The maintainer's part is the alternatives that were rejected.

**An opening block, when there is more than one holder.**
- One line per holder: their part, whether it is for action or for information, and any constraint on them.
- Military and government memos separate "action addressees" from "information addressees" in the same way.
- With a single holder this reduces to stating the bottom line first. It is a pattern, not a form.

### 5. Claim 3: two kinds of constraint

**Constraints on the text: what the communication must not contain or do.**
- Examples: don't reveal X, don't commit the sender, no blame.
- **Scope:** the communication's plausible reach.
  - The message itself.
  - Everything it links to: linking to a document that reveals X reveals X.
  - Plausible forwards, judged by the medium and the sensitivity.
  - Summaries.
- **Statement:** usually no statement to the reader is needed; you simply comply. They must, however, be stated to the drafter, especially an AI drafter, which can only avoid what its brief names.
- **"Never moved" is right about their scope, and the scope is wider than the prior finding said.**

**Constraints on a holder: what a reader must not do.**
- Examples: embargo until Monday, don't share outside the team, don't act before approval.
- These are requirements that the holder must know.
- **Placement:** before the point where that holder could act, usually in the opening block.
- A legal disclaimer in a footer has whole-document scope but a low statement. It works legally, and fails communicatively whenever the reader must act on it.

### 6. When to layer, split or record

| Conflict | Example | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Content | detail vs brevity | Layer by stopping point. |
| Act | one approves, others are informed | Layer, with action vs information marked in the opening block. |
| Visibility | one holder must not see what another must | **Split.** Layering cannot hide content from someone who receives the message. |
| Register | a warm note to a family vs a factual record for HR | **Split.** |
| Unresolvable | | **Record** the conflict openly. |

**Always splitting was rejected.** Separate messages drift apart and multiply work, and the holders lose shared awareness. When you do split, keep a shared core whose facts match. The wording may adapt to the register.

### 7. One principle and three tests

**The principle:** *no holder is stranded or misled, wherever they stop reading and wherever the communication travels.*

**The tests, each grounded in what a reader actually sees:**
1. **Remove the links.** Is every holder still served by what remains?
2. **Read only the top.** Is any holder misled, or unaware of their part?
3. **Forward it cold.** Does it make sense to someone without the original framing?

# [7] Finding: The meaning unit — what a communication conveys

## Question

Is the meaning gap still relevant after the need findings? If so, what is one unit of meaning, and what must travel with it so a communication's meaning can be quoted and checked?

The user named four things that might travel with a unit:
- its reasons and limits;
- its status (settled by the author, taken from a source, left open, proposed by an AI);
- where it came from;
- how it connects to other units.

The answer had to stay generic and not over-define.

## Finding

### 1. Is this still worth doing?

The three-gaps finding named the meaning as Gap 2 because "essence" was defined as "the important meaning for the present use". That definition was circular, and it was allowed to stay unwritten. Meanwhile the content check needs meaning it can quote.

The need finding fixed half of this. "Important" now has a criterion: it serves a requirement (a question, a condition or a constraint) of some holder's state.

The other half remains. Requirements say what must be answered, not what the answer *is* or *where it stands*.

Four losses are invisible without a unit of meaning:
- **an answer false to its source:** you need the unit's origin to compare;
- **an AI decision nobody confirmed:** you need its status;
- **a qualification dropped in a summary:** you need the unit's internal limit;
- **contradiction between split messages:** the several-parties finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_16-28__several-parties-holders-layering-and-constraints/finding.md) requires their facts to match, and comparing facts needs units.

So the gap is still relevant, and smaller.

### 2. The unit: content with force

A unit of meaning has three parts:
- **content:** the point (what it is about and what it says);
- **force:** what is being done with the point;
- **internal limits:** the scope, condition or qualifier that keeps it true.

**Why force is needed.**
- A claims-only unit cannot hold a thank-you, an open question or a recommendation. "Use B" is neither true nor false.
- The need finding showed that expressive needs have *conditions*, such as "a thank-you must name what was done". Something has to meet those conditions, and that is a unit whose force is thanking.
- Wording can change force: "might" vs "will", quoting vs endorsing. Scientific hedges ("may", "suggests") are force too. Summaries routinely drop them, which inflates claims.

**The forces seen so far:**
- asserted;
- recommended;
- asked;
- promised;
- thanked;
- left open;
- disclaimed ("we have not tested Y").

These are examples, not a fixed list.

**Granularity.** The test is whether a reader could accept one part and reject another. If so, they are separate units.

**Units are not sentences.**
- One sentence can carry several units.
- One unit can span several sentences.
- A unit can be meant without being stated. Whether that is acceptable belongs to the conveyance gap.

### 3. Limits inside, reasons linked

The user grouped "reasons and limits" together. They behave differently when removed.

**Limits.** "The migration is done for the EU accounts."
- Remove "for the EU accounts" and the statement becomes false.
- "Friday works, provided Maya approves."
- Remove the condition and it becomes a different commitment.
- So limits are *part of the unit*. Losing one is always a fidelity error.

**Reasons.** "The migration is done, because the load test passed."
- Remove the reason and the claim is still true, just unsupported.
- Reasons are often important claims themselves, with their own standing.
- So reasons are *separate units linked by a relation*.

**How serious a dropped reason is depends on the situation.**
- **In fresh writing**, an omitted reason is a fit question: does any holder need the "why"?
- **When transforming a source** (summarising, replying), dropping a material reason is a preservation loss. The materialization-vs-content finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_13-08__materialization-check-vs-content-check-in-communication-craft/finding.md) already defines faithfulness as *"nothing material from the sources dropped (a reason, a qualification)"*.
- "Material" is judged by whether any holder's requirement asks "why".

"Companions" (from the essence-formation finding) remains a fine umbrella word for both, as long as the difference is kept.

### 4. Connections

The substrate finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_22-09__communication-substrate-foundation-and-dimensions/finding.md) says *"the relationships between these facets carry much of the meaning."*

A small core of connection types carries what changes meaning when dropped:
- **support:** because / therefore;
- **condition:** if / unless, when it links two units;
- **contrast or concession:** but / although;
- **sequence:** then.

Rhetorical Structure Theory, a standard catalogue of how parts of a text relate, lists over 20 relations. Most others, such as elaboration and example, rarely change meaning when dropped. Add them when a real case needs them.

**Connections are lopsided.** One unit is the point; the other supports it. The *main point* of a communication is the unit that answers the primary holder's main requirement. It can be worked out from the need and does not have to be declared.

**Losing a connection while keeping both units is its own problem.** "A. B." instead of "A because B" invites the reader to guess the link, or to guess the wrong one.

### 5. Standing: who settles it, and where it can be quoted from

Every unit has two pieces of standing:
- **Status: who or what settles it.**
  - settled by the author;
  - gathered from a source;
  - produced, i.e. worked out in this work;
  - proposed by an AI;
  - found in a draft;
  - left open;
  - to be asked;
  - attributed (a reader's interpretation);
  - superseded.
- **Origin: where it can be quoted from.** A source document, the author's own text, or an answer the author gave.

**No status may be assigned without a matching quote.**
- **"Settled"** requires an author quote or an author answer.
- **"Gathered"** requires a source quote.
- **Everything else** stays "proposed" or "found in draft".

This keeps the rule that a model never certifies itself. An AI can never mark its own units as settled.

**Status matches practice outside this project.**
- Architecture decision records use proposed, accepted and superseded.
- Nanopublications attach provenance to every assertion.
- The materialization-vs-content quote test is, in effect, a check on standing.

**One concept serves every kind of "meaning" in the project.** Intended, stated, received and source meaning are all units. They differ only in standing: settled or proposed, found in draft, attributed, gathered.

### 6. Held: quotable when needed

The substrate finding allows meaning to stay unwritten. The materialization-vs-content check needs it quotable. Both hold, at different times: **meaning must be quotable, with its standing, when a check needs it.**

**Route 1: write first.** A short brief or a list of units, before drafting.
- Prefer this when stakes are high, or when the author won't be available later.
- A one-paragraph brief is fine. Units are extracted from it when a check needs them.

**Route 2: extract, then confirm.**
- A *separate* agent (not the drafter) extracts units from the draft.
- Each unit is marked "found in draft" or "proposed", unless a source quote makes it "gathered".
- The author confirms the units, which makes them "settled".
- Confirming units is easier and more honest than confirming prose.
- This also produces the list of "what the AI decided" without asking the AI to report on itself.

**For transformations** (summaries, replies, translations), only the units at risk need holding: those serving requirements, plus their limits.

### 7. The removal test

Each part of the meaning is defined by what its loss does:

| Remove… | What happens |
|---|---|
| a unit serving a requirement | the requirement goes unanswered |
| an internal limit | the unit becomes false or a different commitment |
| a disclaimer | the reader assumes more than was claimed |
| a reason | the point is unsupported |
| a connection (both units kept) | the reader may infer the wrong link |
| the standing | the unit can't be verified; the content check can't route |
| the unit's place in the text | it is not conveyed |

This tells you **what changed**, which is the first diagnostic step. The second step, which check handles it, depends on the need and on whether this is a transformation. Section 3 shows the dropped-reason case. That is why this is a test, not a fixed mapping.

### 8. A bridge to explicitness (hypothesis)

The explicitness bank's demanded fillers ask things like:
- which one;
- of what;
- who;
- when;
- by what criterion.

These look like questions about a unit's **referent** (what the content is about) or its **internal limits** (scope, time, criterion). If so, an explicitness gap is usually a unit whose referent or limit the text doesn't carry. That would connect Gap 2 (meaning) and Gap 3 (conveyance) concretely.

**This is untested.** The test is to classify the real gaps in benchmarks 2 and 3 as missing limit, missing referent, or other.

# [8] Finding: Conveyance — what a text may leave unsaid

## Question

What may a text legitimately leave unsaid? The aim was a principle, not another rule.

The explicitness bank (the project's question bank for finding gaps in a text) keeps adding exemption rules. Its rules for "a particular thing isn't needed here" keep spreading, because no principle sits behind them. The user asked for a deep dive into conveyance generally, centred on this principle. The answer must be generic, must judge the text rather than reader knowledge, and must not be over-defined.

## Finding

### 1. Why this was the hard part

The explicitness bank has two tests:
- **demand:** is something particular needed here?
- **sufficiency:** does the text single it out?

Sufficiency is now well defined. Demand is where the bank keeps changing. Every version adds rules for when nothing particular is needed, and the results seesaw:

| Version | Precision | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| v6.6 | 56–62% real | highest |
| v6.7 | 81–83% real | fell, because its new gates exempted about 48 real gaps against the 6–8 predicted |

The planned v6.8 fix was to tighten the wording of each gate. The user's diagnosis: the rules keep spreading because no principle sits behind them.

### 2. The three grounds

**Ground 1: supplied.**
- The text fixes exactly one filler, early enough: at the point, before it, or in the very next sentence.
- It may do so through its own words, its declared setting, or the standard sense of a word.
- Expert knowledge counts only when the setting names the field. For example, a term used in the standard sense of a field the setting names counts as ordinary vocabulary.
- Standard inference counts as supply when it uses only the text, the setting and standard senses. Inference that needs private knowledge does not count.

**Ground 2: any reasonable choice gives the same result.**
- Every reasonable filler leads to the same result for what the text sets the reader to do, know or judge.
- When a need is declared, the result is measured against that need (see devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_16-18__the-need-what-a-communication-must-achieve-and-for-whom/finding.md).
- This is the old idea that information matters only if it could change the decision (the value of information). It is also Grice's maxim "as informative as required", made into a test.

**Ground 3: declared open, or handed to someone.**
- The text says it is open ("to be decided", "found by judging the first 5"), or hands the choice to someone ("each owner chooses").
- Silent discretion doesn't count. Readers often don't know they were given the choice.

**Anything else is a gap.**

| Gap type | Ground that failed |
|---|---|
| CANNOT (not there) | no ground holds |
| LATER | Ground 1 failed on timing |
| MULTIPLE | Ground 1 failed, and the fillers' results differ |
| CONFLICT and UNSPECIFIC | Ground 1 failed |

### 3. The bank's rules all fit

The explicitness bank v6.7.2 is at `artifacts/skills/non-explicitness-detection/references/explicitness-bank.md`.

**Ground 1, supplied:**
- R1 and V6.5 (supplied at, before, or in the next sentence);
- V6.6-A (the quote fills its own slot; the supplier is itself supplied; competitors are listed);
- A2 (sources for "of what");
- A6 (a competitor must fit);
- A7 (announced below);
- A9 (a frame word gives the kind; field terms);
- R3 (the addressee is the only fitting doer);
- A8 (a doer with no fitting candidates).

**Ground 2, indifferent:**
- the substitution test ("any approvals" means the same);
- task values and the act list (slots the task doesn't use);
- judgment words outside a judge task;
- group actors;
- R4 (general terms in lists);
- A1 ("one end state");
- A3 ("the point is about the whole");
- A4 (a criterion only when someone applies it);
- A5 (a second reading must change the point);
- "no relation cue" (separate notices need no link).

**Ground 3, declared open or handed over:**
- R2 (declared openness);
- declared level ("at a high level");
- A4 (declared discretion);
- A8 (authorising the addressee's own plan).

**Overrides and scope rules:**
- **"Demanded when the main point rests on it."** Ground 2 run in reverse: the omission is not indifferent.
- **V6.6-C ("an exemption covers only its own slot").** Indifference must hold slot by slot.

### 4. Why the gates spread

The Ground 2 rules were written as stand-ins for "every reasonable choice gives the same result", not as that test itself. Each stand-in contains a word of extent: "one end state", "the point is about the whole", "only gives a steer", "the point rests on it". A detector can assert the stand-in without checking.

All four recorded failures (from `compare/benchmark_data/explicitness-bank/results-v67.md`) are stand-ins that a check would have refuted:

| Case | Stand-in claimed | Reasonable fillers → results | Under the test |
|---|---|---|---|
| "complete the Airtable records" | one end state | fill empty fields → partly done; verify every field → checked; set status to done → only the status changes | gap |
| "switch off the writing workflows" | point is the set's status | all of them / the three named earlier → different systems stop | gap |
| "your handoff" | not rested on | the 2 Oct note / the handoff section → different content to read | gap |
| "who runs the experiment" | "only gives a steer" | you / Bob → a different person does the work | gap |

**The proposed v6.8 fixes would have tightened each stand-in's wording**, for example "A3 needs the predicate to name the property". That adds new surface conditions with new words of extent. That is the spreading the user named.

**The evidence that the principle is right.** Only one gate was already written as a demonstration of difference. V6.7-A5 reads: *"A gap needs two readings, each cued by something other than the separator or the number itself, that lead to different conclusions, counts, owners or actions."* It was not among the gates that leaked. A1, A3 and A8 were.

### 5. The difference test

**For each slot the wording demands, check in order:**

1. **Supplied?** Quote what fixes exactly one filler, in time. If so, it is fine to leave unsaid.
2. **Indifferent?** List the reasonable fillers and group them by their result. One group means fine; record a representative filler and the shared result.
3. **Declared?** Quote the words that leave it open or hand it over. If so, it is fine.
4. **Otherwise, it is a gap.** Record two fillers from different groups and their two results.

**What makes it hold, where the stand-ins did not:**
- **Bounded fillers.** Fillers come only from:
  - what the text puts in play;
  - the standard senses of the word;
  - the parties the text names.

  Imagined fillers don't count. One representative per group is enough; this is the same idea as testing one input per equivalence class in software testing.
- **Concrete results.** Each group's result must be an observable difference: who does what, what exists, what someone knows. "Both leave the records complete" names no difference, so it cannot support "the same".
- **Cues, not exemptions.** The old gate wordings ("about the whole", "one end state") stay only as cues for *where to run step 2*. They never exempt on their own.
- **An undecided list.** Cases that can't be decided go to a separate list, not into the gap count. If that list grows beyond about 10% of decisions, the result-phrasing rule needs tightening.

### 6. How fine "the same result" must be

The grain depends on:
- **stakes:** the cost of the wrong filler to whoever relies on the text;
- **repairability:** whether the reader can ask back.

A one-day difference is "the same" for a team newsletter and not for a contract deadline. An agent prompt is low in repairability: AI readers resolve ambiguity silently and confidently, so an undetected gap becomes a silent wrong action.

A detector reads both from the declared setting ("a contract", "a prompt to an agent", "a live chat"). With no declaration, it uses a default grain.

### 7. The principle beyond detection

| Use | How the principle applies |
|---|---|
| **Writing** | Before omitting, ask whether any reasonable choice would give the same result. If not, say it. |
| **Fixing** | The author question becomes concrete: "Did you mean A, which leads to X, or B, which leads to Y?" That is more answerable than "which records?". |
| **Economy** | Content whose every reasonable version gives the same result *may* be cut. The same principle decides both what must be said and what need not be. It is "may", because deliberate redundancy (confirming a time zone in a high-stakes setting) is not indifferent at fine grain. |
| **Meaning units** | Every part of a unit gets the test: its referent, its limits, its force (is it a request?), its relations. This fits the meaning finding's hypothesis that explicitness gaps are mostly missing limits or referents (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-09_16-16__the-meaning-unit-what-a-communication-conveys/finding.md). |

### 8. What conveyance covers, and what is left

The principle covers every form of *unsaid*:
- absent;
- late;
- ambiguous;
- implied by standard inference;
- a missing connection;
- a missing force;
- a missing limit.

It does not cover **over-conveyance**: the text saying something unintended, such as an implication the author didn't mean. That is a separate conveyance question, still open.

# [9] Finding: Essence formation — the moves, revisited

## Question

The user summarised what we understood about forming an essence:
- answer the questions the recipient's next act raises, one at a time, with the 14 facets as a final sweep;
- each answer comes from one of seven moves: settled by the author, gathered from sources, selected for this exchange, produced, grounded, left open, or asked of the author.

They asked for a deep dive, generic and not over-defined, now that the need, the meaning unit and conveyance have been defined.

## Finding

### 1. Why revisit this

The earlier formation finding listed seven moves and found that real failures were moves skipped silently. The user later judged that finding too tied to one manager's briefings.

Since then, three foundations have been defined:
- **the need:** a state for each party, which produces requirements;
- **the meaning unit:** a point, plus what is done with it, plus its limits, with a standing that needs quotes;
- **conveyance:** what a text may leave unsaid.

This inquiry rebuilds formation on those foundations.

### 2. The seven moves, re-sorted

**Where an answer comes from:**

| Source | Starting status | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Settled by the author | settled | a quote from the author, or an answer the author gave |
| Gathered from a source | gathered | a quote from the source |
| Produced (conclude, choose or design) | proposed, untested | the author settles it; it is tested |

**What happens to a requirement that can't be answered yet:**
- **Asked:** turned into a concrete question for the author. The reply returns as settled.
- **Left open:** the author decides it stays open for this exchange. That is legitimate, and the conveyance finding counts "declared open" as one of its three grounds.

**What is done to every answer:**
- **Select:** trace it to a requirement, and decide its level and depth.
- **Ground:** attach its standing (who or what settled it, and where it can be quoted from), using the quote rule.
- **Companions travel with it:** limits stay inside the answer; the reasons a holder needs are linked to it.

**Why this matters.** In a flat list of seven, "grounded" sits beside "gathered" as though you might do one or the other. Failures like example 2 and the coworker-endorsement summary happened because grounding was skipped. Making select and ground mandatory for every answer is the point of the re-sort.

These source labels match the meaning finding's status values (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-09_16-16__the-meaning-unit-what-a-communication-conveys/finding.md). Backward formation adds one more: "found in draft".

### 3. Producing: concluding, choosing, designing

Three kinds of work create meaning that didn't exist before:
- **Concluding:** drawing what follows from gathered material. Example 7 described five documents and drew no conclusion.
- **Choosing:** recommending or deciding between options. It needs the options and the criteria stated.
- **Designing:** creating something new, such as an operation, a plan or a structure. Example 1's continuing operation was never designed.

Producing is, in Workcraft terms, Semantic Formulation's work, not Communication's. Formation may include it, but it must be **visible**:
- every produced answer starts as "proposed, untested";
- the author settles it;
- a test challenges it.

The old failures were never "the AI produced something". They were producing silently, or not producing at all when a requirement needed it.

### 4. The two moves at the ends

**First: state the need.**
- Following the need finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_16-18__the-need-what-a-communication-must-achieve-and-for-whom/finding.md), name who must reach which state, with the source for it.
- Derive the requirements from that.
- The request is evidence of the state, not the state itself.
- With no source, state a visible assumption.

**Last: close.**
- **Facet sweep.** Do the 14 facets suggest a requirement nobody stated? Examples:
  - a relationship to maintain;
  - history to acknowledge;
  - a later reader.

  New requirements go back to the need. The sweep runs once per pass.
- **Sufficiency probe.** A fresh reader quotes where the text carries each requirement.
- **Test produced answers.** A fresh agent challenges the plan or recommendation and returns objections, each tied to the assumptions it quotes. The author settles. The test never declares "passed".

### 5. Formation as closing open conditions

Every earlier failure leaves a visible trace:

| Open condition | Example failure |
|---|---|
| a requirement with no source | September: the need was never stated |
| a requirement with no answer | examples 1 and 7: nothing was produced where producing was needed |
| an answer with no standing, or with a lost limit | example 2 and the coworker-endorsement summary |
| an answer not traced to a requirement | the AI chose the level silently |
| a produced answer unsettled or untested | examples 5 and 6: the plan was never tested |

**Formation is done** when none of these is open, or each remaining one has been declared open by the author.

That turns formation from a procedure into a short, checkable list. It scales down: a one-line reply has one requirement, one settled answer, and trivial checks.

**Worked check on a condolence note:**
- **Frame:** the colleague knows the loss is recognised and that support is offered. The source is the occasion.
- **Fill:** the expressed sorrow is the author's own, so it is settled. The offer of support is produced, so it is proposed until the author confirms it.
- **Close:** the facet sweep raises the relationship (how close are they?). That adds a requirement, "warmth that matches the relationship".

### 6. Who does what

| Move | The AI alone | The author |
|---|---|---|
| State the need | proposes it from sources, or states a visible assumption | confirms it when it was inferred |
| Gather | yes, with quotes | — |
| Select | proposes, traced to a requirement | confirms the level and the main point |
| Ground | yes, by the quote rule | — |
| Produce | proposes (proposed, untested) | settles |
| Ask | drafts the question as "A → X or B → Y" | answers |
| Leave open | proposes | decides |
| Sweep, probe, test | yes, using fresh agents | acts on the results |

**Ask only when it matters.**
- If the candidate answers lead to the same result, there is nothing to ask. The AI picks one, marks it proposed, and records the candidates and the shared result.
- Those picks go in a short, skimmable section of the author's sheet, and the author can overturn any of them.

**One settle sheet.**
- It holds the questions, the produced answers and the AI's picks.
- Production questions come first, because they need the most author time.
- The author is interrupted once.

**When the author is absent,** an unconfirmed answer the message relies on is marked as such in the text: "recommendation (not yet confirmed)". Its standing is conveyed honestly instead of hidden.

### 7. Routes and interleaving

Writing research shows that drafting reveals what you think, so formation and drafting interleave. There are two routes in:
- **Forward:** form first, then draft. Better for high stakes, because a missing conclusion or design is caught before anyone writes around it.
- **Backward:** draft first, extract the answers (each starting as "found in draft"), then confirm. Cheaper for low stakes.

Both end in the same list of open conditions.

### 8. What "done" does not mean

Closing every condition means the essence is **formed**:
- every requirement has a traced, grounded answer, or has been declared open.

It does not mean the essence is **correct**:
- a gathered answer can quote a wrong source;
- a settled answer can be a poor decision.

Checking the truth of sources belongs to the content check (Workcraft's Context Accumulation). Judging whether a decision is sound belongs to the produced-answer test and the author.

Following the materialization-vs-content finding's declaration rule, every formation report says what it did not check.

# [10] Finding: the substrate concept that arranges the message

## Question

You asked: if the draft copied the substrate's order, the substrate is missing something. "Maybe substrate should have a concept to, to arrange this thing. Let's dive deep into this."

**Goal:** a conceptual account of the missing concept:
- whether it is really missing;
- what it is;
- how it relates to the existing parts of Communication Craft (the need, meaning units, relations, several holders);
- how it is formed and checked;
- where it lives.

## Finding

### 1. Why this question

The previous inquiry (18-38) found that a draft built from a complete substrate opened weirdly because it copied the substrate's own order:
- the essence was written as a story;
- the facts were a flat list;
- the paragraph plan was ordered by kind of content.

You drew the obvious conclusion: if the substrate's form causes the order, the substrate is missing a concept. This inquiry asks what that concept is.

The **substrate** here means the structured account of what a message must convey, built before the text. Its parts so far are the need (who must reach what state), the essence, the facts with their sources and standing, the choices, and the ask.

### 2. Is something really missing?

The strongest counter-view is that nothing is missing. The concepts exist in earlier findings, and the author simply did not apply them. That view fails for two reasons.

**The concepts had no place to live.** The substrate's sections have:
- no field for rank;
- no field for a fact's relation;
- no declared main point.

The form decides what gets filled in, and the Signals author filled in exactly the form.

**One concept was actually lost.** The 12-53 view-combination finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_12-53__what-determines-the-view-combination-an-exchange-needs/finding.md) said the need fixes "which questions are answered, and in what order. The primary act's questions lead."

The later need finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_16-18__the-need-what-a-communication-must-achieve-and-for-whom/finding.md) widened questions into requirements: questions, conditions and constraints. It kept the questions, but nothing carried their rank. The Signals substrate's "reader's need" listed three things with no rank among them.

So the answer is: **missing from the substrate, but not new to the framework.** What is missing is the *shape* that brings the existing pieces together.

### 3. What the concept is: the point structure

The substrate is written as an outline, main point at the top.

**1. Ranked need.** Each requirement is marked main, next, or depth.
- Rank comes from the same sources as the need, in order:
  - the request;
  - a standing arrangement;
  - the workflow;
  - the genre's convention;
  - asking the author.
- Where nothing settles the rank, the order makes no difference to the result. In the conveyance finding's terms it is indifferent, and any reasonable order is fine.

**2. Main point.** One statement: the message's act ("I'm proposing…", "I need a decision on…", "For information…"), the answer, and the ask or next step.
- It must answer the top-ranked requirement.
- It carries its standing:
  - **settled:** quoted from the author;
  - **gathered:** quoted from a source;
  - **proposed:** produced by the AI or found in a draft.
- The main point is usually the most consequential answer and often the least settled. So a proposed main point is the **first item on the author's settle sheet** (the single list of questions and proposed answers the author confirms, from the formation finding).

**3. Key points.** Each one names the requirement it answers. Usually these are the next-ranked requirements: the questions the main point raises in the reader.
- For a request they are typically: why? what does it take? what does it cost? what do you need from me?
- If the main point raises a question the need did not list, that question is added to the need.

**4. Facts.** Each fact sits under a point, with two things:
- **its relation** to that point, which is its role:
  - support;
  - limit;
  - condition;
  - contrast;
  - background;
  - sequence.
- **its standing.**

This uses the relation core from the meaning-unit finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-09_16-16__the-meaning-unit-what-a-communication-conveys/finding.md), plus "background".

**5. No orphans.** A fact that cannot name its point is one of two things:
- **background** that belongs deeper;
- **a point the author hasn't stated yet,** which goes back to formation.

Research-diary material shows up here, before it can reach the text.

**6. Order note.** Present only when the order departs from top-down, and always with its source.

**With several holders,** branches carry holder labels. Each holder's essentials sit above their likely stopping point. This is the several-parties finding's placement rule, now with a structure to apply it to.

**With documentation,** the structure has several main points (one per top question). The order follows how readers look things up.

### 4. The Signals substrate, rebuilt

Taking the actual Signals content and placing it, with nothing added:

- **Ranked need:**
  1. "what operational response you recommend" (main);
  2. "what the work has established" (why);
  3. "the size of the initial commitment" (cost).
- **Main point (proposed; settle first):** I'm proposing that AI Operations runs a regular Signals review, which I'd initially coordinate. I'm asking you to approve a bounded start.
  - **Why?** Similar-looking reports need very different responses.
    - *Support:* a trial of three reports gave three conclusions: an instruction correction, a known tool limitation, and a recovery question.
  - **What does it take?** The report, the change and the result need separate evidence, so the review must follow work through to its result.
    - *Support:* trial finding.
  - **What does it cost?** The real effort is not known yet.
    - *Limit:* the trial timings are "not a weekly effort estimate".
    - *Background:* one preserved week holds 1,633 reports.
  - **What do you need from me?** A starting time allowance and a review date.

Read top-down, this gives the opening and the four leads that the previous critique judged good. **The 1,633 figure can only land as background under cost.** It cannot open the message, because it is not the main point and does not answer the top requirement.

### 5. Order follows from the structure

The default is top-down: the main point first, each point before its support, and siblings in rank order or sequence order. This follows from the several-parties rule that readers stop early, so their essentials must come first.

Some messages are not top-down. In each case the *structure* is the same; only the *linearization* (the order in which the structure is told) changes:

| Message | How the structure handles it |
|---|---|
| Incident report | The summary leads, and the timeline is a sequence relation among key points |
| Instructions | The goal leads, and the steps are in sequence |
| Bad news with reasons first | A sourced exception (a relational condition), recorded in the order note |
| Scientific paper | The genre's order, recorded as its source |

So the earlier rule "order follows from rank" (16-28) is refined to: **order follows from rank and relations, with sourced exceptions.**

### 6. Where the line between meaning and expression falls

| Part | Layer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rank, main point, relations | **meaning (substrate)** | Changing them changes what the reader takes away (the conveyance finding's difference test) |
| Order | **derived from meaning** | Read off the structure; only exceptions are recorded |
| Wording, transitions, paragraphing | **materialization** | Can change without changing the takeaway |

This resolves an earlier tension:
- The materialization finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_19-33__communication-substrate-materialization-and-review-loop/finding.md) put "selecting and arranging" in materialization.
- The substrate-concepts finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_15-59__communication-substrate-concepts-and-boundaries/finding.md) said ordering that expresses a relation is meaning.

Both are right once "arranging" is split into structure (meaning) and wording (expression).

**The top of the structure is already a short message.** The main point plus the key points can be sent as is. So the substrate is not purely "thinking" separated from "presenting". The ordering is meaning that also reads as a message, which is why copying it is now safe. The substrate's other parts (sources, standing, the need) stay as substrate only.

### 7. How it is formed and checked

**Forming.** The structure is built by steps the formation finding already has:
- **state the need,** now with rank;
- **select:** trace each answer to a requirement, which now also places it under a point;
- **ground:** attach standing.

The new work is:
- one main-point statement;
- one requirement label per key point;
- one relation tag per fact.

**Checking the substrate, before any text exists.** All three checks compare quoted items; none asks "is it good?".
1. **Main point vs top requirement.** Quote both. Does the main point answer it?
2. **No orphans.** Every fact names its point.
3. **Key points traced.** Every key point names a requirement.

In Signals, check 1 would have failed at once. The essence's first sentence, "We have collected a substantial body of feedback…", answers no requirement.

**Checking the text afterwards.** The fresh-reader check from 18-38 stays. The reader states the point after sentence 1 and after the leads, and that is compared with the main point and the key points. The structure is now what the check compares against.

### 8. What this changes in earlier findings

- **16-16 (meaning unit).** "The main point … does not have to be declared" is corrected: the main point is declared, with standing. Signals shows that undeclared means undecided.
- **16-18 (need).** Requirements carry rank. This restores 12-53's "in what order".
- **19-33 (materialization).** "Arranging" splits into structure (meaning, in the substrate) and wording (materialization).
- **18-38 (weird opening).** The separate arrangement plan is absorbed. The leads you review are the key points.
- **16-28 (several parties).** "Order follows from rank" becomes "order follows from rank and relations, with sourced exceptions".

### 3. What harmony is

**Harmony is the property that every part of a communication fits one frame, and fits the parts it depends on.**

The misfits fall into two groups:
- **Items 1–5, 7 and 8:** some element does not fit *who is speaking, to whom, doing what, for what*.
- **Items 6 and 9–13:** elements do not fit *each other* inside that frame.

So harmony has a **centre** and **neighbourhoods**.

**The centre is the frame.** It is one steady, sourced statement:

| Line | Signals example | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker and role | the user, operations manager in AI Operations | §7 "yours as an operations manager" |
| Recipient(s) and role | the boss, who decides support and priority | the dialogue; §1 |
| Relationship | reports to a manager who has asked for distilled judgment | the manager's own words (the manager's thread) |
| Act | propose a recurring review; ask approval for a bounded start | §6 |
| Need | the boss can assess the recommendation without reconstructing the research | §1, using the need finding's definition |
| Medium and moment | written message, under 500 words | §7 |
| Drafter | an AI, for the author's review | the dialogue |
| Names | one name for each key thing ("Signals" = …) | settled by the author |

**This is not new material.** These lines are drawn from four places:
- facets 1, 2, 4, 5, 9 and 12 of the substrate foundation (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_22-09__communication-substrate-foundation-and-dimensions/finding.md);
- the need finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_16-18__the-need-what-a-communication-must-achieve-and-for-whom/finding.md);
- the several-parties finding's opening block (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_16-28__several-parties-holders-layering-and-constraints/finding.md);
- a defined-terms list, as in legal drafting.

What is new is that they are **selected and stated once**, as the reference everything else must fit.

**Signals had the pieces but never one frame:**
- §1 was addressed to the *author* ("Your boss needs…");
- the act sat in §6;
- the voice sat in §7;
- the drafter was nowhere;
- the substrate was written inside the AI-to-author conversation.

That is how the AI-to-author frame leaked into the message (items 1 and 3).

**The established names.** Classical rhetoric calls this fit **decorum**: words and subject "aptly fit to each other, to the circumstances and occasion, the audience, and the speaker". Cicero treated it as "a constant process of attuning and adjusting", not a fixed rule. Bitzer calls a good message "a fitting response" to its situation.

"Harmony" is kept for three reasons:
- it is the user's word;
- it covers all seven kinds of fit found below;
- its musical sense, notes fitting a *key*, matches the structure of a frame and the elements in it.

### 4. What harmony is between: four levels

| Level | What must fit | Signals items | Where the fix goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The frame itself** | one speaker, one addressee, one act, one need; the drafter kept apart | 1, 2, 3 | substrate (author) |
| **The substrate** | need, main point, key points, facts, register, ask and standing fit the frame and each other | 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 13 | substrate (usually asked of the author) |
| **The text against the substrate** | every paragraph carries a key point, in the frame's voice, with the substrate's standing | 5, 7, 12 | rewrite |
| **The text against itself** | one name per thing, one topic in one place, one voice, no repeated point | 9, 10, 11 | rewrite |

**Kinds of misfit.** All seven senses of "harmony" occur in Signals:
- **decorum:** voice and register fit the relationship;
- **coherence:** meaning hangs together;
- **cohesion and consistency:** one name per thing;
- **alignment:** each part serves the point;
- **proportion:** weight follows importance;
- **frame leak:** two communications mixed;
- **standing:** decided things appear as decided only if they were decided.

That is why one word felt right to the user.

### 5. How harmony is formed: derivation from the frame

Each other element is formed *from* the frame, not separately:

| Element | Derived from | Prior support |
|---|---|---|
| register | relationship line + the need's conditions | 16-18: register is "partly need-driven, partly relationship-driven" |
| what is selected | the need | 16-18 |
| main point and order | the act + the need's ranked requirements | 19-31 point structure; Hasan (below) |
| voice | the speaker line | (new) |
| what may be stated as decided | who has decided (standing) | 16-16 meaning-unit finding |
| placement for several readers | each holder's stopping point | 16-28 |

**This is how linguistics describes it.** Halliday's field, tenor and mode (what is happening, who is involved, through what channel) "determine the range within which meanings are selected and the forms which are used for their expression". Hasan's *contextual configuration* determines "the occurrence and arrangement" of a text's structural elements.

**So order, the subject of 18-38 and 19-31, is downstream of the frame.** That is why the point structure is one line of harmony and not the whole of it.

### 6. How harmony is checked without vouching

Alexander's design theory gives the method. Good fit is hard to define positively, so design proceeds by finding and removing **misfits**. Every felt wrongness traces to a specific misfit.

In both real cases, every part of "weird" and "difficult to engage with" traced to a quotable pair. So harmony is checked by **three comparisons of written things**:

1. **Traces (positive fit).**
   - Every element of the substrate and every paragraph names the frame line or key point it comes from, with its standing.
   - An element with no trace is either an orphan or an unbacked decision.
   - This extends the formation finding's "select" (trace each answer to a requirement) and "ground" (attach its standing) to every element.
   - **Catches:** item 6 (plan paragraph 3 answers no requirement) and item 12 (decisions with no source). Misfit pairs alone would miss them, because an orphan has no partner to clash with.
2. **Misfits (contradiction).**
   - Each misfit is recorded as "expected X from line L, found Y at Q", with its level, kind and fix route.
   - **Pass order:**
     - first the **seams**, where elements from different origins meet: the author's words, gathered sources, AI-produced content, an earlier proposal. In Signals, items 4, 6, 12 and 13 sat on seams;
     - then the pairs that depend on each other (need → main point → key points → facts);
     - then continuity in the text (names, one topic, one voice).
   - A disharmony that shows only in aggregate is recorded as a **pattern misfit**: one frame quote plus two or more text quotes showing the same drift. Item 8, register, is one.
3. **The implied-frame diagnostic (what the reader receives).**
   - A fresh reader, given only the text, writes down the speaker, the addressee, the act, the main point and what is wanted.
   - This is compared line by line with the stated frame.
   - It extends the need finding's "implied state versus sourced state" comparison to the whole frame.
   - On Signals, it would show an ambiguous addressee, a "we/I" speaker and a "report" act within four lines.

"No misfit found" is always reported together with what was compared. It is never reported as "harmonious".

**Fix routing follows the materialization-vs-content finding** (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_13-08__materialization-check-vs-content-check-in-communication-craft/finding.md):
- frame and substrate misfits change the meaning, and usually go to the author;
- text misfits against a settled substrate are fixed by rewriting.

**Where judgment remains.** Two kinds of misfit still need a judgment of degree:
- **how much detail** counts as "reconstructing the research" (item 5);
- **how far register may drift** (item 8).

The pair is quotable, but the threshold is not. These are flagged as judgment-bearing in the record.

### 7. How paragraphs are held in harmony

Paragraphs are held in harmony at the last two levels:
- **Against the substrate:** each paragraph traces to one key point, in the speaker's voice, stating as decided only what the substrate has decided.
- **Against each other:**
  - one name per thing;
  - one topic in one place;
  - one voice;
  - the main point stated once.

Most of these reuse existing text checks from the peer taxonomy (compare/devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-05_18-49__peer-categories-of-explicitness-in-communication-problems/finding.md):
- consistency;
- register;
- authority;
- economy.

The new part is that each check now compares against a *stated* frame and substrate, rather than against an assumed one.

### 8. AI drafting: two channels and a voice rule

An AI drafter is in two communications at once:
- **AI → author:** the working conversation;
- **author → recipient:** the message.

Two rules keep them apart:
- **Two output channels.** The message, plus a separate edit list for the author (choices to confirm, placeholders, notes). A placeholder in the message marks a location only, such as "[allowance]". It never addresses anyone.
- **A voice rule.** Every sentence must be sayable by the frame's speaker to the frame's recipient. Every commitment in it must exist in the substrate with standing.

Together these make items 1, 2, 3 and 12 impossible by construction.

The substrate itself must be **written in the message's frame**: "I, as operations manager, am writing to my manager to propose…". It must not be written inside the drafting conversation ("Your boss needs…").

**The drafting conversation is never a source for the message's frame.** This rule came from testing the opposite idea, an inferred frame. That idea was killed because an inferred frame is exactly what produced item 1.

### 9. The real-reception test: the manager's thread

Signals has no recipient reaction, only the author's. The September model-evaluation message has a real one: the manager's verbatim reply (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-05_23-03__highest-value-next-communication-check/development-packet/sources/duncan-thread.md). Every complaint maps onto the frame:

| The manager's words | Harmony reading |
|---|---|
| "Your slack message describes what each document covers which tells the reader very little" | act against need: the message *describes documents*; the need was to say what is understood, concluded and achieved |
| "AI-produced material that you likely have not read through start to finish yourself" | drafter against speaker: the drafted material speaks; the sender does not |
| "This should show your judgement" | the speaker line: the sender must be present as the one who concluded |
| "any other stakeholder who needs briefed on this project, which remember is partly this message's purpose" | a second holder reached *through* the manager: the several-parties "survive being forwarded" condition |
| Their five-part structure (where we are → learned → put in place → next → need from you) | order derived from the relationship and the act |
| "difficult for a person to engage with" | the sum of the above |

The drafter-vs-speaker line was the newest part of this account, and **here it appears in a real recipient's own words.** This is the strongest external support the account has.

### 10. Where this sits in the framework

- **The point structure (19-31)** is harmony along one line: need → main point → order. It is kept, and narrowed from "the missing concept" to "one part".
- **The 15 peers** each check *the text against one thing*. Harmony is not a sixteenth peer. It is the relation the peers sample pairwise. The new parts (the frame, the substrate-level misfits, traces, the implied frame) sit *before* the text, where no peer looks.
- **The three gaps** (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_15-59__three-fundamental-meaning-gaps-of-communication-craft/finding.md: need, meaning, conveyance) each compare two things.
  - They assume a frame and never form it: the speaker, the drafter and the relationship are in none of them.
  - Signals items 1–3 and the manager's "AI-produced material" are failures none of the three explains as stated.
  - That meets the three-gaps finding's own reopen condition, partially. The result is not a fourth gap. It is a refinement: "adequacy", which that finding defined as the verdict over the three gaps, **becomes harmony**: no misfit among frame, need, meaning and text.
- **The meaning-unit finding's open question** (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-09_16-16__the-meaning-unit-what-a-communication-conveys/finding.md) listed "holistic residue (overall tone, impression)". That residue is the sum of the fits, and it is now checkable through misfits.
- **The substrate foundation** said "the relationships between these facets carry much of the meaning" and gave an example chain ("speaks in a role … invite a decision from someone else"). It never asked those relations to *agree*. Harmony supplies that missing half.

### 11. When the frame is needed

The frame is **not** a mandatory form. The substrate foundation rejected "a mandatory full template".
- **Required:** when the drafter is not the sender, when there are several readers, or when the stakes are high.
- **Otherwise:** optional.
- **In an ongoing thread** where every line is already established, the frame is a reference ("same as the thread"). This is the conveyance finding's "supplied" ground.
- **A change of act or recipient mid-thread** is a new frame, and it must be stated.

### 3. The definition

**A communication has the property when every part of it is right as it stands in one communication.**

"One communication" means one presented voice, to these recipients, for one purpose (which may involve several coordinated acts), in one situation.
- **The simple test:** is every part addressed to a recipient of this communication by its speaker?
- **Why the AI's note fails it:** it was addressed to you, the author, who is not a recipient of the message.
- **Several authors:** when a document has several real authors, one voice is presented and the other voices are attributed.

"Right" has three regards. Each has its own reference and its own kind of check. 13-08 (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_13-08__materialization-check-vs-content-check-in-communication-craft/finding.md) defines the two kinds:
- **content:** is the meaning right? It needs sources or the author.
- **materialization:** is the meaning well expressed? It is checked from the text against what was intended.

| Regard | What it means | Checked against | Kind of check | Real failures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Owned** | The part is presented as whose it really is (the speaker's own, or attributed), how firmly it is really held, and on what basis | the record of who decided or settled what. The meaning-unit finding (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-09_16-16__the-meaning-unit-what-a-communication-conveys/finding.md) calls this a unit's "standing". Without such a record, ask the sender | content (faithful and authorised) | Signals item 12; the manager's "AI-produced material that you likely have not read" |
| **Apt** | The part is right for these recipients, this act, this need and this view: its role (point, support, background), place, weight, register and address | the stated situation: recipients, act, need, view, relationship, medium | materialization, given the situation | Signals items 7, 5, 8 and 3; the manager's message parts 1–4; their "stakeholder who needs briefed"; their five-part structure |
| **Agreeing** | The part is consistent with the other parts (one name per thing, no contradiction, each point in one place) and with what the communication builds on | the rest of the text, earlier messages, other versions | materialization | Signals item 9 |

**Owned is not the same as true.**
- A claim can be honestly presented and still wrong. The 19-31 finding's "the missing concept is the point structure" was its real conclusion, later found too narrow. That is a soundness problem, outside this concept.
- A claim can be right and still presented on a basis it doesn't have. The prior finding's "all 13 caught" claimed a literal check that was actually circular. That is an owned failure.

**Where a part came from explains failures; it does not define them.**
- Parts brought in from elsewhere and never made the speaker's own cause most ownership and agreement failures. Examples: the AI's added rules; the trial document's wording; names copied from different sources.
- The speaker's own content can still be in the wrong place. Examples: Signals item 7; the manager's conclusion buried in their third paragraph.
- So "look at the joins between material of different origin" says **where to look**. It never decides whether a part is right.

**When one failure seems to belong to two regards, ask what would have to change to fix it:**
- the speaker must decide, hold or attribute something → owned;
- the part must be moved, re-weighted, re-voiced or re-addressed → apt;
- another part must change to match → agreeing.

Classifying by reference (standing record, situation, other parts) gives the same placement for every real case.

A part can fail twice. The manager's document descriptions were apt failures (descriptions where conclusions were needed) and ownership failures (material sent as their account but likely unread). Those are two failures. One failure is never counted twice.

**What lies outside the concept:**
- missing parts (the need's unanswered requirements);
- unclear wording (explicitness, vagueness and the other intelligibility checks);
- untrue content (support);
- unstated reasons (connection).

**Alberti's test of a whole divides the same way.** He said a beautiful whole is one where "nothing may be added, taken away, or altered, but for the worse":
- "taken away" and "altered" belong to this concept;
- "added" belongs to the need.

### 4. How it is checked

1. **Whole-level, written.**
   - A fresh reader writes four lines: who it is from, to whom, what it is for, and what it wants.
   - They quote any part that did not seem to belong.
   - This is compared with the stated situation.
   - Applied to the manager's original message, this reproduces their real reply ("Tell us what you now understand, what you've concluded…").

2. **Per part, written.** Three questions. Each answer names its evidence, so a bare "yes" is invalid.
   - **"Is it theirs, as presented?"** Who decided or holds this, how firmly, and on what basis? Compare with the standing record, or ask the sender.
   - **"Is it apt here?"** Which slot is this, which recipient and requirement does it serve, in what register? Quote the requirement or situation line.
   - **"Does it agree?"** What else is this called or claimed as?

3. **Which parts get checked.** The places where the real failures occurred:
   - the slots the act creates: the opening, each paragraph's lead, the ask, each reader's part. These come from the 19-31 point structure (main point, key points, ask) and from genre conventions where they can be quoted;
   - every decision or judgment presented as the speaker's;
   - every join between material of different origin;
   - every name of a key thing.

4. **When the situation doesn't say,** for example a voice preference: follow the need finding's sourcing order. That is the request, then standing arrangements, then quotable convention, then asking, then a visible assumption. Convention is a default, never a verdict against the sender's own voice.

### 7. The names

**What a good name had to do:**
- name the whole condition;
- be two or three words;
- say which regards it covers;
- work across all views;
- avoid this project's existing terms ("fit", "consistency", "key", "alignment");
- survive a search for outside collisions;
- be explainable in one plain sentence.

The decisive criterion was your own: could a colleague roughly guess its meaning from the name?

| Variant | Name | What it foregrounds | Covers | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Three-way congruence** | the whole, as one noun | all three: congruent with its source, its situation, and the rest | needs its one-line gloss; sounds slightly technical |
| 2 | **Owned, apt, agreeing** (or **Authentic, apt, agreeing**) | the definition itself; it is also the check | all three, explicitly | a list, not a noun. "Owned" is used for "has a responsible owner" in operational talk, and for "owned workers" in this project's orchestration skills. "Authentic" avoids that but can sound like a moral judgment |
| 3 | **One-voice integrity** | the single source: the risk AI drafting raises | owned and agreeing; apt only weakly | hides the most frequent failure, apt (10 of 14 real failures here); "voice" also means tone |

**Why "congruence".**
- It means agreement and compatibility. It is the most exact near-synonym of "harmony", without the aesthetic overtone.
- It already has a three-way form in communication psychology. Virginia Satir's congruent communication honours **self, other and context**, and her incongruent stances each ignore at least one of these. Carl Rogers' congruence is outward communication matching inner experience, which is the "owned" regard.
- "Three-way" counters the narrower one-way meaning that "communicative congruence" already carries in psychology (words matching the speaker's sense of self).

**How this concept differs from Satir's.**
- **Source, not self.** "Source" covers attributed material, not only the self.
- **Situation, not other plus context.** Her "other" and "context" merge into "situation".
- **Internal agreement is its own regard.** In her model it appears as words matching feelings. Here it is consistency among parts.

**Recommended:** "three-way congruence: owned, apt, agreeing". Keep "harmony" as the everyday word for what a reader feels when it holds.

**Rejected names:**
- **"Communicative congruence":** psychology already uses it for one-way authenticity.
- **"Situated coherence":** it drops ownership, which the coworker-endorsement case and the prior finding's own claim need. It is also a narrower technical term in formal semantics.
- **"Of a piece":** an idiom from cloth meaning "consistent". Opaque, and covers only agreement.
- **"Message unity":** vague.
- **"Decorum":** now reads as etiquette. Its Latin root survives in "apt" (*aptum*, the fitting).

  - owned gathers authority and support (for strength and basis);

  - agreeing gathers consistency.

  What those checks lacked was one shared reference: the stated communication.
- **The frame from the prior finding stays as the statement of "one communication", with three changes:**

### 3. What harmony is

**The definition.** The different parts of a communication belong together as one whole. No part pulls away from the whole, and no part pulls against another part.

**The word's own history.**
- The Greek *harmonia* meant a "means of joining", used of ship-planks.
- *Harmos* meant a joint or a fastening.
- So harmony is about how parts are joined: not pleasantness, and not correctness.

**Belonging, not sameness.**
- Good wholes are made of different parts.
- The philosopher Francis Hutcheson put beauty in "a compound Ratio of Uniformity and Variety".
- The dramatist John Dryden, defending plays that mix comedy and tragedy, wrote that "contraries, when placed near, set off each other".
- **What breaks harmony is a part that does not belong.** Difference does not break it. That is why harmony can never be checked as "consistency" alone. The one exception is names and formats, where variation carries no meaning and should simply not happen.

**The test of belonging.**
- Aristotle's test for a unified plot: the parts are joined so that "if any one of them is displaced or removed, the whole will be disjointed and disturbed. For a thing whose presence or absence makes no visible difference, is not an organic part of the whole."
- Edgar Allan Poe's version, for the short story: "there should be no word written, of which the tendency, direct or indirect, is not to the one pre-established design."

**The criterion: what counts as a kind of harmony.**
- **A kind of harmony compares elements of the same communication with each other:**
  - a part with a part;
  - a part with the whole;
  - how a part is said with what it says.
- **Anything that needs an outside reference is a different property:**
  - the record → fidelity;
  - the request, the relationship, the occasion → fit.
- **Two levels inside the composed communication** (added 2026-10-10).
  - **The account:** the substrate, written before drafting.
  - **The text.**
  - Comparing across the two levels is internal.
  - The need, the sources and the relationship stay outside, even when they are written into the substrate, because they are recorded there, not composed.

**Why this criterion and not a wider one.**
- 20-02's regards mixed comparisons with three different things: the record, the situation, and the other parts. That mixing is what you objected to.
- Readers also feel the two failures differently:
  - a message that **does not hang together** reads as disjointed, stitched or "weird";
  - a message that **does not suit** its reader reads as inappropriate or tone-deaf.
- An email to a company's chief executive written casually from start to finish hangs together and does not suit. An email that is formal except for one slangy paragraph does not hang together.

### 4. The kinds (five since 2026-10-10)

Each kind is defined by what must stay together. The aspects listed are examples, not a checklist.

Since 2026-10-10, each kind is also the one that a particular kind of change can break:
- content: a change of meaning;
- the through-line: reordering;
- the skin: rewording;
- who-to-whom: re-addressing;
- due weight: re-emphasis.

#### 4.0 Content (added 2026-10-10)

- **Holds when** the meanings hold together, whatever order they are told in and whatever words are used:
  - the meanings agree;
  - they bear on the point;
  - they stay within the support the communication itself gives;
  - together they establish one thing.
- **"One thing" can be:**
  - a claim (an argument);
  - a purpose (a manual: help the user do X);
  - an effect (a story).
- **Covers your words:**
  - "Harmony of the. Uh, the content", which "is different from the like phrasing" (2026-10-10);
  - the phrase you started with in this inquiry and then turned away from: "are the meanings told … in harmony with each other".
- **The reader asks when it breaks:** "Wait, which is it? That doesn't follow from what you've shown. What does this have to do with the rest?"
- **What does not break it:**
  - a tension the communication declares, such as "we disagree on X";
  - an apparent clash that a dropped limit explains (restore the limit first).
- **What it is not:**
  - truth (a consistent account can be false);
  - soundness;
  - completeness;
  - fit.
- **What you find:** not named yet.
- **Lineage:**
  - de Beaugrande and Dressler's coherence of the "textual world";
  - classical rhetoric's *inventio*;
  - developmental editing.

Details are in 07-57, sections 2 to 4.

#### 4.1 Through-line (your first variation; narrowed 2026-10-10)

- **Holds when** (updated 2026-10-10): each told part follows from the one before, along the account's structure.
  - One topic is told in one place.
  - Nothing loops back to a topic already closed.
  - No jump goes unmarked.
- **As concluded on 2026-10-09, it held more:** "the communication establishes one thing; every part helps establish it; each part follows from, or connects to, what is around it; and no part contradicts another".
  - Only the telling part of the third clause stays here.
  - The rest is content (4.0).
- **Covers your words** (updated 2026-10-10):
  - "sequential of the text" and "checks the connections" stay here;
  - "contributes toward one meaning" has two faces:
    - the paragraph's meaning bearing on the point is content;
    - the paragraph's place on the path is the through-line.
- **The reader asks when it breaks:** "How does this follow? Why are we back here? I've lost the thread."
- **What you find:** drift. Since 2026-10-10, whether "drift" still fits here, or fits content's off-point parts better, is part of the name test.
- **On watch** (added 2026-10-10). The peer taxonomy (section 8) has an order property: "each meaning comes before what depends on it". The through-line merges into it if five real texts show no path failure that is not also an order failure or a content failure.
- **Lineage:**
  - van Dijk's local and global coherence;
  - Wendell's unity and coherence;
  - the theatre term "through-line".

#### 4.2 Skin (your second variation)

- **Holds when:** the surface (words, names for things, tone, formality, sentence style, person and tense, conventions, format) belongs to one piece. Variation stays within one character of writing. The joins do not show. Names and formats are one per thing.
- **The reader asks when it breaks:** "Why does it suddenly sound different? Are these the same thing?"
- **What you find:** seams.
- **Lineage:**
  - Mailchimp's style guide: "you have the same voice all the time, but your tone changes";
  - the "voice", "word choice", "fluency" and "conventions" traits of the 6+1 writing model.

#### 4.3 Who-to-whom (the third kind)

- **Holds when:** every part is said by the same speaker, to the same audience, at that audience's level.
- **Covers** your earlier words: "harmony of who's sending to this message this communication to who … all uh, paragraphs they should also have harmony with it too".
- **The reader asks when it breaks:** "Who is this for? Who's talking?"
- **What you find:** a misaddressed part, meaning a part from someone else or to someone else.
- **Lineage:** the linguist M.A.K. Halliday's "interpersonal" meaning, "an exchange of moves … between speaker and addressee", which he treats as its own plane beside content.

#### 4.4 Due weight (a fourth kind you did not name)

- **Holds when:**
  - what matters most stands out most;
  - space follows importance;
  - how it sounds matches how serious and how firm the meaning is;
  - headings, lists and signposts match the meaning's structure.
- **The reader asks when it breaks:** "Wait, *that* was the important part? Why so cheerful about this?"
- **What you find:** wrong weight.
- **Lineage:** the classical third principle of composition, alongside unity and coherence. The rhetorician Barrett Wendell called it "mass": placing "the chief parts" where they stand out.

### 5. How the kinds fit together

**Three things stay one, and one thing matches** (as concluded on 2026-10-09; updated below). Every communication is someone saying something to someone in some way, which gives three planes:
- **who** is speaking to **whom**;
- **what** is said;
- **how** it is said.

Each plane must stay one from part to part. That gives who-to-whom, the through-line and the skin. Across the planes, the "how" must carry the "what" at its due weight.

This structure is why there are four kinds and not some other number. Halliday's three kinds of meaning (interpersonal, content, and the text as a message) separate the same three planes.

**Updated 2026-10-10: two kinds in the account, three in the telling.**
- **The "what" plane held two kinds:**
  - what is meant: content;
  - the order it is told in: the through-line.
- **So four things stay one, and one thing matches:**
  - **the account has two kinds:** what is said (content), and who speaks to whom (who-to-whom);
  - **the telling has three:** its order (the through-line), its surface (the skin), and its emphasis (due weight).
- **Each kind of the telling is broken by changing that one thing:** reordering, rewording, or re-emphasis.

**The two levels** (added 2026-10-10).
- **The substrate** is the account written before drafting. There:
  - content and who-to-whom are **decided**;
  - the order, the names and the rank are **planned**.
- **The text** realises all five kinds.
- **The text must match its substrate:**
  - every part of the text has a home in the account;
  - nothing in the text contradicts the account.
- **The repair has two coordinates:**
  - **the kind** names what must change;
  - **the level** names who decides:
    - for a clash in the substrate, the author;
    - for a clash only in the text, it is re-told from the substrate;
    - for a mismatch between the two, a diagnosis comes first.

**The kinds depend on each other in two places** (updated 2026-10-10):
- **Due weight needs content.** You cannot match prominence to importance until the account has a main point and a rank.
- **An even skin can hide broken content.** Fluent text reads as if it holds together. This matters for AI drafting, where a fluent surface comes cheaply.

**Every kind applies at every scale and on every channel:**
- **Across a thread:** a follow-up that contradicts the last message breaks content (updated 2026-10-10; was the through-line).
- **Across a series:** weekly reports that rename the same measure break the skin.
- **Across channels:** a slide that stresses one thing while the speaker stresses another breaks due weight.

### 6. What harmony is not

**Four neighbouring properties are easily confused with harmony.** Each is the communication agreeing with something other than itself:

| Property | The communication agrees with… | The reader asks when it fails | Hangs together, yet fails this one |
|---|---|---|---|
| **fit** | its situation: the request, the relationship, the occasion | "This isn't what I asked. Why are you telling *me* this?" | a coherent, evenly casual email to a chief executive |
| **fidelity** | the record and the sources | "Is that true? Did anyone decide that?" | a well-joined message announcing a decision nobody made |
| **intelligibility** | its own intended meaning (the text says it, one way) | "Which one? What do you mean?" | an evenly vague memo with one point |
| **completeness** | (the exception: concerns presence, not agreement) | "Where's the ask?" | a coherent proposal with no ask |

**Harmony is never a sign of truth.** A well-joined falsehood persuades more easily than a broken one, so fidelity must be checked separately.

**The rule that names each failure once:** write "this clashes with that". It is a harmony failure only if "that" can be quoted from the communication itself.

| Case | What clashes with what | Named as |
|---|---|---|
| a formal letter in which one paragraph turns curt | this paragraph's surface against the rest of the surface | skin |
| an apology that slides from "I'm so sorry" to "The company regrets any inconvenience" | this part's speaker against the letter's speaker | who-to-whom |
| a board memo with one paragraph written for the engineers | this part's audience against the memo's audience | who-to-whom |
| an executive summary saying "on track" over a body showing two missed milestones | one meaning against another | content (updated 2026-10-10; was through-line) |
| "There are three reasons:" followed by two | the signpost against the structure | due weight |
| the author's holiday story inside a recipe | this part against the recipe's point | content (updated 2026-10-10; was through-line). Cut it; there is nothing to connect. |
| an evenly casual email to a chief executive | the manner against the actual relationship | fit |
| a proposal with no ask | nothing on the other side | completeness |

**Two lenses on one failure.** Some failures are also seen through a neighbour's lens. That is still one failure:
- an ask hidden in a closing parenthesis is **due weight** inside the message, and **fit** against the request;
- a contradiction is a **content** failure (updated 2026-10-10; was through-line), and also blocks understanding (**intelligibility**).

**A felt contradiction can be fit** (added 2026-10-10). The UK's guidance of 11 May 2020 said "go to work" and "avoid public transport if possible". The two clash only against many readers' circumstances, which lie outside the text. So it is fit, not content.

## Finding

### 1. Why this question

**The project.** Communication Craft is this project's work on how messages are formed and checked. It builds a **substrate** before any text is written: a structured account of what a message must convey. A substrate holds:
- the need;
- the main point and key points;
- the facts, each with its standing (how firmly it is held, and on what basis);

- the ask;

**The previous step.** 21-40 defined harmony as "the different parts of a communication belong together as one whole". It found four kinds:
- **through-line,** your first variation;
- **skin,** your second;
- **who-to-whom;**
- **due weight.**

21-40 kept "meanings agree" inside the through-line, and deferred the question of splitting it out.

**Your message.** You then said that harmony has another kind, harmony of the content. You said it is different from phrasing. You added that "content's and substrate's harmony" is another version of harmony, and that it is important.

**This inquiry tested three things:**

### 2. What content harmony is

**Definition.** The meanings of a communication hold together, whatever order they are told in and whatever words are used.

**The four aspects.** These are examples of how it holds, not a checklist.

| Aspect | Holds when | Fails as |
|---|---|---|
| **agree** | no two facts, figures, claims, decisions or statuses contradict | "costs £10k" in one paragraph, "£50k" in another |
| **bear on the point** | every meaning relates to what the communication is establishing | a paragraph that relates to nothing else (an orphan) |
| **stay within their grounds** | no claim goes further than the support the communication itself gives | a conclusion stronger than the evidence stated in the same text |
| **establish one thing** | the meanings together establish one claim, purpose or effect, each level summing up what is below it | a slogan "trying to do a minimum of six things" |

**What it is made of.** The project's unit of meaning (from devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-09_16-16__the-meaning-unit-what-a-communication-conveys/finding.md, 16-16) has three parts:
- a content;
- a force (what is done with it: stated, asked, decided);
- internal limits.

Units are linked by relations such as support, condition, contrast and sequence. Content harmony is these relations holding:
- **a contradiction** is an unmarked clash between two units;
- **an orphan** is a unit with no relation to the point;
- **an overreach** is a unit whose force is stronger than the units supporting it allow.

**It occurs in every form tested.** These cases were constructed to show reach, not frequency:

| Form | Content disharmony |
|---|---|
| an explainer | a term defined one way in section 1 and used another way in section 4 |
| a design document | the constraint "no new dependencies", then a proposal that adds a queue service |
| an incident update | "started 09:40" in the summary, "started 09:10" in the timeline |
| a board paper | the goal "grow share" beside the plan "cut all marketing", with the trade-off never stated |
| a performance review | an "exceeds expectations" rating over findings that expectations were missed |
| a condolence note | "I didn't know him well", then "we were so close" |
| a recipe | the ingredients say 2 eggs; the method uses 3 |
| a contract | clause 4 grants a right that clause 9 removes |
| a novel | a character's eye colour changes |
| a scientific paper | the abstract claims an effect that the results report as not significant |

**What readers say when it breaks:**
- "Wait, which is it?"
- "That contradicts what you said above."
- "That doesn't follow from what you've shown."
- "What does this have to do with the rest?"

These complaints differ from the through-line's ("I lost the thread", "why are we back here?") and from the skin's ("why does it suddenly sound different?").

### 3. How it differs from phrasing and from the order of telling

**Your distinction already exists outside the project.** Several traditions separate what is said from how it is told:
- **Text linguistics.** de Beaugrande and Dressler distinguish **coherence** from **cohesion**.
  - Coherence concerns "the components of the textual world, i.e., the configuration of concepts and relations which underlie the surface text".
  - Cohesion concerns how "the components of the surface text … are mutually connected within a sequence". ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_(linguistics)))
- **The psychology of reading.** Kintsch separates "the surface form representation, the propositional textbase representation, and the situation model". ([source](https://sites.pitt.edu/~perfetti/PDF/Kintsch%20&%20Rawson.pdf))
- **Classical rhetoric** has three separate stages of composition:
  - *inventio*, "developing arguments and content";
  - *dispositio*, "organizing them in a coherent structure";
  - *elocutio*, "word choice, tone, and pace". ([source](https://pressbooks.pub/openrhetoric/chapter/five-canons-of-rhetoric))
- **Editing practice** works in three separate passes:
  - developmental editing: "story, organization, clarity, and overall structure";
  - line editing: "style, rhythm, tone, sentence construction";
  - copyediting: "mechanics … inconsistencies … house style". ([source](https://bernoff.com/blog/how-a-developmental-edit-differs-from-a-copy-edit-or-line-edit-and-why-that-matters))

**Content harmony comes apart from each of the four earlier kinds.** For each pair there is a case where one fails and the other holds, in both directions:

| Pair | Content fails, the other holds | The other fails, content holds |
|---|---|---|
| content / through-line | a well-ordered argument that moves cleanly to its conclusion, but gives the cost as £10k in paragraph 2 and £50k in paragraph 4 | correct, consistent installation steps told out of order |
| content / skin | even, polished prose whose summary says "on track" while the body shows two missed milestones | consistent facts, written half in formal register and half in chat slang |
| content / who-to-whom | one speaker to one audience throughout, but a goal ("grow share") that conflicts with the means ("cut all marketing") | consistent content, with one paragraph slipping from "I" into "the company regrets…" |
| content / due weight | the emphasis matches the importance, but the conclusion claims more than the evidence given | consistent content, with the key decision in a footnote |

**The change test.** Apply one kind of change to a text, and see which kind it can break:

| Change applied to a text | Content | Through-line | Skin | Who-to-whom | Due weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| reword every sentence, keeping the meanings and order | — | — | **can break** | — | can break (tone) |
| reorder the parts, keeping the wording | — | **can break** | — | — | **can break** (position) |
| change a figure, a claim or a status | **can break** | — | — | — | — |
| change who a part is addressed to | — | — | — | **can break** | — |
| change the emphasis (bold, space, place) | — | — | — | — | **can break** |

**What the test shows.** Content harmony is the one kind that only a change of meaning can break. That is your sentence, "content is different from the like phrasing", in testable form.

**How the test separates two kinds that one change can touch.** A buried main point is touched by both reordering and re-emphasis.
- **Position as a sign of importance** is due weight.
- **The order between parts that depend on each other** is the through-line.
- **Spin shows the separation on a real case** (section 11):
  - claims beyond the results are content;
  - emphasis on subgroups is due weight.

### 4. What content harmony is not

**Not truth.** A consistent account can be false. In de Beaugrande's words, "a textual world is created that does not have to comply to the real world". Whether the content is true is fidelity, together with the content check of devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-08_13-08__materialization-check-vs-content-check-in-communication-craft/finding.md (13-08). That check asks "is the meaning right?".

**Not soundness.** A consistent plan can be a bad plan. 13-08's example of "four people for a 15-minute job" is consistent and unsound.

**Not completeness.** A coherent account can lack its ask.

**Not the consistency peer.** The project's peer taxonomy (compare/devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-05_18-49__peer-categories-of-explicitness-in-communication-problems/finding.md) lists 15 properties of a text, each checked against one thing. One of them, consistency, says "no two statements, figures, counts or names disagree". It detects one face of content harmony, the agreement of statements and figures, in the text. It does not cover:
- bearing on the point;
- staying within grounds;
- establishing one thing;
- the substrate level.

It also covers names, which belong to the skin.

**Not fit.** A clash that appears only against the recipient's own circumstances is fit, because the other side of the clash is outside the communication. The UK's guidance of 11 May 2020 shows this (section 11).

**A declared tension is not a clash.** A contrast the communication declares does not break content harmony. Examples of declarations:
- "We disagree on X";
- "Clause 9 prevails over clause 4";
- two options set side by side as options.

Only undeclared clashes count. The declaration must be quotable: if none can be quoted, the contrast is a clash. This is the content counterpart of 21-40's rule for the skin, belonging, not sameness.

**Restore the limit first.** Many apparent clashes come from a dropped limit. "Costs fell" and "costs rose" are both true if one is about the EU and the other about the US. So the repair has an order:
1. Check whether the communication itself holds a limit that reconciles the two meanings, and quote it. If it does, restore the limit. That is an explicitness fix, and it needs no new decision.
2. Only if it does not, ask the author which side holds.

### 5. The five kinds after the split

**The map:**

| Kind | What must belong together | Only this change can break it | Lives in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **content** (new) | what is said: the meanings agree, bear on the point, stay within their grounds and establish one thing | changing a meaning | decided in the account |
| **through-line**, now "the path of the telling" | the order of telling: each told part follows from the one before, along the account's structure | reordering | planned in the account, realised in the text |
| **skin** | the surface: words, names, tone, formality, format | rewording | planned in the account (names), realised in the text |
| **who-to-whom** | the speaker and the audience | re-addressing | decided in the account |
| **due weight** | the text's emphasis against the account's importance and gravity | re-emphasising | planned in the account (rank), realised in the text |

**Why these five.** A communication is someone saying something to someone, told in some way.
- **What the account decides:** what is said (content), and who speaks to whom (who-to-whom).
- **What the telling adds:** an order (the through-line), a surface (the skin), and an emphasis (due weight).
- **Each kind of the telling is broken by changing that one thing:** reordering, rewording, or re-emphasising.

**What the through-line keeps.** 21-40's through-line had four clauses. The change test sorts them:

| 21-40's clause (verbatim) | Can reordering break it? | Now belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| "the communication establishes one thing" | no | content: the account has one point |
| "every part helps establish it" | no: a digression stays a digression in any order | content: every meaning bears on the point |
| "each part follows from, or connects to, what is around it" | split | **content**, for whether the relation between two meanings exists. **The through-line**, for whether the telling follows that relation. |
| "no part contradicts another" | no | content: agreement |

**The narrowed through-line, by its own failures.** It holds when:
- each told part follows from the one before;
- one topic is told in one place;
- nothing loops back to a topic already closed;
- no jump goes unmarked.

A concrete check: each part opens from something the reader already has, and moves on to something new, toward the point.

**Your first variation, placed.**
- "Paragraphs or sequential of the text … checks the connections" stays with the through-line.
- "Contributes toward one meaning" now has two faces:
  - **content:** the paragraph's meaning bears on the point;
  - **the through-line:** the paragraph is in its place on the path.

This assignment is the one most open to your reading. Section 13 returns to it.

**A watch on the through-line.** The project already has an order property among its peers: "each meaning comes before what depends on it".
- **The narrowed line has failures that order does not catch:**
  - scattering: one topic told in three places;
  - looping back;
  - unmarked jumps.
- **They can come apart in constructed cases, both ways:**
  - *the line holds while order fails:* instructions told along one steady path, where step 5 needs a tool that step 7 introduces;
  - *order holds while the line fails:* a report that returns to the budget three times, with every dependency satisfied.
- **No real text yet shows the line failing on its own.**

So the line stays a kind, on a stated test (section 13 and Next Actions).

### 6. Substrate harmony: a level, not another kind

**What the substrate is.**
- **The account.** What a communication means: its claims, facts, decisions, questions and requests, the relations between them, its main point, and who speaks to whom.
- **The substrate.** That account written down before drafting, or extracted from a draft afterwards. Two current forms:
  - the two-half brief of devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-09_17-45__signals-proposal-dialogue-as-communication-substrate/finding.md (17-45);
  - the point structure of devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-09_19-31__the-substrate-concept-that-arranges-the-message/finding.md (19-31).
- **The text.** What the recipient actually receives.

**No new kind lives in the substrate.** Every relation found inside it is one of the five kinds, or a neighbour:

| Relation inside the substrate | What it is |
|---|---|
| facts against facts; the ask against the open choices; the essence against its facts | content |
| the account written partly to the drafter and partly to the recipient | who-to-whom |
| the paragraph plan against the point structure | the through-line, planned |
| two names for one thing in the list of names | the skin, planned |
| the planned order putting background before the point | due weight, planned |
| the main point against the need; the register against the relationship | fit (section 9) |
| standing against the sources | fidelity |

What the substrate adds is a **place and a time**: the account, before any text exists.

**Formed, planned, realised.**
- **Formed in the account:**
  - content;
  - who-to-whom. The frame (who speaks, to whom, doing what) is chosen when the account is formed.
- **Planned in the account:**
  - the order (the through-line);
  - the names (the skin);
  - the rank (due weight).
- **Realised in the text:** all five kinds.

**The text is a telling of the account.** The same relation appears in two other fields:
- in software, a data model and its views;
- in Kintsch's psychology of reading, the situation model and the surface form.

Harmony belongs first to the account. The text adds its own telling and must match the account.

**When no substrate is written, the levels still apply.** The account can be:
- extracted from the draft and then confirmed (the backward route in 16-16);
- rebuilt by a receiver, who can check its content harmony without access to the sender's notes.

Live spoken exchanges, where the account is never written, are not yet examined (Open Questions).

**Your phrase, read.** "Content's and substrate's harmony" names a kind (content) and a level (the substrate) together. That pairing is accurate, not a confusion. In the content reading of "substrate" (devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_15-59__communication-substrate-concepts-and-boundaries/finding.md, 15-59: "substrate names the meanings carried by a particular contribution"), the substrate *is* the content, held before the text. So content harmony is the kind, and the substrate is where it is first formed and checked.

**Your first framing of harmony, honoured.** Your first harmony message, in 19-42 (the earlier inquiry on harmony across the substrate), spoke of "a harmony of um, like other other concepts inside the substrate". 19-42 kept "the substrate" as a level. Two later inquiries dropped it:
- devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-09_20-02__naming-and-defining-what-harmony-points-at/finding.md (20-02);
- 21-40.

This finding restores it.

### 7. The text matches its substrate

**The relation.** Every part of the text has a home in the account, and nothing in the text contradicts the account. It is content harmony, and who-to-whom, judged across the two levels. A text part with no place in the account is a stray part across levels.

**A borrowed picture.** Databases have referential integrity: every reference must point to a record that exists. A text part with no home in the account is like a dangling reference.

**It meets the materialization check.** 13-08's materialization check asks whether the text expresses well what the author has settled. It covers:
- explicitness;
- order;
- view and level;
- length;
- readability;
- unintended implication.

On most mismatches the two lenses see the same failure, and the repair is to rewrite.

**What the harmony lens adds.** It is not a new, separate check. It adds two things the materialization list does not name:
- **A text part with no home in the account.** An AI drafter can add a sentence that nobody decided. It is "well expressed", and it belongs to nothing.
- **The question of which side should change.** Sometimes drafting reveals a better meaning. Then the text is right and the account is wrong, and the account is refined on supported grounds, as devdocs/inquiries/2026-10-06_19-33__communication-substrate-materialization-and-review-loop/finding.md (19-33, on the essence) puts it.

### 8. Who repairs: what changes, and who decides

**The rule has two parts.**
- **The kind names what must change:**
  - a meaning (content);
  - an order (the through-line);
  - a wording (the skin);
  - an addressing (who-to-whom);
  - an emphasis (due weight).
- **The level names who decides:**
  - **a clash in the account** is settled by the author or the sources, because it needs a decision about meaning or frame;
  - **a clash only in the text,** where the account holds, is re-told from the account; no new decision is needed;
  - **a mismatch between the text and its account** is diagnosed first: which side is wrong?

**The case that separates the two parts.** The account holds consistent figures, and the text mistypes one.
- **The kind is content:** a meaning in the text changed.
- **The level is the text:** the repair is re-telling the figure from the account, not asking the author.

**Where this comes from.** It extends 13-08's rules:
- every check declares its kind;
- "problems are classified by what is at fault, not by which tool found them".

Here, a harmony report names both what is at fault (the kind) and where (the level).

**One exception lowers the cost.** Suppose the account itself holds the limit that reconciles two meanings (the "restore the limit first" case in section 4). Then the clash can be resolved without a new decision. An AI may propose the fix, and the author confirms it.

# [15] Finding: AlignCraft as the alignment foundation of Communication Craft

## Question

How do AlignCraft’s six layers and four pillars apply to communication? Can they give us a more precise foundation than a broad appeal to “harmony”, help establish the communication substrate, and make communication problems easier to name and correct?

The question also asks what this changes in Communication Craft’s current understanding—and whether a substrate aligned across all six layers makes the resulting communication good.

Communication Craft is the communication part of Workcraft, the project’s framework for doing work with AI. A **communication substrate** means the working understanding of a particular communication in its situation. It includes who is involved, what the contribution is for, the meaning and relationships that matter, their grounds, the expression and what is known about reception.

This finding establishes the conceptual relationship. The infrastructure for applying it is a separate development question.

## Finding

### 1. The larger change: communication becomes work whose alignment we can examine

The recent communication inquiries have been trying to explain a recurring problem: a message can contain relevant facts and follow a detailed brief, yet still fail as a contribution. A general word such as “harmony” points toward a felt problem, but leaves several different questions mixed together.

AlignCraft offers a way to separate those questions. A failure can concern the situation we are working from, the contribution we think is needed, the approach we choose, the particular acts we perform, their effects on other commitments, or the result we observe.

This is more than a resemblance between software and writing. **Creating, receiving and transforming communication are themselves forms of work.** They have intentions, alternatives, constraints, execution and consequences. Applying AlignCraft to them is therefore a specialization of the framework.

The source’s full [Alignment Chain](https://karaposu.github.io/aligncraft/print.html#the-alignment-chain) distinguishes strategy from concrete steps and places outcome comparison after execution. Those distinctions are important to the communication mapping below. The explanations and examples here are this inquiry’s application of them.

Workcraft already treats AlignCraft as a framework used across its capabilities. This finding makes that relationship more explicit within Communication Craft. See the Workcraft overview.

### 2. Workspace alignment: establish the situation we are actually communicating within

Your proposed context-and-environment mapping is sound. Communication depends on more than the words currently being drafted.

Relevant workspace understanding can include:

- Who is speaking, drafting, receiving or being represented.
- Their roles, responsibilities and authority.
- What has already happened or been said.
- Which facts, sources and decisions are current.
- What terminology or background is genuinely shared.
- Which materials and channels the participants can access.

The alignment question is whether our working understanding and available context are adequate for this communication task.

For example, a progress message may clearly report yesterday’s position after a relevant correction has arrived. The problem begins in its grounding. Improving the sentence will not make the position current.

Or the writer may rely on an internal document that the intended recipient cannot open. The writer’s access and the recipient’s access are different facts. A source being available to the drafting AI does not make its explanation available to the recipient.

**What this asks of the substrate:** carry the relevant situation, grounds and limits. More history is useful only when it changes understanding of the current contribution.

### 3. Task alignment: establish what this contribution is for

Task alignment asks whether we understand the communication we are trying to accomplish:

- What should it make understandable or possible?
- For whom?
- What is the speaker doing—explaining, proposing, asking, acknowledging, exploring or something else?
- What particular meaning should be conveyed?
- What scope, depth, stance and open choices belong to this contribution?

“A message about Signals” names a topic. “A recommendation that lets the manager assess a bounded start to Signals review” identifies a more definite contribution.

This clarifies your tentative suggestion that Task alignment is where “the substrate is not defined enough”. **The purpose and intended contribution are part of the substrate, but not the whole of it.** Context, approaches, commitments and reception also belong to the working understanding.

A clear task can still require further work to establish its content. We may know that the recipient needs a recommendation without yet having developed a justified recommendation. The account should expose that missing work rather than fill the space with general language.

**What this asks of the substrate:** make the actual contribution and its requirements clear enough to guide selection and later assessment. Keep the source of a requirement visible: an explicit request, an established arrangement, a supported inference and an author preference have different standing.

### 4. Action-space alignment: choose the approach to communicating

This is the correspondence that needed the most clarification.

**Communication’s action space is the relevant range of approaches available for accomplishing the contribution.** Action-space alignment concerns understanding that range and choosing an appropriate way to proceed within it.

For the same purpose, recipient and channel, we might:

- Give a direct explanation with supporting reasons.
- Compare alternatives against stated criteria.
- Work through an example.
- Tell the sequence of events that makes the result understandable.
- Ask a clarification before making a recommendation.
- Use a short contribution with accessible supporting depth.

These approaches are not interchangeable. A research narrative can be appropriate when someone asks how an investigation unfolded. It can burden someone who needs the conclusion and its basis now.

A channel is one part of the available space. “Email rather than a meeting” can be a consequential approach choice, but Action-space is not just a channel menu. Likewise, conceptual, technical, operational, strategic and evaluative **views**—ways of organizing understanding around different questions—can contribute to an approach without exhaustively defining it.

A useful contrast keeps the task and channel constant:

> The task is to explain why option B is recommended, in one email. A chronological investigation story, a criteria-based comparison and a worked example are three possible approaches.

The third layer asks which approach serves this exchange and why. It does not yet specify every sentence.

**What this asks of the substrate:** make consequential approach choices understandable. We should be able to tell why this kind of contribution fits the need, rather than discover afterward that the AI silently chose a research diary, sales pitch or document inventory.

### 5. Action-set alignment: make the particular contribution appropriate and feasible

After choosing an approach, we still need to decide what specifically to do.

For the comparison example, the concrete contribution might:

1. State the recommendation.
2. Identify the relevant alternatives and criteria.
3. Explain the evidence and trade-off supporting the recommendation.
4. Preserve a condition that limits it.
5. State the response needed, if the task calls for one.

The selected meanings, their relationships, order, wording and form all contribute to those acts. Timing, delivery and follow-up can also matter when they are part of the commissioned task.

A suitable approach can be enacted badly. The comparison may omit a material alternative. A qualification may disappear during shortening. A recommendation may be written as an already approved decision. These are different specific choices from the broad choice to explain through comparison.

**Your preference intuition belongs here:** the particular acts must be preferable and feasible under the actual purpose and constraints. But the layer covers more than choosing a nicer phrasing.

Two distinctions prevent confusion:

- **A plan described in a message is not automatically the action-set of communicating.** In a Signals proposal, running a weekly review is part of the proposed operation. Recommending that operation, explaining its basis and asking for a bounded commitment are communicative acts. Both plans can be examined, but at different task scopes.
- **The level is relative to the task.** “Explain the alternatives” can be one action in a larger workflow and an approach within a writing task. State the scope when that distinction matters.

**What this asks of the substrate:** establish the particular contribution well enough that materialization—the actual expression—can preserve it, and a reviewer can inspect what was actually produced.

### 6. Coherence alignment: understand what the communication affects

Your proposal about other communications, future communications and other people captures an important part of this layer.

Communication can also change the work itself. A message can create an expectation, imply approval, assign responsibility, announce a decision or commit someone to a date. Those effects may disturb an existing arrangement even when every sentence is clear.

For example:

> A message promises Friday delivery, while the same team’s existing commitments already use the necessary capacity.

The problem is not merely inconsistent text. The communication introduces a commitment that does not fit the work. A wording change cannot create the missing capacity or authority.

Coherence can therefore examine relationships:

- Within the contribution.
- Between the contribution and earlier messages or related versions.
- Between what different audiences are told.
- Between the communication and existing responsibilities, decisions or constraints.
- Between a new commitment and the work that would have to honour it.

**Coherence does not require sameness.** A correction should change an earlier false statement. A technical appendix can use a different level of detail from an executive summary. A deliberate joke can belong in an otherwise serious tribute. The question is whether the differences and changes fit the contribution and its relevant relationships.

**What this asks of the substrate:** identify important things that must be preserved, things being deliberately revised, and effects that require consideration. It does not require predicting every possible future consequence.

### 7. Outcome alignment: compare the actual result with the intended result

Outcome is not simply “the communication text exists”. The relevant result depends on the task.

| Task being examined | A relevant result |
|---|---|
| Establish the substrate | A grounded working account adequate for its intended use. |
| Prepare a message | An inspected expression that supplies the commissioned contribution. |
| Deliver it | The contribution reaches the intended destination under the task’s conditions. |
| Establish understanding | Available evidence supports that the recipient understood the relevant meaning. |
| Support a decision or action | The resulting decision or action is compared with the intended contribution and wider task. |

This answers the ambiguity in your proposal. **When the task is forming the substrate, the substrate is the outcome. When the task is communicating to another party, the substrate supports an outcome beyond itself.**

Before communicating, we can establish the intended effect, inspect the draft and anticipate possible readings. Afterward, we can use whatever relevant evidence is available: a reply, a paraphrase, a decision, an action or another observable result.

Those kinds of evidence establish different things. A recipient can understand a recommendation and reject it. An AI can acknowledge an instruction while missing a condition in its subsequent plan. Silence can have several causes. A desired decision can occur partly because of events outside the communication.

A model reading the draft as a simulated recipient can be a useful preparatory check. It does not establish what the actual recipient understood.

**What this asks of the substrate:** distinguish intended, anticipated, observed and unknown effects. Incorporate real feedback when it becomes available, and let that feedback improve the next contribution.

### 8. What it means to form the substrate through the six alignments

The strongest version of your idea is this:

> **Form the working understanding by establishing what each alignment needs, and by connecting those understandings so they can guide the contribution.**

That gives the substrate a practical organizing logic:

| Alignment concern | Understanding to establish |
|---|---|
| Workspace | What situation are we in, what do we know, what supports it and what is relevant here? |
| Task | What should this contribution accomplish and convey, for whom, within which limits? |
| Action-space | What approaches are available, and why does the chosen approach fit? |
| Action-set | What particular meanings, acts and expression choices should carry it out? |
| Coherence | What must remain compatible, what will change and who or what may be affected? |
| Outcome | What result are we seeking, and what evidence can or does tell us what happened? |

The value lies in the connections between the answers.

A recipient’s need should help justify the approach. The approach should help justify the selected meanings and their order. A claim’s grounds should constrain its strength. Existing responsibilities should constrain a promise. Feedback should constrain what we believe the contribution achieved.

The substrate therefore needs more than a list of facts beside a purpose beside a paragraph plan. It should support an explanation of **why those choices belong together in this communication**.

This does not make formation purely deductive. Establishing the account may require retrieving a source, developing a judgment, choosing among approaches or asking about an undelegated commitment. Drafting may reveal a missing distinction. Receiving feedback may show that the original need was misunderstood.

The account can develop through that work. A change remains accountable to its grounds: deleting a requirement merely so that a weak draft passes is not a correction of understanding.

#### Forming the substrate can itself be examined through AlignCraft

At that local scope, the task is to establish usable understanding. The actions might include gathering relevant material, clarifying the intended contribution, developing missing reasoning and checking the assembled account. The outcome is an account adequate for the next use.

This gives a precise meaning to “the substrate is aligned across six dimensions”. Its **formation and adequacy** can be examined through all six questions. That local success does not establish that a later draft, delivery or recipient response will also succeed.

There is no need for endless nested checklists. Use the questions at the scope where a consequential uncertainty or decision needs attention.

#### The amount of explicit representation depends on the use

“Received, thanks” in an established thread may need no separate substrate document. The relevant file, participants and purpose are already clear.

A complex handoff between people or AI agents may need a retained account because the next participant cannot reconstruct the same understanding reliably. A six-section brief is one possible form. Connected prose, an annotated outline or an existing task account can also serve the role.

The general substrate map remains broader than any one record. The substrate foundation already treats its facets as a guide to what can matter, not compulsory fields.

#### The same questions apply when receiving

Receiving is also an activity with a purpose. We may be reading to understand, assess a claim, carry out an instruction or prepare a reply.

- **Workspace:** the situation and context available to the receiver.
- **Task:** the purpose of reading—understanding, assessing or responding.
- **Action-space:** possible approaches, such as reading surrounding exchanges, consulting sources or asking a clarification.
- **Action-set:** the particular interpretation work, checks and questions performed.
- **Coherence:** how the new understanding fits with existing commitments or changes them.
- **Outcome:** what was actually understood or made possible.

A receiver’s account remains an interpretation supported by the expression and context. It is not direct access to the sender’s private understanding. A later reply needs its own stance and purpose: understanding a request does not decide whether to accept it.

The same distinction matters in AI–AI communication. “Understood” is an acknowledgement; a returned plan that preserves the relevant constraint provides different evidence of uptake. The framework applies to both, without treating the acknowledgement as proof of the whole result.

### 9. What the four pillars mean in communication

The pillars separate four responsibilities that are easily collapsed into “make it clear”.

1. **Explicitness:** make the relevant intention, distinction, criterion or uncertainty definite enough to inspect.
2. **Visibility:** make the relevant account and actual evidence accessible to the person or process performing the comparison.
3. **Measurement:** establish the relevant properties or differences under a criterion. For communication this often means qualitative assessment, such as whether a qualification is preserved or a proposal is presented as a decision.
4. **Comparison:** examine that assessed state against its justified reference, and determine whether the difference matters.

A clear requirement that the reviewer cannot access is not visible for their check. Two accessible passages can still be miscompared. A stated criterion can itself be inappropriate. Each pillar contributes something; none removes the need for judgment.

The table applies all four across the layers. It explains the work, rather than prescribing twenty-four records for every message.

| Layer | Make explicit | Make visible | Assess | Compare |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Workspace | Relevant situation, grounds and limits. | Supporting records and current state. | What is supported, current, conflicting or unavailable. | Working understanding against the situation and evidence. |
| Task | Intended contribution, scope and openness. | Request, corrections and interpretation. | What the interpretation actually commits to. | Interpretation against the warranted task and constraints. |
| Action-space | Alternatives and reasons for the approach. | The options and basis for choosing. | What each approach makes possible or difficult. | Chosen approach against the purpose and feasible alternatives. |
| Action-set | Particular acts, meanings and material choices. | Plan, actual expression and executed acts. | What was expressed or done, with which omissions and qualifications. | Actual contribution against its supported intention and approach. |
| Coherence | What is preserved and deliberately changed. | Related commitments, versions and affected work. | Which relationships are preserved, altered or contradicted. | Effects against the commitments and changes justified here. |
| Outcome | Intended result and how to recognise it. | Evidence of the actual result at the task’s scope. | What that evidence supports, including uncertainty. | Observed result against the intended result. |

The Workcraft overview already allows qualitative measurement. This communication application follows that interpretation; it does not invent a numerical alignment score.

The broad Explicitness pillar is also different from the existing explicitness detector. That detector asks whether an actual text supplies particulars its wording demands. It can support a bounded part of alignment work; it does not establish the task’s correctness, all source fidelity or actual recipient understanding.

Private preparation and recipient-facing expression also have different needs. Making a drafting constraint explicit to the AI does not require printing that constraint in the final message. What the recipient needs must be conveyed through the actual delivery and legitimate shared context.

### 10. Why the Signals failure is informative

The Signals example involved an AI helping prepare a proposal for a manager. A detailed account existed before the draft. It stated the reader’s need, the intended substance, supporting facts, open choices, a management ask and a paragraph plan.

The reader’s need included understanding the operational recommendation and the size of the proposed initial commitment. Yet the paragraph plan began with the available reports and trial findings, and the draft opened with the count of 1,633 Signals. You objected to that opening.

This reveals a relationship worth examining: **the account stated the purpose, but its chosen approach and sequence did not clearly follow from that purpose.** The draft then shared the plan’s evidence-first order.

The aligned correction is more specific than “make the opening harmonious”. Establish what the manager needs to grasp first, choose an approach that serves it, and give the facts their proper role within that approach. If the task is to assess a recommendation, the recommendation and its reason can organize the contribution. The count can serve as background where it matters.

That judgment is specific to the task and the author’s objection. Opening with the count could be right in a message whose purpose is to report the size of the collection.

The evidence also limits the diagnosis. It shows the shared ordering and your reaction; it does not independently prove the AI’s internal cause or the manager’s eventual reception. Earlier inquiries overstated the number of defects in this example and later corrected them. This finding uses the full pasted account and draft, rather than inheriting that tally. The source and literal comparisons are recorded in the critique’s Signals analysis.

### 11. How alignment turns into a useful correction

A useful diagnosis can be expressed in plain language:

> Given what this contribution is supposed to do, this part differs from its required meaning or constraint in a particular way. Here is the evidence, why the difference matters, and what needs changing—or what we still need to establish.

For the Signals opening, that means naming the decision-oriented need, the evidence-first sequence and the author’s objection. The correction concerns the approach and arrangement, rather than merely replacing the phrase “one collected week”.

Other mismatches need other work:

| What the comparison establishes | Fitting response |
|---|---|
| The account uses an obsolete fact. | Renew the grounding and affected claims. |
| The contribution answers the wrong question. | Correct the task understanding and selection of meaning. |
| The approach leaves the recipient to assemble the needed answer. | Reconsider how the contribution supplies that understanding. |
| The account contains a needed condition, but the draft drops it. | Restore the condition in the actual expression. |
| A new promise conflicts with an existing responsibility. | Reconcile the commitment or change what is proposed. |
| The recipient’s response shows a different understanding. | Investigate the mismatch and clarify or revise on the available evidence. |

A symptom can involve more than one layer. An inaccessible essential reference can concern context, approach feasibility and delivery. Those are useful angles on one problem, not automatically three separate defects.

Nor does a symptom reveal its cause by itself. A confused reply does not prove that the original task was misunderstood. It gives us a reason to inspect the relevant comparisons.

**The layers make questions referable. The evidence and the actual objects determine the diagnosis.**

### 12. What changes in the current conceptual model

The six-layer view organizes existing understanding and makes several previously underdeveloped relationships more explicit.

| Existing concern | Its continuing role | What the alignment view adds |
|---|---|---|
| Substrate facets | Identify what can matter in the situation, contribution, expression and reception. | Questions about whether that understanding supports appropriate choices and matches the relevant evidence. |
| Communication need | Establish what the contribution should accomplish for its participants. | Explicit connection to task understanding, approach choice and observed result. |
| Meaning, relationships and standing | Specify claims, acts, reasons, conditions, sources and authority. | A way to examine how those meanings govern concrete action and affect commitments. |
| Views | Help supply conceptual, technical, operational, strategic or evaluative understanding. | Placement within the chosen communicative approach, rather than automatic selection from a recipient’s title. |
| Conveyance | Examine what an expression makes available through its words, form and context. | Connection to actual enactment and, separately, later evidence of reception. |
| Content and materialization checks | Distinguish inadequate or unsupported meaning from failures to express established meaning. | Alignment questions across both, with correction directed to the object actually at fault. |
| Internal composition concerns | Examine how meaning, surface, address and emphasis belong together. | Wider comparisons to situation, purpose, alternative approaches, affected work and results. |

The current meaning and fidelity guide, recipient and purpose guide and operational guide already cover many relevant concerns. Their existence does not prove those judgments were performed well in a particular run.

The new foundation therefore should not be described as discovering that all these checks were absent. Its contribution is to make their larger relationship clearer, particularly approach versus enactment, effects on connected work and actual outcomes.

#### What happens to “harmony”?

The latest harmony finding proposed through-line, skin, who-to-whom and due weight: concerns about connected meaning, surface, address and emphasis. Those remain intelligible local questions.

They are not a replacement for all six alignments, and they do not all map exclusively to Coherence. A paragraph can fit the other paragraphs yet answer the wrong task. A consistent tone can be unsuitable for the actual relationship. A clear, connected message can make an unsupported commitment.

Your new proposal lets **communication alignment** carry the broader organizing role. Where a problem occurs, name the particular relationship instead of asking whether the whole message feels harmonious. Local composition still matters, without needing one umbrella word to explain every failure.

#### Two qualifications to earlier rules

The content/materialization distinction survives. But the location of a supporting quote is evidence for diagnosis, not an infallible classifier. We still need to know what the task required, what meaning was established, what was selected and what the expression changed.

Authority also follows the actual delegation. An AI commissioned to synthesize evidence or recommend an option can perform that reasoning. It should not invent a personal promise or organisational decision that was never delegated. Human confirmation and factual correctness remain different matters.

### 13. Is alignment across all six enough?

There are two different readings of your intuition.

**If all relevant alignments are actually achieved—including a justified task, suitable action, compatible effects and an observed result that meets the intended purpose—then we have strong grounds to call that communication adequate for that task.** This is the useful full-activity reading.

**A substrate prepared beforehand cannot establish all those later facts.** It can guide expression and observation, but the draft may lose meaning, delivery may fail, or the recipient may understand something different.

That distinction is already compatible with AlignCraft’s full outcome treatment. The framework closes the loop by comparing results with the original problem; it does not make the presence of a plan sufficient evidence of success.

There is a second limit. Showing that the six questions are useful does not prove that six is the uniquely correct, exhaustive or minimal grouping. Broad layers can overlap. Another grouping may cover the same responsibilities. Their value should be judged by whether they help establish understanding, expose real mismatches and direct better corrections.

So the useful commitment is:

> **Use all six alignments to guide and examine communication work. Keep each claim of alignment accountable to its reference, scope and evidence.**

### 14. What this makes possible next

The conceptual direction is now clear enough to use: **make the important alignment relationships explicit in the work of forming and checking a contribution.**

The next informative application would use a substantial case such as Signals and a different kind of exchange, such as an incoming request or a relational message. For each, show the situation, contribution, approach, particular choices, affected commitments and available result evidence. Then identify concrete comparisons and fitting corrections.

Compare that treatment with ordinary source-grounded review using the same material. The question is whether the six-layer organization improves the understanding and diagnosis, not whether it produces a longer account.

This continues the use-led development finding. The initial trial of the operational guide has already happened: four applications reportedly improved wording only. The next increment should examine what substantive judgments and alignment relationships were missed, rather than repeat the same guidance without inspecting its scope.

A shared reference or new skill may eventually help. Its useful form should follow those demonstrated needs. The conceptual finding does not depend on first building a universal context engine, six-field schema or automated alignment scorer.

### 2. What alignment is

**Definition.** Alignment is agreement between what was meant and what is understood or produced.

**AlignCraft's own words:**

### 5. The six layers in communication

| Layer | AlignCraft's question (quoted) | In communication | Compared: what, against what |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Workspace** | "does the person doing the work understand the environment they're operating in?" | the situation: the people, the history, the current facts and sources, conventions, access | the drafter's understanding, against the actual situation |
| **Task** | "what are we doing and why?" | what the communication must achieve and convey, for whom; the expected effect | the understood task, against the need and the intent |
| **Action-space** | "where do we act? … which region of the solution space" | the approach: a direct answer, a comparison, an example, a narrative, a question first; its genre and channel | the chosen approach, against what the task needs, among the alternatives |
| **Action-set** | "what exactly are the steps? … Each action should be unambiguous" | the particular contribution: the meanings, their order, emphasis, wording, delivery | what was produced, against what was meant |
| **Coherence** | "Are the defined actions aligned with the existing aligned structure?" | the communication against what already exists: its own other parts; earlier messages; other audiences; commitments | each part or message, against the structure it joins |
| **Outcome** | "Did the result actually solve the original problem?" | uptake (what was understood) and effect (what was then done) | the observed result, against the original need |

**All six alignments are required for every communication draft.**

### 8. The pillars, and what detection can check

**What the pillars are for** (this subsection was corrected after your review)

What must be aligned is usually hidden:
- in the author's head;
- in the environment;
- in earlier work;
- in what the recipient already has.

The four pillars are AlignCraft's way of uncovering that context so it can be aligned. They apply at every layer, in both relationships.

| Pillar | AlignCraft's gloss | What it uncovers, in communication |
|---|---|---|
| **Explicitness** | "State it clearly. Intent, context, and boundaries are documented, not assumed." | the situation, the purpose, the approach and why, what exactly must be said, what must be preserved, the intended effect. All stated, not left in someone's head. |
| **Visibility** | "Make it observable. All relevant state, data flows, and transformations are accessible." | that stated context, and the actual state (sources, the thread, earlier messages, commitments, and later the recipient's response), made accessible to whoever has to align with it: the drafter, the author, a reviewer |
| **Measurement** | "Make it verifiable. Differences between intent and outcome are quantifiable, not vague." | how a difference between what was meant and what happened would show. Qualitative is fine: Workcraft says "Measurement can be qualitative". |
| **Comparison** | "Check intent vs outcome. Continuously compare what was meant against what was produced." | the comparison itself, made at each layer |

**Two examples, at two layers:**
- **At the workspace layer,** the drafter needs the situation stated and the sources accessible. Otherwise they work from assumptions about it.
- **At the outcome layer,** the intended effect is stated before sending, and the actual response is made accessible afterwards.

**The pillars are not the project's explicitness detector.** The shared word "explicitness" hides two different things:
- **The pillar Explicitness** is about uncovering context: stating intent, context and boundaries so they can be aligned.
- **The explicitness detector** checks one property of a finished text: whether its wording singles out the particular thing it demands. It is one check at the action-set layer (can the recipient tell what is meant?). It is not a pillar. Nor is it "the base pillar", which an earlier version of this finding claimed.

**What detection can reach.** A check can only compare what has been uncovered.

| Alignment | Reference it needs | From the text alone? | Today |
|---|---|---|---|
| action-set: explicitness | the text's own demands | **yes** | **measured** (the explicitness detector) |
| coherence inside the communication | its own parts | **yes** | defined, not built |
| action-set: matches its account | the substrate | no | a concept (the materialization check) |
| task | the need, the request | no | guides only |
| workspace | sources, the record | no | guides only |
| coherence beyond it | other messages, commitments | no | nothing |
| outcome | replies, readback | no; only afterwards | Pulse (a concept) |

**So "we run the detections to check these six alignments" holds as a direction, with one precondition.**
- Each alignment can be checked once its context has been uncovered, which is the pillars' work at that layer.
- The substrate is where much of that uncovered context is written down for a drafter.

**A detector gap is not uncovered context.**
- A gap shows only that the text does not single something out.
- It does not show whether that thing was ever established. It may be known to the author and simply unwritten, or never worked out at all.
- Telling those apart means going back to the context, which is pillar work, not detection.
- An earlier version of this finding claimed the detector "elicits" missing context. That claim is withdrawn.

**One rule for every finding:**

> relationship · layer · this (quoted from what was produced) differs from that (quoted from where its reference lives).

- Harmony's clash rule is the case where "that" is quoted from the communication itself.
- The rule is also how a failure is placed: the layer is the one whose reference can be quoted on the other side.
- It carries your ban on "is it harmonious?" over to "is it aligned?".

### 4. Alignment, quality, or something else?

**Both alignment and quality, and the two words are not in conflict.**
- AlignCraft treats the precision of a document as what lets it align at all: "When you see documentation this way, the quality bar changes. A vague doc isn't just unhelpful — it's a broken alignment instrument."
- So integrity is a *quality* in AlignCraft's own sense: the quality bar for an alignment document.
- And it is *part of alignment*: the reader's side of it.

**It is not a separate family.** The tempting alternative is two families:
- alignment checks, between objects;
- integrity checks, of one object.

This was tested and rejected (Reasoning). The decisive reason:

- **An integrity check can only detect.**
  - On the project's trip benchmark, "about 90% of the explicitness detector's gaps could only be answered by the author" (13-08).
  - The non-ambiguity guide lists cases "When ambiguity is acceptable or necessary".
- **A separate family would issue verdicts it cannot ground.**
  - It cannot tell a real gap from intended openness.
  - It cannot say whether the text or the substrate must change.

**Integrity also comes before comparison.**
- If "the change" could be either of two changes, no comparison with the substrate's "the schema migration" can say whether the text carries it.
- This is the pattern XML names: "All valid documents must be well-formed."

**So integrity relates to the comparisons in two directions:**
- **a precondition:** no comparison can run where it fails;
- **a detector:** it shows the comparisons where to look.

### 5. Textual integrity and the base reference

**Definition.** Textual integrity means the text can be read as meant by the rules writer and reader share. It has three parts.

| Part | What it requires | Example failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Determinate** | Where the wording needs one particular thing, the text singles it out among those in play. This covers what each part refers to, and what each part does: request, report, offer, decision. | "Approve the change" when two changes are in play. A request that reads as a report. |
| **Consistent** | The parts belong together: none contradicts another or pulls against the whole. This is 21-40's test, "belonging, not sameness": a shift breaks consistency only when it does not serve the whole, or when it changes what the reader takes. | A title that contradicts the data beneath it. A thing renamed halfway, so the reader takes it as a new thing. |
| **Readable form** | Its form is one the declared medium can show. | A markdown table sent as a plain text message. |

**The base reference: the rules writer and reader share.**
- **The standing core is the same for every text:**
  - the language's rules;
  - a definite wording picks out one thing;
  - the parts belong together.
- **The declared part belongs to this exchange.** The three-gaps finding already lists what counts as supplied: "the text, the stated setting, earlier turns in the exchange, conventions of the genre, declared shared ground. What never counts: undeclared reader knowledge."

**The declared kind sets how strictly the core applies.**
- This is your own point: "it's a text message, so we expect this text message to have textual integrity".
- **Examples:**
  - a contract needs stricter determinacy;
  - a chat message can be looser in form;
  - a poem's deliberate ambiguity is openness that was declared.
- **When exactly a wording "needs" one particular thing remains the open question.** The three-gaps finding calls it "the boundary of the implicit". This finding locates it inside the determinacy rule; it does not solve it.

**Where the base reference lives: in the workspace.** AlignCraft keeps rules there:
- "Universal Rules: Apply to every project, every time";
- "Project Preferences: Specific to your tech stack, team, or domain";
- "convention guides", which lock down "The environment and its rules".

**Your "alignment of the workspace" is right about where the rules live.**

**But checking a text against them is not workspace alignment.**
- AlignCraft's workspace layer asks whether "the person doing the work understand[s] the environment they're operating in".
- An open slot in a fluent AI text rarely means the drafter misunderstood the environment. Most were meanings nobody had settled (the 90% above).
- Repairing them by briefing the drafter about the environment would miss.

### 6. Detect, then decide, against settled meaning

**The integrity step produces detections. The six-layer comparison decides each one.** There are three outcomes, as in the explicitness fix skill:

| Outcome | When |
|---|---|
| **fixed** | The substrate holds the meaning as a settled entry. The fix quotes it. |
| **asked** | The substrate is silent, or marks the point as still open. The question goes to the author. |
| **declined** | The text already supplies it, or the substrate marks it "left open on purpose". In that case the openness is made visible in the text, not resolved. |

**The deciding rule: a detection is decided against settled meaning.**
- Settled meaning is a substrate entry whose status is settled, or the author's own answer.
- It is never an entry an AI proposed that nobody confirmed. That keeps the project's rule that "an AI never settles its own units".

**When receiving a message,** settled meaning is the sender's. So detections become questions back.

**Pure form failures need no one's meaning.** A table the medium cannot show is fixed by restoring readable form to the same content.

**The substrate needs one distinction for this.** "Open" (not yet settled) must be kept apart from "left open on purpose" (intended openness). The project's status list already has "left open on purpose"; substrate forms should carry it.

### 7. The test that sorts any check

**The substitution test.**

> "If this were broken, would a reader who has only the text and its declared setting take a different meaning, or be unable to take one?"

- **Yes:** an **integrity check**.
  - It runs on one document and its declared setting.
  - It detects.
- **No, but the text fails what someone meant, needs, or must fit:** a **layer comparison**.
  - It needs both objects: the substrate, raw context, or later the response.
  - It decides.

**Then two more steps:**
- **Which object is checked?**
  - the substrate, at the first check;
  - the text, at the second;
  - the response, after sending.
- **Which layer?** The one whose reference can be quoted on the other side. This is 09-00's rule.

**The test excludes as well as includes.** That is why "everything is alignment" here does not mean "alignment means nothing". Two examples sorted as comparisons, not integrity:
- **House style,** such as British spelling: breaking it leaves the reading unchanged.
- **The author's dislike of unsolicited meeting offers:** the same.

**Each check declares three things.** This extends 13-08's rule "every check declares its kind":
- **(a)** what its reference stands for: integrity (the reader's means of reading) or a comparison, and against what;
- **(b)** where its findings can route: a rewrite from the substrate, or a question about content;
- **(c)** what it cannot vouch for.

**The explicitness bank, declared this way:**
- **(a)** integrity: determinacy;
- **(b)** findings route to a rewrite from a substrate quote, or to a question for the author;
- **(c)** it cannot vouch for whether the meaning is right.

### 9. What each alignment check contains

**Each check has an integrity step for the document it produced, then six comparisons.**

| Layer | First check: the substrate against raw context | Second check: the text against the substrate |
|---|---|---|
| **Integrity step** | the substrate, read by the drafter: decisions captured, levels marked, entries verifiable, consistent and determinate; every entry has a status, with "left open on purpose" kept distinct | the text, read by the recipient: determinate, consistent, readable form, against the base reference |
| **Workspace** | the substrate's account of the situation (people, facts, what the reader can access, conventions in place) against the actual situation | the text's facts and assumptions against the substrate's account. *Example: the substrate has today's figure, and the text states yesterday's.* |
| **Task** | the substrate's need and purpose against the request and the author's intent | the text's evident purpose against the substrate's need. *Example: a report where a decision was needed.* |
| **Action-space** | the chosen approach and genre against what the need calls for, among the alternatives | the text's approach against the chosen one. *Example: an investigation diary where a comparison was chosen.* |
| **Action-set** | the selected meanings, reasons, limits, asks, order and rank against what raw context establishes. *Example: a reply's limits dropped from the account.* | the text's acts against the intended acts: due weight against the rank; every part has a home; nothing contradicts |
| **Coherence** | planned commitments against existing commitments, earlier messages and other audiences. *Example: "Friday delivery" against capacity already committed.* | the text against what the substrate says must be preserved, and against the sender's conventions |
| **Outcome** | the intended effect, and how it would be recognised, against the need. This is built in before sending. | the text's aim (the ask, the next step) against the intended effect |

**Where the references enter.**
- Your references (operational writing, structure, harmony, formatting) are inputs to both checks.
- The base reference is used in each integrity step.
- The genre's conventions are used in the action-space and action-set rows.
- The sender's conventions and preferences are used in the coherence rows.

**Checking the text directly against sources is allowed, as a tool.** Its findings are still located through the chain:
- **a stale fact in the substrate** is a fault at the first check;
- **a current substrate with a stale text** is a fault at the second.

**Where the pillars act.**
- AlignCraft's four pillars (Explicitness, Visibility, Measurement, Comparison) are how the context each check compares against is uncovered: stated, made accessible, made measurable, compared.
- They act on that context, before and around the checks.
- They are not the integrity step, and not the explicitness detector.
- No project tool is a pillar. That was the mistake corrected after your review of 09-00.

2. **Does it agree with what came before?** These are the six layers, checked against the particulars of this exchange.

**Alignment passes through documents.** A document that cannot be read one way breaks the chain even when everything upstream is right. This is AlignCraft's "false checkpoint".

**Three more things follow from this:**

### 2. What the base reference is

**It is the workspace's written ground rules for texts:** "what every text of its kind should be", written down. While unwritten, the same expectations are the implicit contract 14-41 described. Written, they are the explicit one.

**It is an AlignCraft Level 0 artifact.** The match is by what it does, not by a shared word:
- AlignCraft's Level 0 artifacts are "convention guides" that lock down "The environment and its rules";
- Level 0 asks "What conventions are in place" and "What constraints apply (technical, organizational, stylistic)".

So AlignCraft's own criteria for an aligned artifact apply to the base reference:
- it "Captures a decision";
- it "Has a clear level";
- it "Is verifiable";
- it "Is current".

**It holds rules only.**
- What one particular text was meant to say lives in the substrate (the written account of what a message must achieve and say), the request, the sources and earlier versions.
- Each rule names what it is compared with, so a check knows which of those inputs it needs.

**It is a text itself.** It must meet its own rules.

### 3. Its principle level: three principles, set by input

**The axis is what each check must compare the text with.** This project's peer taxonomy defines every property of a text as "one property, checked against one thing, one test question". It groups the properties into three families by that input. The base reference takes the same three as its principles:

| Principle | Compared with | The principle | Its rule groups (the project's 15 properties of a text) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Textual integrity** | the text and its stated setting | The text can be read as meant from the text and its stated setting alone. It is **determinate** (where the wording needs one particular thing, the text singles it out), **consistent** (its parts belong together as one) and in **readable form** (the stated medium can show it). | explicitness; ambiguity (two readings); vagueness; connection; order; consistency; implication |
| **Faithful** | the sources, the author's brief or statement, earlier versions | The text says only what its sources support and its author authorised, and keeps what earlier versions settled. | support; authority; preservation |
| **Fit** | the request, the template for its kind of text, the stated relationship | The text gives this exchange what it needs. It answers what was asked, holds what its kind of text requires, carries nothing that burdens without serving the purpose, and speaks as the relationship calls for. | responsiveness; completeness for purpose; economy; register; processability |

**Why this axis and not a published one.** ISO 24495-1, the plain-language standard, has four principles: readers get what they need, can find it, can understand it, and can use it. They describe what readers achieve, which is tested with readers. This project judges a text from the text and its stated setting, never from what a reader knows. ISO's set also has no principle about being true to sources.

So ISO 24495-1 is a **cited standard** inside the base reference, for its guidelines. It does not organise the base reference. The same holds for:
- **Grice's cooperative principle:** "Make your contribution such as is required, at the stage at which it occurs, by the accepted purpose or direction of the talk exchange";
- **de Beaugrande and Dressler's standards of textuality.**

### 4. Your three references, placed

| Your reference | Where it sits | What holds it |
|---|---|---|
| **Textual integrity** | the first principle | determinate, consistent, readable form |
| **Multiple paragraph text quality** | "consistent", applied to the whole text, plus the structure a kind of text requires | the consistency rules (section 5.2), and the kind's part (section 8) |

### 6. How a rule is written

**Each rule states three things,** AlignCraft's own ground-rule format:
- the rule;
- why it matters;
- its valid exceptions.

**Three more things are stated once, for the principle or group, and inherited:**
- what it is compared with;
- when it applies (the kind of text, the medium, the stakes);
- its status: tested, draft, on watch, or deferred.

This matches what this project's overview asks of every check: "we need to know when it applies, what it examines and what evidence supports its judgment".

**A rule that claims to find problems carries a test pair:** one text that breaks it and one that meets it.
- **The pair makes the rule:**
  - verifiable, AlignCraft's own criterion ("Both sides can point to it and agree or disagree");
  - testable before adoption;
  - usable in the default check (section 11);
  - teachable to an AI drafter by example.
- **This is common practice in code.** Linter rule pages show "Examples of incorrect code for this rule" and "Examples of correct code for this rule".
- **The pair is taken first from the correction that produced the rule.** The original text is the one that breaks it, and the corrected text the one that meets it.
- **A pair an AI writes needs the owner's confirmation.** Otherwise it would encode the AI's own defaults.
- **The pair illustrates the rule.** The rule's wording decides its edges.

**Conventions carry one example, not a pair.** A convention is a decision, such as "we spell British", and makes no claim that a breach is a real problem.

**A rule whose only test is the text's purpose is not admitted as a detection rule.** That judgment belongs to whoever holds the purpose (section 7). This is why "change the surface only where it serves the whole" was dropped.

### 7. Exceptions: one principle, shown strictly

**A deviation from a rule counts as valid only where the rule's reason does not hold here.** Three ways can be shown at detection, from the text alone:
1. **Supplied:** quote what meets the rule's purpose another way.
2. **No difference:** name each reasonable alternative with its own result, judged against the rule's own purpose, and show the results are the same.
3. **The text says so:** quote where it declares the openness, the tension, the level, the summary or the alias.

**The per-rule lists in section 5 are cues** for where to look. They do not decide on their own.

**The fourth way is "following the rule would make this text worse".** Style guides allow it: "Depart from it when doing so improves your content" (Google's developer documentation style guide), and "know when to be inconsistent" (PEP 8, the Python style guide). But it needs the text's purpose, which is what was meant for this one text. So whoever holds that purpose decides it:
- **an author writing their own text** decides it, and keeps the departure consistent through the text ("When you depart from this guide, be consistent throughout your document");
- **an AI drafter or checker** does not decide it alone. It goes to the author as a question, or is decided against what the author has already settled.

An AI that judges "departing improves this" by itself is the self-confirming loop 14-41 described: its defaults write the text and then excuse it.

**Why the showing must be strict.** This project tested this exact question on the explicitness bank:
- **The first trial allowed one shared result:** "All 229 'indifferent' exemptions gave a single shared result … Many of those results only restated the sentence." Wrong exemptions got through.
- **The strict version fixed it:** "per-filler results, the slot's own act". Result: "The leak is fixed".
- **It found no more gaps:** "The principle holds, but it is not a recall lever."

So the strict showing keeps exceptions honest and checkable. It does not make a check find more.

**Each check's findings record the exception used and its evidence.** The bank already does this: its "not demanded" answer names the test that decided it.

### 9. Precedence, written in

**An unstated precedence is one more implicit contract:** the AI would settle conflicts by its defaults. So the base reference states its own. Two relations exist in outside practice, for two kinds of expectation:

- **Floors:** reading rules, faithfulness rules, and items marked high-stakes.
  - The kind of text and the stakes can tighten them.
  - A text may deviate at one point by section 7.
  - A workspace cannot lower them in general.
  - The Scrum Guide has this relation: an organisation's Definition of Done is followed "as a minimum".
  - Lowering a reading rule does not change a convention. It stops the text from being read one way.
- **Overrides:** conventions (style choices, terms, templates).
  - The more local wins: the workspace over a kind part, a kind part over a cited guide.
  - Google's style guide: "1. Project-specific style. 2. This style guide. 3. Third-party references." PEP 8: "Consistency within a project is more important" than consistency with the guide.
  - Once overridden, the convention is applied consistently.

**A conflict between a rule and what was meant for this text** is decided against what the author has settled, or asked. AlignCraft's ground rules say the same: "Ask for clarification if rules conflict with requirements."

**"Floor" marks items, never whole kinds.** A kind part declares how strict it is, and marks each of its items as a floor or a convention.

**Local terms meet the floor; they do not lower it.** A team's abbreviations look like a local lowering of "determinate". They are not:
- the meaning is supplied by the workspace's terms, which count as stated shared ground, since the bank counts "the setting plus the passage";
- this holds only for texts whose setting says the reader shares those terms, and only when the terms are given to the checker as part of the setting;

**This is the recipients' side of the base reference.** The reader enters only through what the setting states. No reader profile is involved.

## Finding

### 1. Why this question

**13-28** showed that "noticing something is off" is a comparison against what every text of its kind is expected to be. It said those expectations should be written in a base reference that lives in the workspace.

**You then:**
- named those expectations an **implicit contract**;
- said AlignCraft lacks it;
- asked how to add a general idea to a methodology born for code;
- asked where the line falls between the parts of AI work that may stay implicit and the "contract parts" that must not.

### 2. What an implicit contract is

**Delegated work is held to two kinds of expectation:**
- **what this particular task needs:** the task description, or for messages, the substrate;
- **what every output of its kind should be:** what good code looks like in this project, what a status update must contain, how formal a message to a client is.

**The second kind is a standing contract.** It holds across tasks.

**It is implicit while unwritten, and explicit once written.** AlignCraft uses both words:
- "Ground rules are your contract with AI": explicit;
- "implicit contracts that nothing documents but everything depends on": implicit.

**This finding is about the standing contract.** The task's own expectations are already the task description's job.

### 3. Why the AI's implicit contract is the problem

**Unwritten expectations do not stay empty.**
- **The AI fills them with its trained defaults.** AlignCraft: "AI brings its own biases to every project … Without explicit guidance, AI defaults to what it 'thinks' is best."
- **The defaults then become the contract you actually get.** Software has a name for this, Hyrum's Law: whatever is not declared gets depended on anyway: "Users build against what the system actually does, not what its documentation says".

**The same defaults judge the work.** You put it as: "it creates the text based on that, judges the text based on that".
- An AI reviewing its own kind of work tends to judge by the defaults it wrote with.
- So it can miss the very things it got wrong.
- The project's own measure supports this: with written rules, 79–82% of reported gaps were real; with an unruled prompt, 65%.

**A colleague would learn the contract; the AI keeps forgetting it.**
- A new colleague learns the workspace's unwritten contract and keeps it.
- The AI starts each session with the same generic defaults. AlignCraft: "'Here are my ground rules…' <- Eventually falls out … AI: 'What ground rules?'".
- So a written contract is the AI's only lasting copy of what is expected.

**AlignCraft's Data Dump says half of this.** It says the AI "has no implicit understanding of your industry, company, or goals". That is true of *your* understanding. But the AI does bring implicit understanding of its own, and uses it wherever you are silent.

**Training cannot fix this.**
- You cannot edit it.
- You cannot read it.
- You cannot hold it to one workspace.

### 4. The contract parts: what must be written

**The rule.** Write every standing expectation someone will hold the work to, unless one of three grounds holds:

| Ground | What it means | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared** | Everyone involved, the AI included, already does it the same way. | Contract law's test for implying a term: "so obvious that … they would testily suppress him with a common 'oh of course'". For the AI, "of course" comes from training, so check what it does without the rule. Check again after a model change, because defaults move. |
| **No difference** | Any reasonable way of meeting it is fine for whoever will judge the result. | The project's conveyance finding: "Any reasonable choice gives the same result". |
| **Declared elsewhere** | A standard you point to already says it. | e.g. a style guide or a published standard |

**Two qualifications:**
- **Write high-stakes expectations regardless.** The first failure would cost too much to wait for a correction.
- **How detailed the contract must be follows the stakes.** The conveyance finding: "A contract or a prompt to an AI agent needs fine grain".

**Competence stays implicit:** how to write code, how to reason, how to write a clear sentence.
- Polanyi: "we can know more than we can tell".
- AlignCraft warns that over-written rules cause "Overly restrictive development".

**AlignCraft already draws this line for code,** in "When to Guide vs When to Follow":
- **"Guide When":** "Domain-specific knowledge required … Business logic involved … External constraints exist … Past decisions affect current … User experience matters". These are expectations that are yours: write them.
- **"Follow When":** "Multiple valid approaches exist" (no difference); "Best practices needed … Common patterns apply" (shared). Leave these to the AI's defaults.

The match is one of purpose, not of shared words. Both chapters ask who should supply the expectation.

**This is the conveyance finding's structure, one level up.**
- A text may leave something unsaid only if it is supplied, makes no difference, or is declared open.
- A workspace may leave an expectation unwritten only if it is shared, makes no difference, or is declared elsewhere.

### 5. How the contract stays explicit and adjustable

1. **Write the known contract parts first,** including every high-stakes expectation.
2. **Give them to the AI before it works,** as AlignCraft's ground rules are given: "establishing clear expectations upfront".

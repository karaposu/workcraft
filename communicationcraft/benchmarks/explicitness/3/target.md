
# Group trip messages: review set

## How to review

Read each paragraph on its own, as the reader its setting names. Use only what that paragraph and its setting give the reader; nothing in one paragraph explains anything in another. Report every ambiguity or missing meaning you find. For each one, give the paragraph ID, the exact words affected, and what the reader cannot establish from them. If you find nothing in a paragraph, say so.

---

## T01

Setting: Message from Dana, who is organising a weekend trip to Porto for six friends, to her cousin Leo, who is joining on Saturday and has not been in the group chat where the trip was planned.

> Hi Leo, glad you can make it for Saturday and Sunday. Your train gets into Campanhã station at 12:10 on Saturday; the ticket is in the email I sent you on Monday. From the station, head straight to the Blue Door and we'll sort out the rest when you get there. Bring your share of the deposit in cash, as agreed.

## T02

Setting: Script that Sam, the trip leader, will read aloud on the coach to the twelve travellers at the start of a three-day trip to Edinburgh. The travellers hear it once and have no written copy. Sam's co-leader, Ade, is also on the coach.

> Good morning everyone, and welcome aboard. We should reach the hotel at about six this evening, with one stop for lunch. Count heads against the booking list before we pull away from the lunch stop. Tomorrow's walk is shorter, so normal trainers will do. Breakfast vouchers have to be picked up at reception before eight.

## T03

Setting: Message from Priya to her brother Raj, the morning after a phone call in which they agreed that Priya would pay the whole hotel bill for their trip to Glasgow and Raj would pay her back half.

> As we agreed on the phone last night, I've now paid the full hotel bill: £340 for our two rooms, three nights. Please send me your half, £170, before we leave on Friday. The booking confirmation is attached in case you need it at check-in.

## T04

Setting: Group-chat message from Marta, who arranged a weekend in Bruges for six friends, to the other five. Two things are booked: the guesthouse for Friday and Saturday nights, and a canal boat tour on Saturday. Tom has the login for both bookings.

> Quick update before Friday. Tom, please confirm the booking by Wednesday, but if the price has gone up a lot, cancel it instead. Tom and Ben, you both still need to send me your passport numbers. He also owes me £42 for his train ticket.

## T05

Setting: Message from Rosa, who booked dinner for six friends on their first evening in Madrid, to Mei, one of the six, who asked where and when to meet. Mei is staying at the same hotel as the others.

> We're meeting in the hotel lobby at 8:15pm on Thursday and walking together to the restaurant, which is ten minutes away. The table is booked under my name for six people at 8:30pm. If you can't reach the lobby by 8:15pm, go straight to the restaurant instead: it is at Calle de las Huertas 14, and I'm sending you the map pin now. Each of us pays for our own meal.

## T06

Setting: Email from Grace, who is organising a four-day book-club trip to Vienna, to Hannah, a club member who has offered to help with the admin. Hannah has edit access to the shared trip calendar and the shared folder but has not looked at either yet.

> Thanks for offering to help with the Vienna admin. We hold provisional concert seats for both Wednesday and Thursday under my name, and each night has an entry in the shared trip calendar. We've decided to go on Wednesday, so please remove Thursday. The shared folder has three drafts of the itinerary, and I'd like the times copied into the itinerary by Sunday.

## T07

Setting: Message from Oskar, who coordinates a club's annual city trip, to the eight members going to Kraków this year, none of whom has been on one of the club's trips before. Each member arranges their own travel insurance.

> Think of what happened on last year's trip: one of us had the receipt but not the form, so she got nothing back. So, two things this time. Pick a decent policy before you book your flight, and keep the paperwork somewhere safe.

## T08

Setting: Note from Femi, the trip organiser, pinned in the group chat for seven friends flying to Seville on Friday on different flights. Most of them have never been to the city. Femi will already be at the apartment.

> When you land, take the airport bus and get off at the stop for the cathedral. From there, go to the green gate and ring the bell for flat 4; I'll come down and let you in. Some of you land after midnight. The green gate is the side entrance of our apartment building, on the narrow lane that runs along the east side of the cathedral.

## T09

Setting: Reply from Ines to her friend Carl, who asked her in a message: "Is the hostel you found any good for the six of us?" Ines is collecting the money for the trip.

> Short answer: I think it's fine. The reviews mention thin walls, it has a private room that sleeps six, and we've already paid the £90 deposit. We have £600 in the shared kitty, which should be enough. I'll send you the hostel's page tonight so you can see for yourself.

## T10

Setting: Message from Omar to his friend Tess, one of seven friends driving to a music festival on Saturday.

> Final travel plan for Saturday: Marco and Lena are both coming with us, but Lena has no driving licence, so Marco will collect the hire van at 8am. He'll pick you up outside your flat at 8:30. The van seats seven, so there's room for all of us and our bags.

## T11

Setting: Message from Ruth, who looks after the money for a ten-person weekend in Dublin, to Joe, who has agreed to take over collecting payments while she is away next week. Joe has not handled any of the trip's money before.

> Thanks for taking this on. Collecting the money is one request in the group chat, not ten separate reminders. The kitty card and the trip account both show £1,200, and about £300 of that is already committed to the apartment. Each person still owes £45, to be paid into the trip account by the 14th.

## T12

Setting: Weekly update email from Lena, who coordinates a walking club's four-day trip to the Lake District, to the fourteen members going. Lena keeps the route details, including her own A-to-D difficulty grades, in a planning spreadsheet that only she can open.

> Here is this week's update for the trip. Day two is graded C, so please make sure your boots are well worn in before we go. The lake ferry only runs until five o'clock in September. On day three, the group lunch is booked for 1pm at the café where the walk finishes, and the bill will be split evenly.

## T13

Setting: Email from Daniel, who booked the train tickets for six relatives travelling to Paris, to his sister Sara, one of the six.

> I've booked all six of us on the 09:01 train from London St Pancras to Paris on Saturday 14 November, and your e-ticket is attached as a PDF. You can show it on your phone or print it out. Check-in closes 30 minutes before departure, so please meet us at the departure gates by 08:15. Our seats are together in coach 7, and the return train leaves Paris Gare du Nord at 17:13 on Monday 16 November. The tickets cannot be refunded, but yours can be moved to another train for a £30 fee, which you would pay, if you tell me by Thursday 12 November.

## T14

Setting: Information sheet from Victor, who is organising a three-night trip to Amsterdam for the twenty members of his choir, sent to all members before they pay.

> The trip costs £320 per person, which covers everything. That price includes the hotel, the return train and the hire of the hall for our concert. Meals and the canal cruise on Saturday are extra, so allow about £90 on top. Rooms are shared in pairs, so please send me your preference by the 1st.

## T15

Setting: Message from Nadia, who is organising a cycling weekend in Ghent for five friends, to the group. She has reserved five bikes at a hire shop near the station, and the friends sent her their café suggestions last week.

> Bike pick-up is at the hire shop on Saturday at nine. On Thursday I'll email each of you the shop's hire form, which needs your signature. On Thursday evening I'll also put your café suggestions through a route-planning app. Helmets are included in the price.

## T16

Setting: Text message from Paul to Mia, who are organising a twelve-person reunion weekend in York together, sent on a Wednesday morning. They have booked twelve rooms at one hotel; Ana is one of the twelve guests.

> Can you ring the hotel next Friday to confirm the twelve rooms? Ana's room is the overflow, so keep that in mind when you ring. If they ask about late check-in, say yes for Ana, Sam, Jo and me.

## T17

Setting: Message from Bea to her friend Kit. Kit sent Bea links to two hostels in Bologna for their two-night trip and asked which one they should book.

> Let's book the second one. It's £12 a night cheaper, and it's closer to the station where our train arrives. The first one has better reviews, but not by much, and we only need beds for two nights. I'll book it tonight unless you'd rather I didn't.

## T18

Setting: Message from Ola to her flatmate Kim, who is coming on the same flights from Manchester as Ola's group of four for a long weekend in Helsinki. Kim has not travelled abroad before and has not seen any of the bookings.

> Here's the plan for the long weekend. Our flight lands at 14:40 on Friday, and the hotel's check-in opens at three. Book your own ticket for the airport train before we fly, and remember the time difference when you choose the departure. Sunday: taxi to the airport, 5am.

## T19

Setting: Message from Arjun, who is organising a Friday-to-Sunday trip to Berlin for six members of his running club, to Bea, one of the six. At the planning meeting, which Bea attended, the group chose a walking tour along the route of the old wall for Saturday.

> Here are the meeting points for each day of the trip. Friday: under the clock in the main hall of the central station at 10:00. Sunday: the hotel lobby at 8:30, with your luggage. Could you also book the tickets for Saturday's walking tour, and send me the confirmation?

## T20

Setting: Message from Hugo, who has rented a holiday apartment in Florence for himself and four friends, sent to the four the evening before they arrive. Hugo is at a wedding until Sunday and will join them then; Ivo is one of the four.

> Welcome to Florence! The street door and the key box on the landing each have a code. When you get in, check whether the cleaner left the second set of keys on the hall table. If there's only one set, that's a problem for Saturday. Marina, the owner, will speak to the cleaner today, and she'll text Ivo the code after six.

## T21

Setting: Message from Kemal to the five friends coming on his weekend trip to Liverpool on 10 and 11 October, sent on Sunday 4 October.

> The tickets for the concert on Saturday 10 October cost £48 each, and I've paid for all six of us with my card. Please each send me £48 by bank transfer by Sunday 18 October; my bank details are in the next message. Doors open at 7pm, and we'll meet at the venue's main entrance at 6:45pm. All six tickets are on my phone, so we need to go in together.

## T22

Setting: Message from Zoe, a parent who organised a museum day trip to London by train for eight families, to Will, another parent who is taking over as group leader after lunch because Zoe has to take her son home. Will has not led a group trip before.

> Thanks for taking over after lunch. The group needs to be back at the station we arrived at this morning for the 17:40 train home. Do the Buddy Card check every time the group changes trains. If the trains are badly delayed, sort out an alternative with the families, and text me what you decide.

## T23

Setting: Reply from Kofi, who handled the hotel search for a five-person trip to Barcelona, in the group chat after Laila asked: "Have we got everything sorted for the rooms?" Over the past month the chat has discussed three hotels and two different price quotes for the hotel they chose.

> Nearly. The rooms are ready and they come out cheaper, so I'm happy. I still need everyone's full names for the reservation by Thursday. Breakfast isn't included, but there's a market two streets away.

## T24

Setting: Email from Bettina, treasurer of a gardening society, to the twelve members on its weekend trip to Bath. Each member paid the society £45 for a spa visit on Saturday.

> The spa visit on Saturday has been cancelled by the spa itself because of a burst pipe. Refunds will be given to anyone with a good reason. Everyone else will receive a spa voucher for the full £45, valid for twelve months. The rest of the weekend goes ahead as planned.

## T25

Setting: Message from Nora, who is leading a guided group of ten walkers on their first morning in Lyon, sent to the group the evening before.

> Tomorrow we meet at 9am at the Copper Kettle, the small café on the corner directly opposite our hotel's front entrance. I'll be at the outside tables with a red umbrella. From there we walk to the old town, about twenty minutes on foot, and we'll be back at the hotel by 1pm.

## T26

Setting: Message from Lucia, who is coordinating a family trip to Rome for eleven relatives, to her uncle Bruno, who has agreed to check everyone's passports. Bruno has two versions of the guest list: the one Lucia posted in the family chat in May and a revised one she emailed him in August.

> Thanks for taking on the passport checks for Rome. Please check that everyone on the guest list has a passport valid until at least the end of the trip, apart from the young ones, whose parents are handling theirs. Aunt Rosa and cousin Gina both renewed their passports this year, but hers still shows her old surname, so her flight booking may need changing. Send me the names of anyone with a problem by 30 October.

## T27

Setting: Message from Greta, who paid up front for most of a five-person trip to Prague, to the other four after the trip. Before the trip the group agreed on a room weighting: the couple who shared the double room count as one share between them for the apartment.

> I paid for the apartment and the airport transfers, and Felix paid for Saturday's dinner. Tonight I'll put all of it into the expenses app and apply the room weighting. After that, everyone pays what the app says by Friday.

## T28

Setting: Message from Yusuf, one of five friends sharing a rented house in Cornwall for a week, to Clara, who arrives on Wednesday afternoon after the others. The others will be out when she arrives.

> The house is at 3 Harbour Row in Porthmellin: the white cottage with the blue door at the end of the lane. The key is in the grey lock box to the left of the door, and the code is 4719. We'll all be out on a boat trip until about 6pm, so let yourself in. Your room is the small single room directly facing you at the top of the stairs, and the bed is already made up.

## T29

Setting: Message from Ana, who is organising a two-night stay in a mountain hut for six friends, to Theo, who is joining for the first time and has never stayed in a mountain hut.

> The hut sleeps all six of us in one room and provides blankets, so you won't need a sleeping bag. Bring a decent jacket and only essential electronics, as there is just one socket. We leave from the car park at the end of Valley Road at eight on Saturday, and the walk up takes about three hours.

## T30

Setting: Message from Ivo, who organises a yearly trip to Copenhagen for ten former classmates, to Nell, who has agreed to look after the bookings this year. Nell has edit access to the group's booking spreadsheet, where each planned booking has its own row.

> Thanks for taking over the bookings this year. Friday's restaurant is already in the spreadsheet, so you can tick that off. The boat tour on Saturday still needs approving before anyone pays for it. The hotel is booked and paid, so there is nothing to do there.

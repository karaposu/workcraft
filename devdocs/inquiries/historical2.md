## Finding 11: Discovering the understanding a communication depends on

**Original:** [finding.md](../../research/inquiries/2026-09-27_20-20__communication-dependencies-and-omitted-context/finding.md)

**Inquiry:** 2026-09-27_20-20__communication-dependencies-and-omitted-context

**Compilation edits:** Generalised a historical example about a workflow and its record, retaining the relationship without the company-specific tool context.

### Question

How should a future review of worker-to-orchestrator communication identify the understanding the message depends on—including important context that the draft never mentions?

The orchestrator is the person or AI coordinating the wider work. The user occupies that position now. A worker's message should let the orchestrator understand and assess its contribution without reconstructing the worker's investigation.

This inquiry develops issue 2, “Understanding what the communication depends on,” in the [eight-issue discussion note](../../research/analyses/orchestrator-context-review-eight-issues.md). It follows the [inquiry into the orchestrator's needs](historical%20finding%201.md#finding-10-what-the-orchestrator-needs-to-understand-from-a-workers-communication). Its outcome is a general reasoning approach for the future review; the exact skill procedure remains open.

### Finding Summary

- **The draft cannot supply the whole standard for its own review.** The actual task and relevant supporting work can reveal questions the draft never raises.
- A communication dependency is understanding needed to interpret, assess or use this contribution for its actual purpose. Being related to the subject is insufficient.
- **Contextual questions are a useful unit of attention.** They can concern an object, relationship, earlier decision, assumption, qualification or unanswered requirement.
- Discovery works in two complementary directions: from the task and work toward what should be communicated, and from the message's claims toward what makes those claims understandable and justified.
- A plausible question is not automatically a defect. The reviewer must establish why the understanding matters and whether the actual delivery omits or distorts it.
- **Knowing that an answer is needed differs from knowing the answer.** A critic can identify a required unresolved question without inventing the missing fact.
- Necessary context may already be adequately delivered. Some findings call for preserving a limitation or narrowing a claim; some messages need no additions.
- The approach supports a more inspectable review. It does not guarantee discovery of every hidden fact or establish measured reliability.

### Finding

#### 1. Start from the purpose of the communication

The project is developing Orchestrator Inbound Communication: how a worker explains its work to the person or AI coordinating it.

The earlier inquiry established that the orchestrator's needs follow the actual exchange. An explanation, an exploratory discussion and a recommendation can require different kinds of understanding, even when addressed to the same person.

This inquiry asks how a reviewer discovers the understanding needed for that particular exchange.

A **communication dependency** is a piece of understanding without which the recipient cannot adequately follow, assess or use the contribution. It might explain what an object represents, why an action was proposed, which evidence supports a conclusion, or what a result does not establish.

This does not make every fact about the work necessary background. The connection to the current communication must be explainable.

Understanding is also a legitimate purpose on its own. The reviewer need not invent a possible bad decision to justify explaining a relationship that the reader needs to follow the account.

#### 2. Extracting entities provides starting points, but can preserve the draft's blind spots

Entity extraction can identify the people, systems, documents and concepts named in a message. It is useful for asking what each reference means.

However, the [original ambiguity analysis](../../research/analyses/nonambiguity-problem-framing.md) records a recurring problem: an answer may name the right things while leaving their relationship unexplained.

A workflow and a record about it can both be named without explaining that one performs the work and the other records information about it. That relationship can be necessary to understand the reported problem.

The draft may also omit an entire requested answer. If the reviewer only expands the draft's existing nouns and headings, that missing answer may never become a question.

Therefore, entity extraction should contribute to discovery without defining its outer boundary.

#### 3. Give the reviewer grounds outside the draft's selection

The reviewer needs to consider the actual request and relevant underlying work alongside the draft.

Different material can reveal different missing understanding:

| Starting point | What it can reveal |
|---|---|
| The actual task or recipient's request | A required explanation, comparison or answer absent from the draft. |
| Relevant work, evidence or earlier corrections | An assumption, limitation, changed position or relationship lost in the summary. |
| The meaning of the message's claims and proposed actions | What a reader must understand to follow the point, and what the wording may imply without adequate support. |

These are complementary grounds for reasoning. They are not a required three-file arrangement or a closed taxonomy.

The important distinction is where the question comes from. A second pass that reconstructs “required context” only from the same draft-shaped summary can repeat the original omission.

Likewise, a worker-provided context list can be useful without being complete. Calling it complete does not establish that it contains everything the worker forgot to communicate.

The reviewer need not independently repeat the whole investigation. It needs relevant task and source content capable of challenging the draft's selection. Where that content is unavailable, its judgment remains limited.

#### 4. Use contextual questions to connect discovery with the recipient's needs

A contextual question names something the recipient may need to understand. For example:

- What does this record represent?
- Which action is being proposed?
- What earlier decision explains why this option is being discussed?
- Which conditions limit this result?
- Does the communication answer the question the recipient actually asked?

These prompts help the reviewer inspect relationships and implications as well as objects. They are examples of the reasoning, not fields that every message must satisfy.

Questions can be discovered in two directions.

**From task and work toward the message:** what must be understandable, given the actual request and what the work establishes? Does the delivered account convey it?

**From message toward its grounds:** what does this claim, comparison or action rely on? Does the available material support that meaning, including its scope and qualifications?

The directions complement each other. They do not imply separate agents, independent verification or a guarantee produced by running two passes.

#### 5. Separate candidate questions from supported findings

Broad consideration helps the reviewer notice omissions. Broad consideration alone cannot establish a defect.

Before turning a question into a finding, the reviewer should determine:

1. **Why this understanding matters here.** Connect it to interpreting, assessing or using the actual contribution.
2. **What is established about it.** Separate known facts, inference, conflicting evidence, missing information and deliberate openness.
3. **What the recipient will receive.** Check whether the required meaning is absent, misleading, contradicted or already adequately supplied.

These are judgments the reviewer needs to make. This inquiry does not prescribe three new report fields.

A guide might suggest asking who owns a piece of work. That can be a useful candidate question. It does not establish that an exploratory explanation requires an ownership assignment.

Similarly, domain knowledge can suggest checking whether approval matters. It cannot establish a company-specific approval requirement, person or deadline without grounds.

This separation allows the reviewer to search broadly in thought while remaining careful about what it asserts.

#### 6. Knowing the missing question does not mean knowing the missing answer

Consider a constructed situation with explicit roles:

- The orchestrator asks a worker AI whether an experiment provides enough evidence to proceed.
- The worker drafts a message describing the experiment's activities.
- A reviewing AI examines that draft against the request and the experiment material.

The reviewer can notice that the requested assessment is absent even if it cannot determine the correct assessment itself.

Its finding should identify the unanswered requirement and propose addressing it using adequate evidence—or explain that the assessment remains unestablished. It should not manufacture a recommendation.

There is a further distinction: an unresolved work question is not automatically defective communication. A message may accurately explain that the evidence is insufficient, why it is insufficient and what remains open. Whether that fulfills the communication task depends on what was requested.

The [vital-context-gap inquiry](historical%20finding%201.md#finding-02-exposing-vital-missing-context-and-possible-recovery-sources) remains applicable. When evidence identifies a possible source, the review can preserve that lead. A statement that a coworker coordinated a trial may support asking them where results are kept; it does not prove they possess the answer.

A vital unanswered question can matter even when no supported recovery lead exists.

#### 7. Some missing context is a missing limit

A communication can include true facts and still convey more than those facts establish.

For example, a worker might have tested one workflow while describing the result in terms that suggest general readiness. The missing context is the scope of the evidence.

The appropriate proposal may be to qualify the main claim. Adding several paragraphs of background would not necessarily correct its meaning.

The same reasoning applies to status: a completed design, an implemented process and an observed result establish different things. A message should preserve whichever state the evidence actually supports.

This expands the review's attention beyond “what else should be added?” It also asks what the reader must not be led to infer from the current wording.

That judgment should concern plausible readings of the actual communication. Unlimited hypothetical misunderstandings would create endless caveats.

#### 8. Judge the whole delivery, including useful supporting context

An explanation can be necessary without requiring a new paragraph in the main message.

If a directly usable supporting entry already explains the relevant relationship, the review should take it into account. The existence of that need does not automatically justify duplicating the explanation.

However, private worker notes are not part of the recipient's delivered understanding. A filename or source link also does not automatically provide the explanation the reader needs.

Material qualifications require particular care. A supporting file cannot be the only place that makes a misleading main claim accurate.

Long communications need both local and whole-account attention. Individual sections may be understandable while their relationship is missing or their combined implication is misleading. This duty already appears in the [comm-critic-d entrypoint](../../artifacts/skills/comm-critic-d/SKILL.md), the existing outgoing communication critique skill. Its inspection here does not turn it into an implemented inbound review skill.

#### 9. Preserve intended openness

A communication exploring options may properly leave a decision unresolved. A review should help the reader understand what is being explored and whatever boundaries are relevant.

It should not demand a final recommendation merely because recommendations often appear in managerial communication.

The same applies to an emerging concept. A provisional definition can be appropriate. The reviewer may identify language that falsely presents it as settled, but should not force precision the task deliberately leaves open.

The actual task controls whether an absence is a defect. Recipient title and generic writing conventions cannot decide that alone.

#### 10. Keep discovery, retrieval and correction distinguishable

The review may discover a material question that its available sources cannot answer. That is useful information for the surrounding workflow.

The next work might involve recovering an existing source, investigating conflicting evidence, asking the user, or continuing with a clearly qualified account. Which response is appropriate depends on the situation and authorization.

The critique's own endpoint remains findings and proposals. Identifying a source holder does not itself authorize contact. Identifying a possible correction does not apply it.

The same boundary protects against context expansion without a purpose. Further investigation should have a reason connected to what could change in this communication.

A fixed number of searches, tools or link-following steps can bound effort, but cannot certify that all relevant information was found.

#### 11. What the future review can take from this

The general reasoning can be expressed as:

> Establish what this communication needs to make understandable. Use the actual task and relevant work to raise questions the draft may omit. Examine the draft's claims and implications too. For each material question, distinguish why it matters, what is known and what the recipient actually receives. Report supported gaps or distortions while preserving intended openness and uncertainty.

This gives a later dynamic review prompt a stronger basis than “extract entities, then add context.”

It also supports improvement through inspectable findings: a missed dependency can be examined together with the task, source material and reason it mattered. An unnecessary addition can be examined in the same way.

The exact prompt, report arrangement and calibration method remain separate design work.

### Reasoning

The strongest surviving direction is grounded comparison between required and delivered meaning. It addresses all four aspects of the question: what a dependency is, where omitted questions can originate, how unknowns differ from communication gaps, and how the scope of discovery remains bounded.

Flexible prompts about objects, relationships, history and assumptions survive as aids to attention. Their value is generating candidates; the grounds for a finding come from the actual task, material and delivery.

The two comparison directions survive because they expose different omissions. Task-to-message reasoning can find an entirely absent answer. Claim-to-ground reasoning can expose a qualification missing from an otherwise present answer.

The inquiry rejected several stronger claims:

- **Entity extraction or draft-only review establishes completeness.** These approaches cannot reliably represent a requirement absent from their only input.
- **Write an ideal answer and treat every difference as a defect.** Differences may reflect style, optional detail or invented requirements. Another draft can suggest questions, but is not an authoritative standard.
- **Require a complete knowledge graph before review.** The graph can itself be incomplete, and a direct task requirement can reveal an omission without one. Persistent context remains an optional future aid.
- **Require a complete worker-supplied context manifest.** This presupposes the discovery the review is meant to perform. Such a manifest is fallible input.
- **Resolve every uncertainty or always add context.** Either would change valid exploratory tasks and encourage unnecessary material.

The distinction between evidence for a need and evidence for its answer survives because an actual request can establish that an answer matters before that answer is known. Evidence for a recovery lead is a further distinct matter.

Whole-delivery assessment survives because supporting explanation can satisfy a need, while a contradictory main claim remains misleading. Scope and version checks survive because an older related source can help locate information without proving current state.

Optional analogies involving recipes or maps helped test relational dependence. They provide illustrations, not empirical evidence. Long-term context reuse and learning from review examples remain future directions whose benefits require observation.

The first pass identified source-selection and question-support ambiguities. The second clarified them. The third challenged the resulting account with conflicting evidence, adequate support, hidden information and complete-manifest alternatives. The conceptual landscape remained stable across the last two comparisons.

## Finding 12: Use Unplugged Surfacing to ground Orchestrator Inbound Communication

**Original:** [finding.md](../../research/inquiries/2026-09-27_22-10__inbound-communication-unplugged-surfacing/finding.md)

**Inquiry:** 2026-09-27_22-10__inbound-communication-unplugged-surfacing

**Compilation edits:** No content edits; only metadata, heading levels and navigation links were adjusted.

### Question

Should the communication workflow that prepares a worker AI's contribution for the orchestrator use Unplugged Surfacing to gather and accumulate context? Does this bring it back to the context → dynamic critique → supported revision pattern developed for Fixcomm?

Here, the orchestrator is the person or future AI coordinating the work. Inbound communication is the contribution sent to that orchestrator: an explanation, result, proposal, update or other response.

The goal is to establish the right general composition before writing the skill. This inquiry examines component fit, existing-context reuse, recipient-specific duties and the limits of the context capability.

### Finding Summary

- **Yes. Use Unplugged Surfacing as the inbound workflow's context-discovery and renewal component.** Its existing task-based inputs and integrated context output already support this use.
- **The closest existing pattern is Fixcomm Wild.** Ordinary Fixcomm uses ordinary Surfacing; Fixcomm Wild already combines Unplugged Surfacing, dynamic communication critique and supported revision.
- **Keep an inbound-specific communication review.** Gathering sound evidence does not automatically explain the contribution, its significance or its qualifications to the orchestrator.
- **Reuse actual work already done.** Compare the current communication's questions with the evidence and source coverage already available. Investigate uncovered or changed questions without repeating unchanged inquiries.
- **Keep a route back to discovery.** A later critique can expose a new evidence need; it should return through the same discovery owner and update the affected review.
- **Working context, critique findings and Supporting Context have different purposes.** Appropriate content can be reused between them, but one artifact's existence does not establish that the other jobs are complete.
- **The existing component already provides substantial task-level Context Accumulation.** Broader continuity across separate tasks remains a different, unfinished responsibility.

### Finding

#### 1. Why context discovery belongs here

We are designing communication that lets the orchestrator understand a worker's contribution without having to reconstruct the worker's investigation.

Some communication failures come from weak explanation of facts the worker already knows. Others come from missing facts, history, relationships or qualifications. The second kind cannot be repaired reliably by examining the draft alone.

The [previous inquiry on omitted context](#finding-11-discovering-the-understanding-a-communication-depends-on) established that the communication's task and the actual work must also inform the review. A draft can leave an essential subject out entirely. Extracting only the draft's entities would preserve that omission.

Unplugged Surfacing supplies a concrete way to recover this wider basis. It can investigate the origin of the task, follow discovered connections, make independent inquiries in the task's available sources, and integrate what it finds. This is the same need that justified its use in outgoing communication.

#### 2. The reusable component already exists

The [packaged Unplugged Surfacing entrance](/Users/nsstorm/Desktop/projects/AIOpsMan/artifacts/skills/unplugged-surfacing/SKILL.md:22) accepts a task, purpose, starting material, constraints, source environment, work allowance and prior context account. Its operation does not require an outgoing message or a particular recipient.

It already maintains an account containing:

- The purpose and bounds of the investigation.
- The questions, assignments and source areas actually investigated.
- Supported understanding, usable evidence, qualifications, contradictions and unfinished needs.

It also accepts renewed questions and changed context. Reuse therefore includes its integration, persistence and continuation behavior.

The fact that this component currently lives inside the Fixcomm Wild package does not make its operation exclusive to outgoing communication. The actual input and output duties establish its fit.

The exact packaging choice remains open. An inbound workflow could invoke a bundled copy or consume an actual task-owned investigation using the same discipline. Either arrangement needs a clear way to request further context and receive usable evidence.

#### 3. The same overall formula fits

The proposed journey is:

1. **Establish the communication's purpose and available work.** What contribution is being communicated, what prompted it, and what must remain open?
2. **Use Unplugged Surfacing to establish the context basis.** Supply the current task, source environment and any existing account. Retain applicable evidence and investigate uncovered or changed scope.
3. **Have an actual candidate communication to review.** Use the existing draft, or compose a first supported message and its proposed Supporting Context from the work.
4. **Prepare and execute an inbound-specific dynamic critique.** Use the current context and applicable references to tailor the review. Save findings and proposed remedies.
5. **Apply supported corrections separately and inspect the resulting delivery.** Check the main message, supporting explanations, references and important qualifications together.

A material evidence need discovered during critique or revision returns to the context-discovery operation. Once the evidence has been integrated, the communication workflow revisits the parts whose basis changed.

These are responsibilities and dependencies. They do not require five agents, five new files or a newly named component for each responsibility. The same AI can perform them sequentially.

The third responsibility matters because the [existing Fixcomm entrance](../../artifacts/skills/fixcomm/SKILL.md) and [Fixcomm Wild entrance](../../artifacts/skills/fixcomm-wild/SKILL.md) operate on drafts. A worker explaining fresh work may not have written its first message yet. The future inbound workflow must account for that starting condition without treating context gathering as permission to complete missing substantive work.

#### 4. Reuse must be based on what the context actually covers

“Use Unplugged Surfacing” does not mean repeat every search for every message.

The communication owner supplies the current purpose and relevant work. The discovery coordinator compares the current questions with:

- What earlier inquiries actually investigated.
- The source contents that are available and support the answer.
- The time, version and scope to which those contents apply.
- Questions still unanswered and source opportunities not yet served.

The coordinator retains applicable coverage, investigates changed or uncovered scope, and reports consequential work it cannot complete within the bounds.

This comparison is already part of the [coordinator's continuation and resume instructions](/Users/nsstorm/Desktop/projects/AIOpsMan/artifacts/skills/unplugged-surfacing/SKILL.md:138). A familiar topic, an existing context file or a fluent draft cannot substitute for it.

For example, earlier work may have established how an experiment was configured. A new communication may need to explain whether its result supports a wider conclusion. The configuration facts remain useful, but they do not establish that the result or its limitations were investigated.

Conversely, if the current questions are already answered by available evidence with appropriate coverage, another external search may add nothing. Zero additional retrieval can be the result of the comparison.

The workflow need not know every contextual question before discovery starts. Initial readings can reveal missing identities, earlier decisions or unexpected relationships. The starting questions remain provisional within the actual task.

#### 5. Preserve the breadth that makes Unplugged Surfacing useful

The integration must retain both of the coordinator's discovery movements:

- **Independent inquiry:** investigate task questions in the available context sources within the task's work environment, including sources with no direct link from the starting message.
- **Linked inquiry:** follow meaningful references and relationships discovered in the material.

Following a link to one record does not establish that other relevant records in that source were considered.

Depth can vary with what missing context might change. The investigation remains finite, and it preserves unserved or unavailable source opportunities when it returns. A fixed pass count or an exhausted allowance does not establish completeness.

The task defines the source territory. A request to explain a supplied local result can be grounded in local material. A short claim about the readiness of wider company work can require a broader investigation. Message length alone cannot make that judgment, and access to unrelated accounts does not bring them into scope.

Every actual ordinary Surfacing invocation retains its full instruction reads and execution duties. Using the component's name in a prompt is insufficient by itself.

#### 6. The inbound review has a different purpose

For Orchestrator Inbound Communication, the review asks whether the recipient can understand and assess this particular contribution.

Depending on the task, that may require explaining:

- What the worker actually established or produced.
- Why that result matters to the work.
- Which objects, relationships or earlier decisions make it intelligible.
- What the evidence supports and what remains uncertain.
- Where the recipient can find further explanation without reconstructing it from raw records.

These are possible communication needs, not compulsory sections for every message. A technical explanation, exploratory discussion and operational update can require different treatment even when all three go to the same orchestrator.

The [current comm-critic-d entrance](../../artifacts/skills/comm-critic-d/SKILL.md) explicitly excludes operator-specific reporting and delivery orchestration. Renaming its recipient would leave the inbound purpose under-specified. Its useful general principles and dynamic-review mechanism can inform the new review, while their applicability must be assessed.

The critic still diagnoses and proposes. It does not apply its own remedies, decide the underlying work or manufacture an answer to make the message appear complete.

#### 7. Acquired context and Supporting Context serve different readers

Unplugged Surfacing's working account helps the AI understand and manage an investigation. Supporting Context helps the orchestrator understand the delivered contribution.

The working account may contain assignment status, source coverage and unresolved investigation paths. These can matter to the review without all belonging in the recipient's explanation.

Supporting Context should answer useful contextual questions in a coherent form. The [existing supporting-context design](historical%20finding%201.md#finding-09-numbered-supporting-context-with-optional-depth) already provides the general arrangement: a meaningful main message, local numbered references to supporting explanations, and optional deeper material where it helps.

The main message must remain faithful at its own level. An important qualification cannot be buried in a deeper file if its absence makes the main claim misleading.

Suitable existing explanations can be reused. The review should check their purpose, scope, state and actual accessibility. Different responsibilities do not impose duplicate documents or a new appendix for every small exchange.

For a future AI orchestrator, a file link alone is not enough: the receiving session must actually be able to access the explanation. The exact transport and selective-loading mechanism remains future implementation work.

#### 8. A missing explanation and a missing answer lead to different work

Three situations need different treatment:

| Situation | Appropriate response |
|---|---|
| The worker has a supported understanding but the message does not explain it | Propose a communication correction using that basis. |
| Necessary evidence or context is missing | Return a concrete inquiry through the discovery owner; integrate its result before relying on it. |
| The task deliberately leaves an issue open | Preserve the openness and explain its provisional state when that matters. |

A critic can identify a necessary question without knowing its answer. The answer must not be invented during revision.

If a vital answer may exist in an inaccessible tool or with a person, preserve the evidenced recovery lead and explain what remains unavailable. The AI's missing context is not evidence that the team lacks the information. A person's involvement is a possible route to the answer, not proof that they possess it.

The user or authorized workflow can then decide how to pursue that lead. Discovery does not independently contact people.

A qualified partial update can be a valid contribution when the task calls for one. It cannot silently replace a requested final recommendation or completed result.

#### 9. Keep the evidence basis stable through review

A review must use actual evidence and its decisive qualifications. A source title, search trace or completion notification does not contain the meaning needed to assess a claim.

The existing coordinator already issues an identified context handoff. Fixcomm Wild already requires the caller to read the returned account and necessary contents.

The inbound workflow should retain those duties. If new evidence arrives later, the discovery owner integrates it and issues an updated basis. The communication owner then reconsiders the affected critique and delivery.

This preserves a clear relationship between the evidence, the findings and the revised message. It also permits renewal without a compulsory restart of all completed work.

#### 10. What this establishes about Context Accumulation

The [earlier Context Accumulation inquiry](historical%20finding%201.md#finding-06-unplugged-surfacing-already-implements-substantial-context-accumulation) found that Unplugged Surfacing already provides substantial task-level accumulation: it integrates, retains and refreshes understanding.

Using that capability for inbound communication strengthens the practical connection between Context Accumulation and Communication in Craft. It avoids inventing a second context mechanism solely because the recipient changed.

It does not settle how unrelated tasks discover old accounts, how shared understanding is maintained across many consumers, or who notifies them when a fact changes. Those wider coordination duties remain open.

#### 11. The advantage of the composition

The proposed arrangement gives the future workflow several concrete advantages:

- **It can challenge omissions in the draft.** Discovery starts from the task and work, so the draft does not determine everything the critic can know.
- **It preserves a reusable context basis.** Later messages can draw on prior investigation while keeping uncovered and changed questions visible.
- **It gives new evidence needs a defined destination.** The critic can identify a problem without silently becoming a separate, untracked investigator.
- **It keeps findings inspectable.** The critique artifact remains available for understanding misses and improving applicable references.
- **It separates evidence from explanation.** The workflow must still make the contribution understandable to the orchestrator.
- **It supports proportional effort.** Existing evidence and suitable supporting explanations can be reused after assessing their fit.

These are supported design advantages. The inquiry inspected authored instructions and tested conceptual counterexamples; it did not measure retrieval accuracy or recipient comprehension in live use.

### Reasoning

#### Why the complete composition survived

The general context → critique → supported revision pattern survived because each operation addresses a different source of failure. Task context supplies the basis; dynamic critique diagnoses the communication; supported revision changes the delivery.

The focused inbound composition survived because the coordinator already accepts the right kinds of inputs, while the recipient-specific review supplies obligations absent from the outgoing critic.

Purpose-aware comparison, actual evidence reuse, one discovery owner and controlled renewal survived because they make existing work useful without treating familiarity as completeness. A task-owned provider is also viable when it actually returns evidence and accepts further inquiries.

Conditional first composition survived because fresh worker contributions need a concrete target for review. It does not require a new standalone authoring skill.

Different human, AI and embedded-outgoing audiences survived within the same structure. Their explanation and access needs differ; the underlying evidence can still be shared.

Proportionate supporting-context reuse, main-claim qualifications and separation of missing evidence from missing explanation survived the final stress cases. They avoid both unexplained summaries and unnecessary duplicate documents.

The editorial and museum comparisons helped explain these roles. They remain illustrations, not independent evidence of reliable execution. Future shared context across separate sessions remains a research direction rather than an entry condition.

#### Alternatives rejected across the three passes

- **Copy the outgoing skill and rename the recipient.** The existing critic's scope explicitly excludes operator reporting, and the copy would not supply the new contribution-and-support duties.
- **Never acquire context within or for inbound communication.** A supplied account can omit decisive material. An actual external context provider is viable; having no accountable recovery route is not.
- **Always forward the working context account as Supporting Context.** That account may contain correct evidence while leaving the contribution or its significance unexplained. Suitable passages can still be reused.
- **Repeat the whole investigation for every message.** This discards valid coverage and can spend the allowance on unchanged work while missing a new question.
- **Let the critic independently gather evidence, settle the underlying task and apply remedies.** This obscures which evidence supports the report and crosses the agreed critique boundary. Specific questions can instead return through the workflow.
- **Require a universal memory platform first.** A grounded task can support useful inbound communication without solving all cross-task continuity.
- **Require a complete, closed question list before discovery.** The readings may be what reveal a missing identity, relationship or earlier correction. The task bounds question growth without pretending the initial list is complete.
- **Eliminate the communication review because context integration already explains everything.** Acquired understanding can still be badly communicated. Direct content reuse remains possible after assessing its fit.

The first pass exposed a material weakness in the initial proposal: “reuse context when sufficient” did not explain who determines sufficiency or how. The second pass located that judgment in the comparison between the current purpose and actual investigation, with the communication owner consuming the result.

The final pass tested local tasks, consequential short claims, initial composition, mixed audiences, lost evidence, changed sources, adequate old support, partial returns and future AI access. The same composition handled those cases without another required subsystem.

## Finding 13: How the inbound critic should justify context gaps and remedies

**Original:** [finding.md](../../research/inquiries/2026-09-27_22-44__inbound-critic-justified-context-gaps/finding.md)

**Inquiry:** 2026-09-27_22-44__inbound-critic-justified-context-gaps

**Compilation edits:** Omitted one evidence-table row citing company-specific feedback; retained the general critique findings and their other evidence.

### Question

How should the inbound communication critic use the task and available context to determine what the orchestrator needs explained, identify justified communication gaps, and propose remedies without adding unnecessary background or forcing intended openness to become settled?

The orchestrator is the person or future AI coordinating the work. The proposed critic examines a worker's contribution to that recipient and produces findings and mitigation proposals. Applying those proposals remains a separate process.

The goal here is to establish the critic's judgment and general thinking process before authoring its exact prompt or reference set.

### Finding Summary

- **The critic should compare the understanding this exchange requires with the meaning the recipient actually receives.** The task and underlying work supply grounds beyond the draft's own selection.
- **A possible contextual question is not yet a finding.** The critic must explain why the understanding matters here and identify the actual omission, distortion or reading difficulty.
- **Actively check whether the communication is already adequate.** Immediate shared context or a useful supporting explanation may satisfy the need without new text.
- **Uncertainty limits a finding's assertion; it does not create the justification.** A concrete reliance on unestablished context can matter. Generic speculation that the reader might not know something is insufficient.
- **Keep required understanding, knowledge of the answer and intended openness distinct.** A critic can identify an unanswered requirement without inventing its answer or demanding a decision the task leaves open.
- **Judge proposed remedies by the same facts, purpose and openness as the original communication.** A valid finding can have an invalid mitigation.
- **Dynamic preparation should produce tailored questions and instructions. Execution must remain able to reject, refine or extend those questions.**
- The existing findings-and-mitigations report can carry this reasoning. A new numerical score, mandatory coverage ledger or quota of findings is unnecessary.

### Finding

#### 1. Start from the actual contribution and exchange

The original problem was that the AI wrote from inside its detailed working context. The user then had to recover what its terms referred to, how objects related and why a point mattered before assessing the answer.

The [original problem framing](../../research/analyses/nonambiguity-problem-framing.md) makes this more specific than a request for simpler language. An explanation can use ordinary words and correct names while omitting the connection that makes the point understandable.

The critic therefore needs the actual communication task, the contribution being explained, applicable instructions and relevant work context.

The recipient's title does not determine the required answer. The same orchestrator can ask for a file inventory, a technical explanation, a progress account or an exploration. A useful response to one can be incomplete or distracting for another.

The larger project's eventual needs also do not automatically become requirements for this exchange. A project may eventually need a final decision while the present task deliberately explores alternatives.

If the purpose remains materially ambiguous, the critic should preserve that uncertainty in its judgment. It should not select whichever purpose makes its preferred critique easiest to justify.

#### 2. Raise questions from both the work and the message

The [earlier omitted-context inquiry](#finding-11-discovering-the-understanding-a-communication-depends-on) established two complementary directions of examination.

**From the task and work toward the delivery:** what needs to become understandable, given the actual request and what the work establishes? This can reveal a required answer that the draft never mentions.

**From the message toward its grounds:** what does this claim, comparison, reference or proposed action depend on? This can reveal an unexplained relationship, hidden qualification or unsupported implication.

Names and entities provide starting points. They do not define the boundary of the review. Two objects may be named correctly while their relationship remains missing.

Reference check items help the critic ask these questions. They do not establish a problem merely by applying to the general topic. Their conditions and limits must survive translation into a tailored prompt.

A worker's supplied context account is useful evidence, but calling it complete cannot establish that it contains every omitted requirement. The critic needs enough actual task and source substance to challenge the draft's selection.

#### 3. Distinguish necessary understanding from related background

Context is necessary when it enables the recipient to follow, assess or use this particular contribution for the current purpose.

The reviewer should be able to explain the connection:

> What point, relationship, conclusion or requested answer becomes difficult to understand or assess without this explanation?

This can establish a need even when no immediate action follows. Understanding an emerging concept or the relationship between two systems is a legitimate communication purpose.

A possible mistake or consequence can help make the need concrete. It should not become an invented harm story used to justify optional background.

A fact may be relevant to the project without being needed in this communication. The history of a tool, every intermediate attempt or an unrelated dependency should not be added solely because discovery found it.

The [orchestrator-needs finding](historical%20finding%201.md#finding-10-what-the-orchestrator-needs-to-understand-from-a-workers-communication) supplies the guiding distinction: needed understanding, what is already available to the recipient, and what must be newly explained are separate questions.

#### 4. Test a candidate before turning it into a finding

A candidate concern deserves examination. A retained finding needs a reasoned basis.

The critic should establish:

1. **What understanding is needed and why.** Connect it to the actual request, an applicable communication requirement or the meaning of the present contribution.
2. **What the delivery does with that understanding.** Identify the passage, missing answer, misleading connection or specific reliance on context.
3. **Whether relevant existing context already resolves the concern.** Inspect the actual shared exchange and pertinent Supporting Context.
4. **What difficulty or risk remains.** Explain the consequence at the certainty supported by the available material.

These are thinking obligations. They do not require four new report fields or a separate artifact for every candidate.

The third obligation is especially useful: **test the strongest supported reading under which the communication is already adequate.**

That means looking for a real explanation in the exchange, a usable supporting entry, or a convention legitimately established by the task. It does not mean imagining an exceptionally knowledgeable recipient who can fill every gap.

If the concern disappears under that reading, the critic should drop it. This protects adequate concise communication and avoids duplicate background.

An alternative “ideal answer” can suggest questions, but differences from it are not themselves defects. The alternative may contain extra scope, a preferred style or invented requirements.

#### 5. Handle uncertain reader knowledge without guessing

The critic rarely knows exactly what a recipient currently remembers. That does not make contextual review impossible.

A communication can visibly rely on task-private shorthand, an unintroduced relationship or unexplained history. If the actual delivery is expected to restore that context, the critic can identify the reliance without claiming that the recipient has forgotten.

For example, a worker preparing a return-to-work explanation might refer to “the five” without identifying the objects or the relevant action anywhere in the delivered account. The finding can name that omitted meaning and why it matters to the explanation. It need not diagnose the recipient's memory.

The opposite error is to say “the reader might not know this” about every conventional term. Without a specific task or delivery basis, that possibility does not justify a risk finding.

Access follows the same distinction. An essential explanation offered only through a worker-local file to a separate recipient presents a concrete delivery reliance worth examining. If accessibility is unestablished, the critic should say precisely that; it cannot claim confirmed access failure.

Merely failing to test the permissions of every normal shared reference does not automatically create a communication risk.

The useful rule is:

> **First establish why the concern matters in this communication. Then qualify the particular condition that remains uncertain.**

This does not resolve every future method for modelling recipient knowledge. It establishes how the critic can remain useful and honest while that knowledge is incomplete.

#### 6. Keep the need, its answer and intended openness separate

The task can establish that an assessment is required before the reviewer knows the correct assessment.

If a worker's draft replaces the requested assessment with a list of activities, the critic can identify the missing answer. It should not invent the assessment to complete its mitigation.

An adequate explanation of why the assessment cannot yet be supplied may already communicate the actual state faithfully. That does not mean the underlying work is complete, and unfinished work alone should not automatically become another communication defect.

Intended openness is another question. An exploratory task may deliberately leave a selection, definition or estimate provisional even when substantial facts are known.

The critic should preserve that specific openness while continuing to assess surrounding meaning. It can flag an unclear subject, contradictory factual statement or wording that falsely presents a choice as settled.

The [Meaning and fidelity guide](../../artifacts/skills/comm-critic-d/references/meaning-and-fidelity.md) already captures this boundary in its intended-openness check. The task establishes what should remain open; a vague passage does not prove that all its ambiguity was intended.

When intent itself is uncertain and the distinction would materially change the critique, the judgment should remain qualified. The critic should not quietly transform exploration into decision-making.

#### 7. Examine the complete delivery and its reading arrangement

The recipient may receive a main message, a supporting explanation, a longer document or a combination. Private worker notes are not automatically part of that delivery.

The [Supporting Context foundation](historical%20finding%201.md#finding-09-numbered-supporting-context-with-optional-depth) gives the existing arrangement: a meaningful main account, numbered connections to useful explanations, and optional deeper material.

The critic should recognise when a precise, accessible supporting entry already supplies the needed explanation. Necessary understanding does not always require another paragraph in the main message.

However, optional support cannot be the only place that makes a misleading main claim accurate. If the main message implies general readiness while the evidence concerns one trial, the scope of that claim needs correction at the point where it matters.

Longer communication also needs both local and whole-account attention. Read coherent sections with the context needed to understand them, then inspect their connections and combined implication.

A section may legitimately raise a question that the next section answers. It should not receive a final missing-context finding before the relevant whole has been considered.

Conversely, every individual section can contain true statements while the conclusion implies something stronger than the evidence. Factual verification alone would miss that communication problem.

Mixed audiences require care. An outgoing draft shown to the orchestrator retains its eventual audience; the surrounding explanation serves the orchestrator. The critic should not automatically insert private editorial notes or inbound supporting markers into the outgoing text.

#### 8. Propose the change that would resolve the actual difficulty

A mitigation should explain what the recipient would understand differently after the proposed change.

Depending on the problem, a sufficient remedy may be:

- Naming the particular object or action.
- Explaining a necessary relationship.
- Qualifying a claim's scope, evidence or status.
- Bringing an existing explanation closer to where it is needed.
- Pointing directly to a useful supporting passage.
- Removing an unsupported implication or distracting detour.
- Identifying the specific substance that must be obtained or developed before a stronger answer is possible.

More background is one possible remedy, not the default result of every finding.

“Proportionate” means enough to resolve the problem while preserving the task and its constraints. It does not mean the fewest possible words. Removing a necessary qualification to meet an invented brevity target would fail the same review.

Actual length and medium constraints still matter. The critic should seek a faithful formulation within them. If the requirements cannot jointly be met, it should expose the specific conflict rather than hide it by overstating the result.

A proposal must be checked independently of the diagnosis. A valid concern about an unclear plan does not justify inventing an owner or deadline. A valid concern about unexplained evidence does not justify claiming that additional tests were performed.

The critic's endpoint remains the proposal. Applying it, contacting people, performing new substantive work or changing references belongs elsewhere.

#### 9. Make the dynamic prompt an examination brief

The previous [Unplugged Surfacing integration finding](#finding-12-use-unplugged-surfacing-to-ground-orchestrator-inbound-communication) established the surrounding pattern: context discovery and renewal, inbound-specific critique, then supported revision.

Within that arrangement, dynamic preparation should use the current task, contribution, available context and applicable references to produce questions suited to the target.

The generated prompt needs to retain the grounds for those questions and the relevant conditions. For example, an ownership check should not become a demand to assign an owner when the present communication intentionally leaves responsibility open.

Preparation should not precompute a closed list of defects and make execution merely confirm them.

Execution examines the actual target and support. It can reject a candidate because the explanation is already present, refine it because the uncertainty concerns a different matter, or discover an omitted question that preparation missed.

A genuine new evidence need should return through the established discovery owner. When the basis changes, the affected findings and proposals need reconsideration. The critic should not independently build a second untracked investigation.

These are general responsibilities. Exact prompt sections, naming and packaging remain later authoring decisions.

#### 10. Keep the report inspectable and proportionate

The existing [comm-critic-d entrance](../../artifacts/skills/comm-critic-d/SKILL.md) already provides a report with Risk, Category, Severity, Impact, Context, Affected areas and Mitigation.

Those fields can carry the necessary grounds without adding a compulsory score or a parallel checklist:

- The risk and affected area explain the actual missing or misleading meaning.
- The impact explains why that matters to this recipient and task.
- The context supplies what is necessary to understand the finding.
- The mitigation explains a supported remedy and its boundaries.

Severity follows the actual consequence for the exchange. A communication problem need not involve a harmful action to matter, and an uncertain issue should not automatically receive high severity.

Several applicable checks can support one finding. Repeated symptoms can be combined when they have the same actual problem and remedy. Distinct causes or required changes should remain distinguishable.

If no supported concerns remain after review, a report with no findings is legitimate. It should not manufacture risks to demonstrate activity.

This preserves the user's preferred scope: criticism and proposals, followed by improvement of references through experience. It does not introduce a separate approval process or declare perfect detection.

#### 11. What this adds to our understanding of the skill

We already understood that the critic needs task context, recipient awareness and principles. This inquiry makes the judgment connecting them more concrete.

The critic must be able to justify both its diagnosis and its proposed intervention. Its references help it notice concerns, while the actual task, evidence and delivery determine which concerns survive.

That gives later skill authoring a clearer foundation than “add enough context”:

> Establish what this contribution needs to make understandable. Raise questions from the task and actual work as well as the draft. Examine how the intended delivery supplies that understanding. Test whether relevant shared or supporting context already resolves each concern. Retain grounded problems, preserve specific uncertainty, and propose sufficient changes that remain faithful to the facts and intended openness.

The method still depends on judgment. Its value is making that judgment more explicit and improvable; effectiveness must be learned from actual use.

## Finding 14: Discovering what deserves a supporting-context reference

**Original:** [finding.md](../../research/inquiries/2026-09-28_10-00__supporting-context-reference-discovery/finding.md)

**Inquiry:** 2026-09-28_10-00__supporting-context-reference-discovery

**Compilation edits:** No content edits; only metadata, heading levels and navigation links were adjusted.

### Question

When an AI hands a communication to the orchestrator, how should it discover what deserves a supporting-context reference, including several things within one sentence or paragraph?

The orchestrator is the person or future AI coordinating the work. The user wants self-contained understanding across the delivered documentation, with generous supporting context available for inspection. Missing useful context is considered worse than providing extra context the recipient may choose to skip.

This inquiry concerns discovering and attaching those references. The separate question of how to group the resulting explanations into entries remains deferred.

### Finding Summary

- **Use generous coverage as the default.** A useful reference does not require an existing communication defect or proof that the recipient will be confused.
- **Look for distinct contextual meanings, rather than one reference per sentence or one entry per noun.** A result, the objects involved and the relationship between them can each deserve support.
- A supporting reference connects a particular expression or claim to material that identifies, explains, substantiates or qualifies it.
- **Discover before compressing or grouping.** Preserve the contextual questions the text raises before deciding which explanations might share an entry.
- Detection should examine expressions, claims and relationships, compare the message with the actual task and available work, and inspect the supporting explanations for further missing context.
- Place the number beside the phrase or claim it actually supplements. Multiple numbers in one sentence and repeated pointers to the same explanation are legitimate.
- **More references need not mean more duplicated writing.** An existing explanation can be reused when its meaning, state and accessibility fit.
- Supporting material can explain an unknown or deliberately open matter. It must not invent an answer to make the documentation look complete.
- Self-contained understanding means the delivered account supplies its meaning without requiring private chat memory or reconstruction from raw sources. Useful deeper material can remain available after the first explanation is sufficient.

### Finding

#### 1. The goal is to make the AI's contextual understanding available

The communication problem is broader than unclear wording. The AI may have a detailed understanding of the work while the orchestrator sees only its compressed message.

The orchestrator might recognize a phrase such as “evaluation results” without knowing which evaluation, which result set or which limitations the AI has in mind. The sentence can sound clear while its underlying meaning remains difficult to recover.

The [original problem framing](../../research/analyses/nonambiguity-problem-framing.md) describes this difference between remembering the direction of work and holding its detailed context.

Your current instruction establishes a stronger delivery preference: make useful surrounding understanding available generously, so you can choose what to inspect.

That changes how references should be considered. The AI should not first reduce the communication to the smallest set of indispensable explanations. A clear summary can still benefit from references to the precise objects, detailed results, relevant history and supporting reasoning behind it.

This extends the reference-discovery aspect of the [numbered Supporting Context foundation](historical%20finding%201.md#finding-09-numbered-supporting-context-with-optional-depth). Its main-message, supporting-entry and optional-depth arrangement remains applicable.

#### 2. What a supporting reference connects

A supporting reference is an association between:

- A particular expression, claim or passage in the communication.
- The contextual meaning the reader can inspect through it.
- A usable destination supplying that meaning.

The number is the compact address for this association.

The destination may identify the actual thing being discussed, explain its role, show how things relate, describe relevant history, provide evidence, explain a judgment or expose a limitation.

These roles can coexist. A note about an evaluation result might identify the evaluation and explain its outcome, then point to the actual result records.

The reference must not imply more than its destination provides. A document identifying which workflows were tested does not automatically establish what the results prove about other workflows.

The [earlier inquiry into omitted context](#finding-11-discovering-the-understanding-a-communication-depends-on) remains relevant: named objects are only starting points. Relationships, assumptions and missing requested answers can matter just as much.

#### 3. The results and the tested workflows can both have references

Consider a hypothetical worker AI reporting an evaluation to the orchestrator:

> We obtained evaluation results[1] from two tested workflows[2]. Their applicability beyond those workflows remains unestablished[3].

The numbers in this illustration represent different supporting meanings:

| Marked meaning | What the supporting material could make inspectable |
|---|---|
| **[1] Evaluation results** | Which evaluation and run; which models or approaches; what was measured; the actual outcomes; relevant result records and limitations. |
| **[2] Two tested workflows** | Which workflows; what they do; their role in this evaluation; the relevant test setup; any recorded reason for choosing them. |
| **[3] Applicability beyond those workflows** | What the evidence covers, what has not been established, and why the wider conclusion remains open. |

This is an illustration of reference scope, not an assertion that a real evaluation occurred or a mandatory three-entry template.

The second reference does not make the first unnecessary. Understanding the tested workflows cannot substitute for identifying the results.

The third reference illustrates something else: a relationship or qualification can deserve support even when both objects already have references.

More opportunities may appear in the actual material. A date, comparison criterion, model version or particular testing method could have its own useful association. Nothing in this example caps the number of references.

The discovery operation should preserve these meanings independently. Whether some of their explanations later share an entry is the separate organization question you postponed.

#### 4. How the AI should detect the opportunities

A general instruction such as “add useful references” leaves too much to a single judgment. The inquiry supports several deliberate movements of attention.

They describe work the AI should do. They do not require a separate agent, file or rigid fixed-count pass for each movement.

##### Read the expressions for what they mean here

Read a coherent passage and notice expressions that carry particular meaning: names, objects, concepts, records, quantities, dates, versions, actions, pronouns and shorthand.

Ask questions such as:

- Which exact thing or set is meant?
- What does this term mean in this use?
- What does this object represent or do here?
- Which event, action, version or earlier result is being referred to?

Familiar vocabulary is not a reason to skip this inspection. “Friday” is familiar, but a handover may still need to identify the date. “Two workflows” needs no explanation of the number two, but it may need to identify the members of the set.

A term can have both a general meaning and a particular instance. Explaining the general concept of evaluation does not identify the evaluation results the message is discussing.

##### Examine the claims and relationships

Read what the passage says about those things.

Look for comparisons, causes, reasons, criteria, dependencies, conditions, changes, status and uncertainty. Words such as “better,” “ready,” “because,” “previous,” “selected” and “only” can point to substantial context.

Useful questions include:

- Better by which criterion, and compared with what?
- What makes this conclusion follow from the evidence?
- How do these objects relate?
- Why is this example or earlier decision relevant here?
- What has actually been completed?
- Which conditions limit the statement?
- Is this choice settled, proposed or deliberately open?

These questions guide attention. They are not a closed taxonomy or a requirement to add every possible field to every message.

For example, noticing the phrase “the selected approach” should prompt checking whether a selection actually happened. It should not prompt the AI to make that selection.

##### Compare the draft with the task and available work

A draft cannot reveal everything it omitted merely through the words it contains.

The AI should also consider the actual request, relevant work, source material and prior corrections. It should ask what contextual understanding from that material is absent from, or hard to reach through, the delivery.

This comparison has two jobs:

- Discover required meaning the draft never represents.
- Preserve useful available detail that the compressed message leaves implicit.

The second job matters under your generous-context preference. Detailed outcomes or relevant history can deserve accessible support even when they do not change the main conclusion.

When an entire requested subject is absent, the critic may need to propose representing it in the communication before a reference can be attached. Adding numbers only to existing nouns would miss that problem.

##### Inspect what the supporting explanation itself assumes

A supporting note can move the original problem elsewhere.

If the note explains a result using an unexplained internal term, the reader still lacks the context. The AI should identify that new dependency and explain it or provide a usable reference.

The test is whether the explanation actually answers its contextual question. A successful link or an informative title is insufficient.

Once the explanation supplies that understanding, further useful detail can remain optional. Completing the first explanation does not mean removing its deeper references.

##### Check the account as a whole

Finally, inspect the associations together with the main message.

Individually accurate notes can accompany an unsupported overall conclusion. Repeated names can silently refer to different versions. Several clear sentences can leave their relationship unexplained.

For a longer document, examine coherent sections with their context, then examine the complete account. A whole-document skim cannot replace the local work.

#### 5. The inclusion rule should favor useful context

The central question is:

> What could the orchestrator identify, understand or inspect about this communication through the proposed support?

A concrete answer supplies a reason to keep the association.

The support may concern identity, role, method, history, rationale, evidence, alternatives, limitations or other contextual understanding. Its usefulness need not depend on preventing a serious error or changing an immediate decision.

Where a contextual connection is plausible but not yet clear, retain it for examination rather than silently discard it. Grounding still matters: the AI must not invent the connection or its answer.

This is different from a general preference for maximum visible numbers. A number that opens no meaningful explanation gives the reader little to inspect.

Likewise, an unrelated document does not become useful support merely because it shares a word or appears beside a relevant source. If the AI can establish how that document explains the actual contribution, it belongs back in consideration.

Distance and age are not exclusion rules. An old decision can explain a current constraint. A separate workstream can explain a present dependency.

The preference is therefore broad available contextual coverage, including optional depth. It does not require a universal glossary or an unprocessed copy of the whole workspace.

#### 6. Where the number should go

Place the marker beside the expression or claim its destination actually supplements.

An object reference can follow the identifying phrase. A reference about reasoning or scope can follow the relevant claim. A paragraph-wide explanation may attach to the passage it explains.

The association should preserve decisive qualifiers. A note about a provisional result should not appear to support an unqualified claim of completion.

Several markers within one sentence are acceptable when the addressed meanings differ. They need not be forced into a single trailing number.

Some meanings overlap. A model's identity and the comparison involving that model may each deserve support. Their notes should make the different purposes apparent.

This is a scope requirement rather than a final typography decision. The exact visual style of the number can be chosen for the delivery medium later.

#### 7. More access does not require duplicate explanations

The same explanation may be useful in several places.

When its meaning and state still fit, the communication can repeat the same local reference number. A later section should not lose useful access merely because the object appeared earlier.

An existing supporting document may also supply the explanation. Reusing it requires inspecting the relevant content and its fit, rather than relying on its title.

Reuse must preserve identity and state. A document describing last week's evaluation might not explain this week's result, even when both have the same project name.

These principles permit generous references while keeping explanations consistent. They do not require a global identifier registry or a new document for each occurrence.

#### 8. What self-contained understanding requires

Self-contained understanding can be distributed across a connected set of delivered documents.

The main communication gives a meaningful account of the contribution. Supporting entries explain what their markers refer to. Deeper material provides further detail or evidence the recipient can choose to inspect.

The recipient should not need the worker's private chat memory to identify the subjects or reconstruct the central explanation.

A source link may provide access to evidence without providing the explanation. If the recipient must search through a long source and assemble the point themselves, the handover may still be incomplete.

The main message must also preserve qualifications that affect its claim. A deep note cannot be the only place revealing that “ready” means a completed design rather than an implemented process.

The [orchestrator-needs finding](historical%20finding%201.md#finding-10-what-the-orchestrator-needs-to-understand-from-a-workers-communication) distinguishes necessary understanding, what is already available and what needs to be newly explained. Under the current handover requirement, an earlier explanation can satisfy a need through usable access; merely assuming the recipient remembers it cannot.

This is not a promise of offline independence. If the delivery relies on an external source or a file in another session, actual recipient access still matters.

#### 9. Unknown and deliberately open matters still deserve context

Discovering a contextual question does not establish its answer.

The AI may know which evaluation is meant but lack the recorded reason for selecting its workflows. The missing reason should not be invented to complete a note.

Support can explain what is known, what remains unestablished and any evidenced route to recovering it. If the task deliberately leaves a choice open, the note can explain that choice and its current alternatives without settling it.

A note describing an unresolved state can be a complete explanation of that state. It does not make the underlying question or work complete.

The same distinction governs stopping. If an explanation still depends on an unintroduced contextual meaning, the AI should continue or preserve the specific limit. If the question is answered in the delivered material, it need not recursively explain every ordinary word used in the answer.

Useful deeper access remains available after that point. A finite retrieval allowance bounds the work performed; it does not prove that all possible context was found.

#### 10. How this fits the inbound critic

The [justified-gap finding](#finding-13-how-the-inbound-critic-should-justify-context-gaps-and-remedies) established how the critic should justify a communication problem. This inquiry adds a distinct requirement for discovering supporting-reference opportunities.

During preparation, the AI can consider contextual questions broadly, including opportunities for useful optional access. A candidate question does not have to be a defect before it can inform the supporting material.

During critique, the explicit requirement for a generously referenced, self-contained handover becomes part of the standard. Missing useful associations can therefore matter even when the main sentence is grammatically clear.

The critic still needs to explain what is missing or misleading and how its proposal helps. It should not create one risk record for every noun or every successfully supplied reference.

The critic remains proposal-only. Writing or revising the communication and its Supporting Context belongs to the later correction or authoring stage.

This inquiry establishes the detection and association logic. It leaves final entry grouping for the next discussion, as requested.

### Reasoning

The main surviving approach combines broad discovery with truthful contextual associations.

Broad discovery serves the user's preference against missing context. Exact target meaning and visible attachment make that breadth useful. Reading the task and work catches omissions beyond the draft; reading the support catches ambiguities displaced into the notes.

The first pass left its stopping judgment too vague. The second clarified that an explanation can answer its immediate question while retaining useful optional depth. The third tested overlapping spans, changing identities, long passages, open concepts and hidden qualifications.

The alternatives considered explain why this combination is preferable:

- **One note for every word or noun:** raises the count while still missing relations and absent topics. Entity extraction remains a useful beginning.
- **A new target for every occurrence:** creates duplicate explanations that can diverge. Repeated fitting pointers preserve access more directly.
- **Only one reference per sentence:** can suppress a result association merely because the workflow association already exists.
- **Only the first mention receives a reference:** can leave later passages hard to recover and can hide a change in referent.
- **Put all context into the main message and use no references:** can work for a genuinely simple account, but defeats selectable depth for a substantial handover.
- **Wait for the recipient to request missing context:** requires the recipient to discover the omission and recreates the interruption problem.
- **Include only context that changes the immediate decision:** removes useful explanation and evidence inspection that the current task explicitly values.
- **Treat all available source links as sufficient support:** preserves raw access while potentially leaving the reader to assemble the meaning.
- **Follow every link or define every word recursively:** confuses topic reachability with contextual understanding and has no meaningful endpoint.
- **Require a final answer or external citation for every reference:** suppresses open questions and can encourage invented substance.
- **Treat reference count as proof of completeness:** does not reveal omitted relationships, inaccessible targets or misleading combined claims.

### Question

How should an AI show, choose and count the questions answered by each Supporting Context entry accompanying a message to the orchestrator?

The proposed normal structure is up to three tailored questions plus one history-and-connections question. The aim is to give the recipient enough understanding without requiring them to remember the worker AI's detailed context.

The orchestrator is the user now and may be a coordinating AI later. A supporting entry is the explanation reached through a numbered reference in the main message.

### Finding Summary

- **Use visible questions with answers directly underneath them.** The reader should be able to see what each entry explains.
- **Adopt three tailored questions plus one history/connections question as the normal upper target.** One or two tailored questions may be enough.
- **Choose the tailored questions from the task, underlying work and meaning of the reference.** They are not fixed identity/reason/next-step fields.
- **Count explanatory purposes.** Several facts can answer one question; several independent questions must not be hidden under one broad heading.
- **Make the fixed concern the subject's fit with earlier or related work.** The wording and answer should match the particular subject.
- **Preserve relevant context when an entry does not fit the normal shape.** Reorganize coherently before considering an explicit exception; do not silently omit meaning.
- **Keep discovery broader than the visible question count.** Four questions do not prove that all useful context was found.
- The structure is ready as a design direction. Its practical reliability and best question count still need evidence from use.

### Finding

#### 1. Visible questions make the explanation easier to inspect

The worker AI holds details of its investigation that the recipient may not remember or have seen. Supporting Context exists to make those details understandable at the point of handover.

A paragraph can explain something accurately while leaving its purpose hard to identify. A visible question makes that purpose explicit: which subject is being explained and what the answer should supply.

The question also helps a reader decide whether to read the answer now. Someone who already knows which workflows were tested may still want the answer about why they were selected.

This makes omissions easier to discuss later. The user can identify a missing question, an inadequate answer or a misleading assumption in the question itself. That is more concrete feedback than “this still lacks context.”

These are explanatory questions written into the documentation. They do not automatically become questions that the AI asks the user or sends to colleagues.

#### 2. Use three plus one as the normal arrangement

For an ordinary entry, use:

- Up to three questions tailored to what this reference needs to explain.
- One visible question about how its subject fits with earlier or related work.

Use fewer tailored questions when they already supply the explanation. Do not invent an extra rationale, implication or action merely to fill three positions.

In a simple historical reference, the history/connections question might itself supply the whole explanation. A second question is unnecessary if it would only repeat the answer.

The proposed count is a useful starting discipline. It encourages the author to consider whether an entry has become too broad. This inquiry found no evidence that exactly three tailored questions is optimal for every kind of work.

I therefore recommend a **normal upper target**, with a controlled response when it cannot preserve the explanation. An absolute limit would sometimes force an artificial split, a hidden compound question or an omission. Section 7 explains how to handle that case.

#### 3. Choose questions from the understanding the reference should provide

Before selecting the final questions, identify what the entry is meant to make understandable.

For an evaluation-scope reference, the purpose might be to explain which workflows the evidence covers and what that means for interpreting the result. For a file reference, it might simply identify the file and explain its role. For an emerging concept, it may explain the idea while preserving its provisional state.

The AI should consider both:

- The task and underlying work, including meaning the draft may have omitted.
- The actual expression, claim or relationship to which the reference is attached.

Then it should turn those needs into concrete reader-facing questions. Depending on the subject, a question may concern identity, a relationship, rationale, evidence, scope, status, significance or uncertainty. These are possibilities, not mandatory categories.

A question should name enough of its subject to be understandable. “Why?” is less useful than “Why were these workflows selected for this evaluation?”

A question must also avoid inventing its own premise. “Why did we approve this approach?” is wrong if the work only explored it. A more faithful question might ask what informed the proposal.

#### 4. Count one question by what its answer explains

One question has one recognizable explanatory purpose.

“Which workflows were tested?” can have an answer containing two workflow names, their relevant versions and enough description to distinguish them. Those are several facts answering one question.

“How do these results compare with the earlier trial?” may need several observations about two sets of results. It can still be one comparison question.

By contrast, “Which workflows were tested, who approved them, what did they cost and what happens next?” contains several independent questions even if written under one heading.

The practical test is to inspect the answer:

- Can it give a direct response to one clear question, with details that support that response?
- Or does it switch between separate explanatory purposes that deserve their own questions?

This is a judgment about meaning. Counting question marks alone cannot enforce it.

The same distinction applies to entries. Related questions may form one useful explanation. Separate explanations may concern the same object. Multiple entries may live in one Supporting Context file. The number of questions, entries, files and inline markers are different counts.

#### 5. Keep the history/connections question focused

A useful general formulation is:

> **How does this fit with earlier or related work?**

In the delivered entry, replace “this” with the actual subject when needed—for example, “How does this evaluation fit with earlier or related work?”

The answer might explain an earlier attempt, a decision that led here, a change from a previous position, or a current dependency. Include what helps situate the present subject.

The question does not require both a complete timeline and a list of every connection. Historical and current facts can jointly explain one relationship to existing work. Unrelated historical and dependency questions should not be packed into this heading.

Keep the question visible in the initial design. If the relevant connection is already explained elsewhere in the delivery, give a brief local orientation and a precise pointer instead of repeating the explanation.

If the available context does not establish earlier work, say that accurately. “The available notes do not establish an earlier trial” is different from “There was no earlier trial.” A claim about what was searched must reflect the search actually performed.

The fixed question does not authorize unlimited retrieval or contacting people. Context discovery supplies its evidence and known recovery leads through the existing process.

#### 6. Put usable answers beneath the questions

Each answer should supply its substance directly, followed by the details needed to understand it. A short answer is welcome when sufficient; a one-sentence limit would be arbitrary.

Retain the relevant evidence, source pointers and qualifications. They can sit beside the answer they support. The Q&A format does not require repeating a separate evidence/status/limits form under every heading.

An answer can truthfully explain what remains unknown or deliberately open. Making an entry understandable does not require finishing the underlying task.

The main message also retains its important qualifications. Optional Supporting Context should not be the only place that makes an otherwise misleading main claim accurate.

#### 7. Preserve context when the entry becomes overfull

When more than three tailored questions seem necessary, examine the arrangement before cutting anything:

1. **Remove duplicate asks.** Two headings may be asking for the same understanding.
2. **Keep supporting facts with their answer.** Several relevant details do not always need separate questions.
3. **Separate genuinely distinct purposes.** For example, evaluation scope and a detailed results comparison may each deserve a coherent entry.
4. **Use deeper explanation for useful detail.** Keep an adequate answer in the initial entry, then point precisely to the expansion.

A deeper pointer must not replace the whole explanation with “see the report.” The recipient should understand the answer and its important limits before deciding whether to read more.

If an additional distinct question must remain with the entry for the explanation to make sense, keep it visible as an exception to the normal arrangement. That is preferable to disguising several questions as one or losing context.

The exception should be recognizable during authoring or review. It does not require a new reporting field or a policy explanation in every message. Repeated exceptions are a reason to reconsider the count or entry boundaries during practical use.

#### 8. A concrete illustration using this inquiry

The following illustrates the entry form using facts from this design discussion.

#### 9. Keep the larger process intact

The previous [reference-discovery finding](#finding-14-discovering-what-deserves-a-supporting-context-reference) established broad examination of expressions, relationships, task/work requirements and dependencies within supporting explanations.

That discovery remains broader than the number of delivered questions. The AI must not stop looking simply because it has found four easy questions to answer.

A retained finding needs a reason grounded in the communication's task and actual delivery. The critic proposes remedies; applying them remains separate.

Question-based entries give that review something more inspectable. They do not establish that the AI noticed every possible gap.

#### 10. Reuse earlier explanations without rebuilding all history

An earlier explanation can still be useful even if it was written as prose rather than Q&A.

The current entry should make its question visible, orient the reader and point to the specific explanation that answers it. Verify that the earlier content still fits the current subject, scope and status.

Do not infer fit merely because both documents use the same topic name. A previous proposal and a current implementation may need different explanations.

Nothing in this entry structure requires reading every old Supporting Context file or creating a global registry. Broader discovery machinery becomes a separate question if actual reuse repeatedly fails.

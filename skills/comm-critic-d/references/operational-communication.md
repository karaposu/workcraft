

# Operational communication

Supplementary checks for progress briefings, decision requests and coordination messages. Select the relevant items by the exchange’s purpose. This guide evaluates how existing work is communicated; it does not instruct the critic to manage the work or construct a plan.

Apply these checks through [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); its application rules govern every item.

## O1. The brief states the substantive position

- **What to examine:** The current position, findings, conclusions and implications a substantive operational briefing is intended to convey.
- **What counts as a problem:** The brief mainly describes documents, activities or topic coverage and leaves the recipient to assemble what the work establishes.
- **Why it matters:** The recipient receives references to work without the understanding needed to judge its result or significance.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Apply to substantive briefings, not every message or a requested document inventory. State only supported conclusions; if the conclusion has not been developed, the critic may identify the gap and propose clarification or further work without inventing the answer.

## O2. Reported progress states what is actually enabled

- **What to examine:** The meaningful progress being reported, its actual usability and what the result enables at the current stage.
- **What counts as a problem:** Activity labels give no relevant result, artifact production is implied to be operating capability, or unfinished arrangements are presented as usable without support.
- **Why it matters:** The recipient cannot judge how far work has advanced or may rely on a capability that does not yet exist.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Research, clarified understanding and design can be real progress. Explain their contribution without requiring deployed functionality. Not every sentence must be a completed work fact; questions and proposals have other roles. Preserve the actual status and remaining limitations.

## O3. Requests are explicit and realistically answerable

- **What to examine:** The contribution requested, the subject to be settled, the needed response and the basis for expecting this recipient to provide it.
- **What counts as a problem:** The ask is buried or ambiguous, combines materially different questions so the required responses are unclear, presumes unavailable knowledge or authority, or presents an optional proposal as the recipient’s obligation.
- **Why it matters:** The recipient cannot tell what to respond to or is asked to invent a standard, decision or commitment they do not own.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** A request can properly ask for investigation, a referral or clarification rather than an immediate answer. No fixed four-part order or proposal is required. Ask for precision only when warranted. A purely informative message needs no invented ask or approval request.

## O4. Responsibilities and decision authority are represented accurately

- **What to examine:** Who performs the relevant work, supplies an input or makes a decision when the message’s coordination purpose depends on that distinction.
- **What counts as a problem:** Necessary responsibility is missing, an involved person is treated as an owner without support, or temporary work is confused with a standing responsibility or broader decision authority.
- **Why it matters:** Recipients may assume someone else will act or treat a decision as belonging to the wrong person.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** A role or team can identify responsibility adequately. Preserve genuine open ownership and established shared context. Do not demand an owner for every descriptive statement, assign responsibilities or expand someone’s authority to make the text more definite.

## O5. The communicated route forward is understandable

- **What to examine:** The stated next outcome and the order, dependencies, contributions or timing needed to understand the proposed route to it.
- **What counts as a problem:** A requested account of what happens next is an unexplained list, omits a material dependency or implies readiness or timing the evidence does not support.
- **Why it matters:** The recipient cannot understand what progress requires or how their contribution affects the next useful result.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Apply only when a route forward belongs in this communication. Include timing when required and supportable; preserve unknown dates and open choices. The critic may propose explaining a gap, but does not invent a plan, schedule, approval gate or commitment. No fixed briefing headings are required.


# Enes's author preferences

Apply when reviewing outgoing communication in Enes's name. These are his preferences for representing him, including at Storm Ideas; they are not organisation-wide policies or rules for other authors. Current explicit instructions may override a preference for a particular text.

Apply these checks through [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); its application rules govern every item. These checks supplement the shared guides.

## E1. No unsolicited call or meeting offers

- **What to examine:** Offers or suggestions to hold, arrange or join a call or meeting in Enes's name.
- **What counts as a problem:** The draft introduces such an offer without Enes requesting or authorising it, or restores a meeting line he asked to remove.
- **Why it matters:** It creates an unwanted invitation or commitment and misrepresents how Enes wants to communicate.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Applies to outgoing communication authored for Enes. Reporting an existing meeting or discussing one that the task explicitly concerns is not an unsolicited offer. Do not extend this preference to other authors.

## E2. Personal messages use a direct, natural voice

- **What to examine:** First-person expression of Enes's own actions and views, conversational phrasing and the presentation of personal messages to colleagues.
- **What counts as a problem:** The message reads as a field-value form with labels such as “Next step:”, “Status:” or “For your approval:”; uses procedural softeners such as “please find” or “as per”; or adds expressive filler and invented motivations instead of the work-related point.
- **Why it matters:** The wording represents Enes in a voice he rejected and can obscure the substance or relationship behind the message.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Use a warm, direct register for personal work messages. First person does not require every sentence to begin with “I”. Bullets for parallel points remain useful. Requested templates, tables and labelled fields in structured documents are legitimate; do not apply this preference to the critique report itself.

## E3. Requests to Duncan respect the intended working relationship

- **What to examine:** How a message from Enes addresses Duncan when communicating with him as Enes's manager, especially requests and statements about decision responsibility.
- **What counts as a problem:** The draft uses “What I need from you” as a demand or “my recommendation is that it's you” to assign Duncan the final say, or introduces an equivalent presumptive framing unsupported by the brief.
- **Why it matters:** It can make a request sound like an instruction to Enes's manager or imply authority and responsibilities that the communication has not established.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Applies to this author-recipient relationship when it is relevant to the task. Keep requests explicit and preserve established responsibilities; respectful wording must not make the ask vague or create an extra approval requirement. Do not infer a universal ban on direct requests from this preference.

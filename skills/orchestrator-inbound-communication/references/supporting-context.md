
# Supporting Context for the Orchestrator

Use this guide when composing, critiquing or revising a contribution addressed to the human or AI coordinating the work. Apply it to the main message, supporting explanations and their connections.

## Entry form

Use a local reference number and an informative title. Beneath them, show the actual questions and put each answer directly below its question. In Markdown, link each number to its entry's heading anchor; use the delivery medium's equivalent when needed.

Normally use **up to three tailored questions plus one history/connections question**, phrased for the subject: **How does this fit with earlier or related work?** Use fewer tailored questions when sufficient. A history-only entry needs no filler.

Start each answer with its substance; include enough detail to understand it. Keep relevant evidence, source locations and limits beside the answer or in an identifiable shared basis. Distinguish what sources establish from the AI's interpretation.

Use a precise deeper pointer for useful expansion after an adequate initial answer. Several entries may share a file or a fitting deeper explanation. Question numbering is optional; it is separate from the reference number identifying the entry.

## I1. The contribution is understandable for this exchange

- **What to examine:** What the orchestrator needs to understand about the actual contribution, its significance, state and basis, given the task and available context.
- **What counts as a problem:** The delivery supplies activity or artifact descriptions while omitting the substantive answer, or relies on unshared worker context needed to assess it.
- **Why it matters:** The recipient must reconstruct the investigation before using or challenging the contribution.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Adapt to the requested technical, operational, exploratory or other aspect. Do not infer ignorance or expertise from the recipient's title. Explain genuine limits without demanding a decision or approval that the task does not require.

## I2. Useful contextual meanings are discoverable

- **What to examine:** Situated expressions and referents; claims, comparisons and relationships; task/work meaning absent from the draft; and dependencies assumed by supporting explanations.
- **What counts as a problem:** A meaningful contextual association is missing or misleading, or useful available context is discarded solely because it is not essential to an immediate decision.
- **Why it matters:** Clear sentences can still leave the recipient unable to identify, connect or inspect what the worker means.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Favor generous access, including useful optional detail. Retain uncertain candidate connections for examination, without inventing them. Do not require a number for every noun, use one number per sentence as a limit, or copy the whole source corpus. Show what understanding an association would supply.

## I3. Each reading level supplies faithful meaning

- **What to examine:** The main message, first supporting explanation and deeper material at the points where a reader may stop.
- **What counts as a problem:** A main claim becomes accurate only after a buried qualification is read, or a supporting entry merely redirects the reader to assemble its answer from raw sources.
- **Why it matters:** The recipient can leave with a misleading conclusion or be forced into a document hunt.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Keep main qualifications with their claims and give useful initial explanations before optional depth. A requested source inspection may properly require reading evidence. Self-contained understanding can span connected documents; it does not require reproducing every source or defining every ordinary word.

## I4. Visible questions organize coherent explanations

- **What to examine:** Question selection, their actual answers, entry purpose and the normal three-plus-one arrangement.
- **What counts as a problem:** Questions are hidden, generic or misleading; headings conceal independent asks; filler is added; or a numerical limit removes needed meaning or fragments one explanation.
- **Why it matters:** The recipient cannot see the explanatory scope or reliably follow what the reference supplies.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Choose tailored questions from the task, work and marked meaning. One question has one recognizable explanatory purpose; its answer may contain several facts or a comparison. For overflow, remove duplicate asks, keep supporting facts with their answer, and separate genuinely distinct purposes or deeper detail. If those arrangements would damage understanding, retain an explicit exceptional extra question. Discovery is never capped at four questions.

## I5. History and connections reflect what is established

- **What to examine:** How the subject fits relevant prior attempts, decisions, changes or connected work, and the basis for that account.
- **What counts as a problem:** The history/connections concern is silently omitted, its answer invents a reason or predecessor, or unknown history is presented as evidence that no prior work exists.
- **Why it matters:** The orchestrator may misunderstand why the current contribution exists, what changed or how it relates to other work.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Keep one visible question about that fit, with subject-specific wording. Include relevant prior or current relationships; do not force both a biography and an exhaustive dependency list. If already explained, give brief local orientation and a precise pointer. State unavailable history at the actual evidence scope. Preserve intended openness.

## I6. Numbered associations and reuse remain usable

- **What to examine:** Marker placement, target identity, actual explanatory fit, evidence access and agreement between the message and its support.
- **What counts as a problem:** A number points to the wrong meaning or state, an important source is inaccessible to the recipient, or an old explanation is reused solely because its title matches.
- **Why it matters:** Apparent support can lead to an unrelated, stale or unusable account.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Place each marker beside the expression or claim it supplements; several markers may occur in one sentence. Reuse the same local number for the same fitting explanation. Check the relevant content and preserve delivered associations when updating. A source citation and a contextual explanation serve different purposes. Use precise existing passages without requiring a global registry or rewriting old files.

## I7. Consequential unavailable context and recovery leads are visible

- **What to examine:** Required understanding still unavailable, the actual reason it is missing, and evidence of a possible person, source or system from which it could be recovered.
- **What counts as a problem:** The delivery equates the AI's missing information with nobody knowing, conceals a supported recovery lead, or presents a polished partial answer as a completed substantive result.
- **Why it matters:** The orchestrator may make a judgment before obtaining available information or misunderstand what the work established.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Distinguish unread, inaccessible, unestablished, disputed and deliberately open material. State vital gaps even without a known lead; do not claim a person has an answer merely because they were involved. Return evidence needs to the discovery owner. The critic proposes; revision applies supported communication changes. Neither invents facts or settles intentionally open work.

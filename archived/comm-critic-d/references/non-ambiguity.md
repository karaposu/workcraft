

# Non-ambiguity

Shared checks for identifiable meaning and connected explanations in outgoing communication. These concerns can appear in any medium; required detail follows the actual reader and purpose.

Apply these checks through [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); its application rules govern every item.

## N1. Identifiable referents

- **What to examine:** The referents of important names, nouns, pronouns and shorthand, including the scope needed to distinguish them.
- **What counts as a problem:** The intended reader cannot identify the thing meant, or another plausible referent would materially change the point.
- **Why it matters:** The reader must reconstruct private context or may understand a different object, task or decision.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Use the text and legitimate shared context. Established names and pronouns need no repeated definition. Preserve ambiguity deliberately serving the brief; do not guess an unknown referent.

## N2. Object roles and relationships

- **What to examine:** What important objects represent and how they relate: a record and the thing recorded, a method and its application, or evidence and its subject.
- **What counts as a problem:** Objects are named but their necessary roles or connections are missing or misleading, preventing the reader from following the point.
- **Why it matters:** Correctly understood names can still produce an incorrect account of how the work fits together.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Require only relationships needed for this explanation. Do not demand identifiers, locations or system descriptions when the connection is already clear, or invent a relationship unsupported by the sources.

## N3. Concrete actions and actual actors

- **What to examine:** Who or what acts, what the action involves, and what it affects where these details carry the meaning.
- **What counts as a problem:** Abstract nouns, broad verbs or passive wording hide a necessary action or misidentify its actor.
- **Why it matters:** The reader cannot tell what actually happens or may attribute work to the wrong person or system.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Actors may be people, teams, applications or workflows. Passive wording and descriptive sentences are acceptable when clear. Naming an actor does not establish a permanent owner or require assigning one.

## N4. Purpose, scope and judgment criteria

- **What to examine:** The purpose, comparison, scope or criterion behind consequential terms such as right, enough, ready, useful, better and significant.
- **What counts as a problem:** The relevant meaning is neither stated nor reasonably established, so the reader cannot understand the claim or question being made.
- **Why it matters:** Different implied criteria can change what counts as success, what is being compared or what response is needed.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Qualitative criteria can be sufficient. Do not invent numerical thresholds or finalise criteria meant to remain open. An exploratory question may name the unresolved criterion without answering it.

## N5. Prerequisite context available to the reader

- **What to examine:** The prior event, reason, distinction or background the recipient needs for the present point, and where the intended delivery supplies it.
- **What counts as a problem:** Understanding depends on private working context or unexplained history that the intended reader cannot reasonably be expected to have.
- **Why it matters:** The reader must reconstruct why an object or example matters before they can understand the explanation.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** A standalone document and a reply in an established thread have different context needs. Include necessary background rather than the entire history. Do not assume every recipient shares the operator’s context or recall needs; qualify material uncertainty about reader knowledge.

## N6. Connected sections and compatible claims

- **What to examine:** The question each section answers, each point’s contribution, and relationships or apparent contradictions across the whole communication.
- **What counts as a problem:** The text silently changes subject or reasoning level, presents unlike claims as equivalent, or leaves incompatible claims unexplained.
- **Why it matters:** Individually clear sentences can leave the reader with an incoherent account or a conclusion about the wrong thing.
- **When it applies—and its limits:** Different viewpoints, time periods and levels can coexist when their relationship is clear. A heading need not be a question. Preserve intentional narrative structure and unresolved alternatives; assess evidential support separately under Meaning and fidelity.
